Checkpoint 6 Science — Practice Test 3

A full two-paper practice test in Checkpoint style — 80 marks across a science museum, a Sinai camping trip, the school laboratory, a bicycle ride, a pond and a doctor's clinic — with full solutions and a mark scheme.

Before you start

  • This is a full practice test for the Cambridge Primary Checkpoint Science test (code 0097), taken at the end of Stage 6. It covers the whole Stage 6 curriculum: Biology, Chemistry, Physics, Earth and Space, and Thinking and Working Scientifically.
  • There are two papers, just like the real test. Each paper lasts 35 minutes and is worth 40 marks (80 marks in total).
  • Calculator: the cover of the real Paper 1 says "You may use a calculator". For Paper 2, ask your teacher; the sums in this practice test can all be done without one.
  • Equipment: no extra materials are needed. In the real test you answer on the question paper with a black or dark blue pen and use an HB pencil for diagrams and graphs.
  • Each question has parts (a), (b), (c)… worth 1 or 2 marks. The number of marks tells you how much to write: 2 marks usually means two separate points.
  • You may print the question paper (PDF) and write on paper first if you like, then type your answers in.
  • How marking works here: typed short answers (numbers, words, letters and multiple-choice) are checked automatically. For answers you write in sentences, you mark yourself with the mark scheme shown after you finish — be honest and strict, like a real examiner.
  • Your score here is a practice score to help you find your strong and weak topics. It is not an official Cambridge result.
قبل ما تبدأ

الامتحان ده تدريب كامل على امتحان Checkpoint العلوم للصف السادس: ورقتين، كل ورقة ٣٥ دقيقة و٤٠ درجة. اقرأ السؤال كويس، وبص على عدد الدرجات: لو السؤال عليه درجتين يبقى محتاج تكتب نقطتين مختلفتين. الإجابات القصيرة بتتصحح لوحدها، والإجابات اللي بتكتبها بجمل صحّحها بنفسك من نموذج الإجابة بأمانة. الدرجة دي للتدريب بس، مش نتيجة رسمية.

After the test
  • Go through every question you lost marks on. Read the full solution and write the correct answer in your own words.
  • For written answers, compare your words with the mark scheme: did you give two points for a 2-mark question? Did you use the key science words (artery, refraction, upthrust, solidification…)?
  • Make a list of your weak topics and revisit those lessons. Investigation questions (Paper 1 Question 4, Paper 2 Questions 5 and 10): Thinking and Working Scientifically: Investigations, Variables, Tables and Graphs.
  • Biology (Paper 1 Questions 1–3, Paper 2 Question 4): The Heart and Circulatory System; The Lungs and Respiratory System; Diseases, Hygiene and the Body's Defences; Food Chains, Food Webs, Energy and Toxins.
  • Chemistry (Paper 1 Question 4, Paper 2 Questions 1, 2, 5 and 7): Properties of Substances: Melting and Boiling Points, Gases, Conductors; Reversible Changes and Dissolving; Boiling and Evaporation; Chemical Reactions: Reactants, Products and Evidence.
  • Physics (Paper 1 Questions 6–7, Paper 2 Questions 6, 8 and 9): Force Diagrams and Forces on Moving Objects; Light: Reflection and Refraction; Electric Circuits, Symbols, Series and Parallel; Mass, Weight and Gravity; Floating and Sinking.
  • Earth and Space (Paper 1 Questions 5 and 8, Paper 2 Questions 3 and 10): The Solar System and the Phases of the Moon; Rocks and Fossils; The Rock Cycle; Types of Soil.
  • Wait a few days, then try the test again without looking at the solutions, or move on to Practice Tests 1 and 2.
