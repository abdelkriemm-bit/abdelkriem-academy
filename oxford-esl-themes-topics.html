The Three Themes and All Topics

The three themes and every topic behind all four 9280 papers, with a word bank and ready-to-use ideas for each topic.

British & IGCSE › Oxford International GCSE English as a Second Language

Every part of the 9280 exam is built on three themes. The reading texts, the writing tasks, the listening recordings and the speaking photocards all come from these themes. This page gives you every topic, a word bank for each one, and ready-made ideas you can use in any paper. Learn the words in sentences, not alone.

The three themes at a glance

ThemeTopicSub-topics / what it covers
1 Identity and cultureMe, my family, friends and people I knowRelationships with family, and relationships with friends and people I know
Technology in everyday lifeSocial media and mobile technology
Free-time activitiesMusic, cinema and TV, sport, and customs and festivals
2 Local, national, international and global areas of interestHome, town, neighbourhood and regionWhere you live and what it is like
Social issuesCharity/voluntary work and healthy/unhealthy living
Global issuesThe environment and poverty
Travel and tourismHolidays, trips and visiting places
3 Current and future study and employmentMy studiesSubjects, learning and exams
Life at school/collegeThe school day, rules, activities, pressures
Education post-16What you study after 16: sixth form, college, university, apprenticeships
Jobs, career choices and ambitionsPart-time work, careers, future plans

How the themes appear in each paper

PaperHow the themes are usedWhat this means for you
Reading (9280/R)All four texts are linked to the themes, e.g. an email about a charity event, an advert for holiday activities, an article about careers, a travel memoir.Knowing theme vocabulary makes texts faster to read and helps you guess unknown words.
Writing (9280/W)Questions 1, 2 and 3 are each based on one theme. Question 4 can draw on one or more themes.Have ideas ready for each topic so you never sit with an empty page.
Listening (9280/L)All five tasks are linked to the themes: everyday announcements, interviews, talks, podcasts.Theme words help you catch key details on the first listening.
Speaking (9280/S)The photocard covers one theme. The conversation covers the other two themes.You must be ready to talk about ALL three themes, because you do not choose.
How to study this page
  • Take one topic a day. Read the word bank aloud. Cover the meanings and test yourself.
  • Write five sentences about YOUR life using new words from the topic.
  • Use the 'ideas' lists as building blocks: each idea can become a paragraph in writing or a one-minute answer in speaking.
  • Look for the same words when you read English articles. Real texts repeat theme vocabulary all the time.

THEME 1: Identity and culture

This theme is about you: the people in your life, how you use technology, and what you do for fun. It is the most personal theme, so it is easy to use in Speaking and in personal writing such as blogs, diaries and letters.

Topic 1.1 Me, my family, friends and people I know

Sub-topics: relationships with family, and relationships with friends and people I know. Expect texts and questions about who you are close to, arguments, support, role models and growing up.

Word / phraseMeaning / use
get on well with (someone)have a friendly relationship
fall out with (someone)argue and stop being friends for a time
make upbecome friends again after an argument
close-knit familya family whose members support each other
extended familygrandparents, uncles, aunts and cousins

role modela person you admire and want to copy
look up to (someone)respect and admire someone
generation gapdifferences in ideas between young and old people
supportivehelping and encouraging others
reliable / trustworthyyou can depend on them; they keep secrets
loyalalways supporting a friend
peer pressurepressure from people your age to behave like them
acquaintancesomeone you know, but not well
bring up (a child)care for a child until adulthood
independencebeing free to make your own decisions
stricthaving many rules
share the same interestslike the same things
keep in touchstay in contact
Ideas you can use
  • Family gives security and advice, but teenagers also need independence and privacy.
  • A good friend is loyal, honest and supportive in difficult times, not only in good times.
  • Grandparents pass on traditions and experience; the generation gap can be closed by spending time together.
  • A role model does not have to be famous: a parent, teacher or older sibling can inspire us most.

Topic 1.2 Technology in everyday life

Sub-topics: social media and mobile technology. This topic often appears as an opinion article with good and bad points, which is perfect for collation (list) questions.

Ideas you can use
  • Mobile phones keep families in touch and help students learn, for example with dictionary apps and online lessons.
  • Too much screen time can damage sleep, concentration and physical health.
  • Social media lets young people share ideas and support charities, but it can also spread fake news and cyberbullying.
  • The answer is balance: phone-free times such as meals and an hour before bed.

Sub-topics: music, cinema and TV, sport, and customs and festivals. Adverts and brochures for activities (Text 2 style) and diary/blog writing about a celebration can appear here.

Ideas you can use
  • Hobbies reduce stress after school and help us make friends with the same interests.
  • Team sports teach cooperation, discipline and how to accept losing.
  • Festivals such as Eid bring families together: visiting relatives, special food, new clothes and gifts for children.
  • Streaming is convenient, but watching a film at the cinema with friends is a shared experience.

THEME 2: Local, national, international and global areas of interest

This theme moves from your home to the whole world: your town, helping others, health, the environment, poverty and travel. Opinion articles (Text 3) and literary travel writing (Text 4) often come from here.

Topic 2.1 Home, town, neighbourhood and region

Expect descriptions of places, advantages and disadvantages of city and village life, and improvements to your area.

Word / phraseMeaning / use
neighbourhoodthe area around your home

Ideas you can use
  • City life offers jobs, shops and entertainment, but brings noise, pollution and traffic.
  • Village or suburb life is calmer and greener, though facilities and transport may be limited.
  • My area could be improved with more green spaces, cycle lanes and youth clubs.
  • A good neighbourhood is safe, clean and friendly, with people who help each other.

Sub-topics: charity/voluntary work and healthy/unhealthy living. Charity events, sponsored challenges, diets and exercise can appear in emails and articles.

Ideas you can use
  • Volunteering helps others and gives young people skills, confidence and experience for their CV.
  • Even small actions help: collecting clothes, visiting the elderly, a sponsored walk.
  • A healthy life needs a balanced diet, regular exercise, enough sleep and time away from screens.
  • Schools could help by selling healthy snacks and offering more sports clubs.

Topic 2.3 Global issues

Sub-topics: the environment and poverty. These topics suit Writing Question 4 (argue or persuade) and opinion articles in Reading.

Word / phraseMeaning / use
climate change / global warmingthe rise in Earth's temperature

Ideas you can use
  • Individuals can help the environment: recycling, using less plastic, saving water and electricity, walking instead of driving.
  • Governments should invest in renewable energy and public transport.
  • Education is the strongest way to break the cycle of poverty.
  • Rich countries and charities can provide aid, but long-term jobs and fair pay matter most.

Holiday adverts and brochures (Text 2) and travel memoirs (Text 4) are typical. Writing Question 1 photographs may look as if they come from a holiday brochure or travel blog.

Ideas you can use

  • Tourism brings jobs and money, but too many tourists can damage historic sites and nature.
  • Egypt has world-famous attractions: the pyramids, Luxor's temples and the Red Sea coast.
  • A holiday at home (exploring your own country) can be cheaper and greener than flying abroad.

THEME 3: Current and future study and employment

This theme is about your studies now and your plans for the future. Letters to parents about school events, reports on school activities and articles about careers or apprenticeships are typical.

Topic 3.1 My studies

Subjects you like and dislike, how you learn best, homework, revision and exams.

Word / phraseMeaning / use
compulsory / optional subjecta subject you must / can choose to study
timetablethe plan of lessons in the week

assignmenta piece of work set by a teacher

Ideas you can use
  • My favourite subject is useful for my future career and taught in an interesting way.
  • A revision schedule, short breaks and past papers help students prepare for exams.
  • Some students learn best in groups, others alone and in silence.
  • Exams create stress, but good planning and sleep reduce it.

The school day, rules, uniforms, clubs, trips, events and the pressure students feel.

Word / phraseMeaning / use

Ideas you can use
  • Clubs and trips make school more enjoyable and build friendships and confidence.
  • School uniform creates equality and saves time, though some say it limits self-expression.
  • A school council lets students share ideas and improve their school.
  • Long school days and homework can leave little time for rest and hobbies.

What students do after 16: staying at school or sixth form, going to college, university, apprenticeships or training.

Word / phraseMeaning / use

Ideas you can use
  • University opens doors to professions such as medicine and engineering, but it is expensive.
  • Apprenticeships give practical skills and a salary with no student debt.
  • A gap year can build maturity through travel or volunteering, but some students lose motivation.
  • Studying abroad improves your English and independence, but you may miss your family.

Ideas you can use

  • A good job offers a fair salary, job satisfaction and a healthy work-life balance.
  • Part-time jobs and work experience teach responsibility and time management.
  • Skills such as teamwork, communication and technology are important in every career.

Use one idea in all four papers

Here is how one idea from Topic 2.2 (volunteering) can help you everywhere:

PaperHow the idea helps
ReadingYou meet 'raise funds', 'sponsor', 'volunteers' in an email about a charity walk and understand them instantly.
WritingQ2 letter: invite parents to a charity concert. Q4: explain why young people should volunteer.
ListeningAn interview with a teenage volunteer: you catch 'donations' and 'the elderly' easily.
SpeakingPhotocard of students cleaning a beach: describe it, then give your opinion with reasons.

  1. Which theme does a text about choosing between university and an apprenticeship belong to?

    Show answer
    Theme 3 (Current and future study and employment), topic: Education post-16.

  2. Your speaking photocard is about Theme 2. Which themes will the general conversation cover?

    Show answer
    Theme 1 (Identity and culture) and Theme 3 (Current and future study and employment): the two themes NOT on the photocard.

  3. Give a word that means 'give money to a charity'.

    Show answer
    Donate (noun: donation).

  4. What is the difference between 'salary' and 'wage'?

    Show answer
    Both mean money from work. A salary is usually a fixed yearly amount paid monthly, while a wage is usually paid by the hour or week.

  5. Explain 'off the beaten track'.

    Show answer
    A place away from the usual tourist areas, not visited by many people.

  6. Give two disadvantages of social media and one solution.

    Show answer
    For example: (1) cyberbullying and (2) too much screen time, which harms sleep. Solution: phone-free times, such as an hour before bed.
