Oxford ESL 9280 · Speaking

The Speaking Test (9280/S)

How the 10-minute Speaking test works, how your photocard decides the conversation themes, what every marking criterion rewards, and the A-R-E method for developing top-band answers.

British & IGCSE › Oxford International GCSE English as a Second Language

The Speaking test (9280/S) is worth 20% of your Oxford International GCSE ESL grade, the same as Listening. It is the only part of the qualification you do with your own teacher, in your own school, and it is the part where good preparation makes the biggest difference. This page explains how the test works, how the photocard decides what you talk about in the conversation, what each of the four marking criteria rewards, and a simple method — A-R-E — to turn short answers into top-band answers.

The Speaking test in one line

About 10 minutes + 10 minutes' supervised preparation · 40 marks · 20% · Part 1 Photocard (15 marks, about 3-4 minutes) · Part 2 General conversation (25 marks, about 6-7 minutes) · conducted and recorded by your teacher · marked by an OxfordAQA examiner.

1. The test at a glance

ItemWhat you need to know
TypeNon-exam assessment (NEA): it is not a written exam, but a recorded speaking test.
Who tests youYour teacher (the *teacher-examiner*) asks the questions and records the whole test as an mp3 file.
Who marks itAn OxfordAQA examiner listens to the recording and gives the marks. Your teacher does not decide your mark.
WhenDuring a five-week window set by OxfordAQA (for the summer series, around April-May).
How many attemptsOne attempt only. (If you retake the whole qualification later, a Speaking result can be carried forward.)
Preparation10 minutes, supervised, with your photocard. You may write notes on a separate sheet.
LengthAbout 10 minutes: Photocard about 3-4 minutes, then General conversation about 6-7 minutes.
Marks40 = Photocard 15 + Conversation 25
DictionaryNot allowed at any time, including the preparation time. No phones or other materials.

2. What happens on the day, step by step

  1. Preparation room (10 minutes). You are given one photocard, chosen by a fixed sequence (you cannot choose it). The card shows a photo, the topic, and the three questions your teacher will ask. You may make notes on a clean sheet, but you must not write on the card. You are supervised and cannot talk to anyone.
  2. Exam room. You take the card and your notes with you. The teacher starts the recording and says your name, candidate number and the card letter.
  3. Part 1: Photocard (about 3-4 minutes, 15 marks). The teacher asks the three printed questions in order. The teacher may add short follow-up prompts if you need help. Then the teacher asks two more questions that are not on the card (unseen questions), chosen from a list of eight. You may use your notes in this part.
  4. Notes collected. At the end of Part 1, the teacher takes back the photocard and your notes.
  5. Part 2: General conversation (about 6-7 minutes, 25 marks). The teacher announces the first theme (*Now, the general conversation beginning with the theme…*) and talks with you about it for about three minutes, then moves to the second theme for about three minutes.
  6. End. The recording is stopped only at the end. It is sent to OxfordAQA for marking.
You are allowed to ask

If you do not understand a question, you can ask the teacher to repeat or explain it: *Sorry, could you repeat the question, please?* If you then answer well, you are marked as if you had understood the first time. But any word the teacher gives you in the conversation does not earn you marks for vocabulary.

3. How the photocard decides the conversation

The course has three themes. Your photocard is on one theme (and one topic inside it). The General conversation then covers the other two themes: about the same time on each. So you will talk about all three themes in your test.

If your photocard is on…Your General conversation covers…
Theme 1: Identity and culture (e.g. a card on sport, music, family or social media)Theme 2: Local, national, international and global areas of interest + Theme 3: Current and future study and employment
Theme 2: Local, national, international and global areas of interest (e.g. a card on travel, volunteering, healthy living or the environment)Theme 1: Identity and culture + Theme 3: Current and future study and employment
Theme 3: Current and future study and employment (e.g. a card on your studies, school life, post-16 plans or jobs)Theme 1: Identity and culture + Theme 2: Local, national, international and global areas of interest
  • Your teacher chooses which topics and sub-topics to ask about inside each theme. There is no fixed number of topics.
  • You will not know the conversation questions in advance. Prepare all topics in all three themes.
  • A good teacher-examiner follows your answers with new questions (*Why? What happened? Would you do it again?*), so be ready to explain and give details.
  • The teacher should say clearly when the second theme starts. Listen for it: it is a signal to change your vocabulary.
ThemeTopics you must be ready for
1 Identity and cultureMe, my family, friends and people I know (relationships with family, and with friends and people I know) · Technology in everyday life (social media and mobile technology) · Free-time activities (music, cinema and TV, sport, and customs and festivals)
2 Local, national, international and global areas of interestHome, town, neighbourhood and region · Social issues (charity/voluntary work and healthy/unhealthy living) · Global issues (the environment and poverty) · Travel and tourism
3 Current and future study and employmentMy studies · Life at school/college · Education post-16 · Jobs, career choices and ambitions

Important rule

If you score zero for Communication in the conversation, all the other conversation criteria are also zero. Apart from that, the four conversation marks are given separately: you can lose a mark for pronunciation and still get full marks for communication.

5. What each criterion needs (the bands, in simple words)

Examiners use levels. They start at the lowest level and move up until they find the one that best describes your performance. Below is a summary in our own words; it is not the official wording.

Photocard: Communication (10 marks)

LevelMarksWhat the examiner hears
59-10Clear answers to all the questions · most answers are developed · an opinion is given and explained.
47-8Clear answers to all or almost all questions · some answers developed · an opinion given and explained.
35-6Understandable answers to most questions · at least one answer developed · an opinion given (maybe without a reason).
23-4Understandable but short or repetitive answers · the teacher needs more prompts to get an answer.
11-2Answers to only some questions · short and repetitive · the student clearly depends on the prompts.

Photocard: Knowledge and use of language (5 marks)

MarkWhat the examiner hears
5Very good language: mostly accurate, with subordinate clauses (*because, when, although, which…*) and some complex language.
4Good language: generally accurate, uses subordinate clauses and tries complex language.
3Reasonable: more accurate than inaccurate · uses subordinate clauses and compound sentences (*and, but, so*).
2Limited: often inaccurate · tries to use subordination, but the language and structures are repetitive.
1Poor: errors (e.g. subject-verb agreement, word order) make the meaning unclear.

Conversation: Communication (10 marks)

LevelMarksWhat the examiner hears
59-10Consistently long, developed answers · tells a story or event clearly when asked · always clear · opinions given and explained convincingly.
47-8Usually long, developed answers · narrates events with relevant detail · almost always clear · opinions given and explained.
35-6Some developed answers · narrates with some detail · usually clear · opinions given and often explained.
23-4Mostly short answers · narration brief or not very successful · some unclear moments · at least one opinion.
11-2Very short answers only · longer attempts are unclear · sometimes no answer.

Conversation: Knowledge and use of language (5 marks)

MarkWhat the examiner hears
5Excellent: wide variety of structures and wide vocabulary · past, present and future used confidently · high accuracy (small slips are fine).
4Very good: some variety of structures and a range of vocabulary · past and future generally successful · minor errors when trying harder language.
3Good: some more complex structures and vocabulary, usually successful · past and future attempted, sometimes successful · errors do not block meaning.
2Reasonable but simple and sometimes repetitive · past or future attempted with limited success · errors sometimes block meaning.
1Basic, simple and repetitive · little or no past or future · frequent errors that block meaning.
No past and future = no top band

Examiners have reported that some students could not reach the higher language marks because they only used the present tense. In every topic, show the past (*Last year…*), the present (*These days…*) and the future (*Next year I'm going to…*). Accuracy alone is not enough: the top band also needs variety of structures and vocabulary.

Conversation: Spontaneity and fluency (5 marks)

MarkWhat the examiner hears
5An excellent, natural exchange: reacts naturally to each question, sounds spontaneous, answers promptly and flows well (it does not have to sound like a native speaker).
4Usually reacts naturally and is often spontaneous · usually prompt, with some flow.
3Sometimes natural, but some answers are clearly pre-learnt · some hesitation, but a reasonable pace overall.
2A little spontaneity · much of it pre-learnt · frequent hesitation and slow delivery.
1No spontaneity · depends on learnt answers · long pauses · the conversation does not flow.

Conversation: Pronunciation and intonation (5 marks)

MarkWhat the examiner hears
5Good pronunciation and intonation all the way through.
4Good, with only occasional slips on harder words.
3Generally understandable, with some natural intonation.
2Usually understandable, a little intonation · the listener sometimes needs time to understand.
1Only sometimes understandable · difficult to follow.

  • استخدم الماضي والمضارع والمستقبل في كل موضوع، فبدونها لن تصل إلى أعلى درجة في اللغة.
  • لا تحفظ فقرات كاملة وتسمّعها؛ الممتحن يلاحظ ذلك وتنخفض درجة الطلاقة والتلقائية.
  • احفظ عبارات وكلمات مفيدة لكل موضوع، ثم تكلّم بشكل طبيعي حسب السؤال.

6. How to develop every answer: the A-R-E method

The difference between a 5/10 and a 10/10 for Communication is usually not difficult vocabulary. It is development: top students never stop after one sentence. Use A-R-E for every question:

StepWhat you doUseful starters
A: AnswerAnswer the question directly in one sentence.*Yes, definitely… / To be honest, not really… / My favourite… is…*
R: ReasonSay why. Use a subordinate clause.*because… / since… / the main reason is that… / which means that…*
E: Example / ExtendGive a real example from your life — ideally in a different tense — or extend with a comparison, a future plan or a hypothetical idea.*For example, last summer… / When I was younger… / Next year I'm planning to… / If I had the chance, I would…*

See the difference. Question: *Do you like living in your town?*

Weak answer (Level 1-2): *Yes, I like it. It is nice.* (one opinion, no reason, present tense only)

Top-band answer (A-R-E): A: *Yes, I really like it, although it's getting quite crowded.* R: *The main reason is that everything I need is close by: my school, the club where I train and my grandparents' flat are all within walking distance.* E: *Last weekend, for example, I walked to the new market with my cousins and we spent the whole afternoon there. In the future, though, I'd like to live somewhere greener, because the traffic here is getting worse every year.*

A-R-E in action87 words · Communication: Level 5 quality (developed, clear, opinion and narration) · Language 5 (past, present, future and subordinate clauses)
Task: Conversation question (Theme 1): What do you usually do at the weekend?

Well, my weekends are usually split between sport and family. On Fridays I play football with my school team, because it's the only time all my friends are free at the same time. Then on Saturday evenings we always have dinner at my grandmother's house. Last Saturday, for instance, she cooked stuffed vine leaves and my uncle told us stories about his trip to Jordan. Next weekend should be different, though, since I'm going to a robotics workshop in New Cairo, which I'm really looking forward to.

Why this gets the marks
  • A: *my weekends are usually split between sport and family* answers immediately.
  • R: *because it's the only time all my friends are free* gives a reason with a subordinate clause.
  • E: *Last Saturday, for instance…* narrates a real event in the past tense with detail.
  • Future: *Next weekend… I'm going to… which I'm really looking forward to* shows the future and a relative clause.
  • Spontaneity: *Well,* and *though* make it sound like natural speech, not a recited paragraph.

7. Tense variety: show past, present and future in every topic

TimeSignal phrasesExample
Present (habits, facts)*Usually · these days · every week · at the moment I'm…**At the moment I'm learning to swim properly.*
Past (events)*Last year · when I was ten · the last time I… · a few weeks ago**When I was ten, I broke my arm playing basketball.*
Present perfect (experience)*I've never… · I've been… since… · I've just…**I've been playing the oud since I was eight.*
Future (plans)*Next summer I'm going to… · I'm planning to… · I'd like to… · I hope to…**Next summer I'm going to volunteer at a summer camp.*
Conditional (hypothetical)*If I could…, I would… · If I were the head teacher, I would… · I would love to…**If I could change one thing in my town, I would build a library.*
Used to (changes)*I used to…, but now… · I didn't use to…**I used to hate reading, but now I read every night.*

8. Fillers that sound natural (and help you think)

Real speakers pause. A short, natural filler is much better than silence or *eeeh*. Use them to buy two or three seconds, but do not use the same one every time.

Buying time

  • Well, …
  • Let me think…
  • That's a good question.
  • Hmm, it's hard to say, but…
  • I've never really thought about that, but…
  • How can I put it…

Giving yourself a second when you lose a word

  • What's the word… it's a kind of…
  • I mean…
  • Sort of… / kind of…
  • Something like…

Correcting yourself

  • Sorry, I mean…
  • Actually, no — what I meant was…
  • Let me put it another way…

Asking for help (allowed)

  • Sorry, could you repeat the question, please?
  • Do you mean… or…?
  • Could you say that in a different way, please?

Linking your ideas

  • On top of that, …
  • Another thing is that…
  • That's why…
  • Apart from that, …
  • Having said that, …
  • In the end, …

9. What loses marks

ProblemWhy it loses marksWhat to do instead
Memorised speechesExaminers recognise recited answers: flat intonation, answers that do not match the question. This lowers Spontaneity and fluency, and often Pronunciation and intonation too.Learn phrases and ideas for each topic, not whole paragraphs. Practise with new questions every time.
One-word or one-sentence answersCommunication depends on developing answers. *Yes. / I like it.* cannot go above the lower levels.Use A-R-E every time: answer, reason, example.
Present tense onlyLanguage 4-5 requires confident past and future references.Add a past example and a future plan to every topic.
Answering a different questionIf your answer does not match the question, communication suffers.Listen to the key word: *why, when, would you, have you ever…*
Very long silencesFluency drops and the conversation stops flowing.Use a filler, then start with a simple sentence and build on it.
Reading from notesNotes are collected before the conversation; in Part 1, reading full sentences sounds unnatural.Write key words only in your preparation.
Too fast or too quietThe examiner hears only the recording. If it is not clear, it cannot be credited.Speak at a steady pace, towards the microphone, in a clear voice.

10. Pronunciation and intonation tips for Arabic speakers

  • P and B. *Pen* is not *Ben*. Practise: *people, problem, park, pollution, public.*
  • V and F. *Very, visit, volunteer, environment*: put your top teeth on your bottom lip and use your voice for V.
  • Th. *Think, three, the, together*: put your tongue between your teeth. Avoid *sink* for *think* or *zis* for *this*.
  • -ed endings. *Watched* = /t/, *played* = /d/, *visited* = /ɪd/. Do not add an extra syllable to *watched* (not *watch-ed*).
  • Word stress. *TECH-nology?* No: tech-NOL-o-gy. Also: en-VI-ron-ment, e-DU-ca-tion, PHO-to-graph, pho-TO-graph-er.
  • Do not add vowels at the start of words: *school*, not *ischool*, and *street*, not *istreet*.
  • Intonation. Let your voice go down at the end of statements and up for yes/no questions. Stress the important words: *I REALLY enjoyed it.*
  • Chunking. Pause between ideas, not between every word: *Last summer / I went to Alexandria / with my cousins.*

11. Practice: develop these weak answers

  1. *Q: Do you use social media?* Weak answer: *Yes, I use Instagram.* Develop it with A-R-E.

    Show answer
    Sample: *Yes, quite a lot, mainly Instagram and WhatsApp, because they help me keep in touch with my cousins who live in Dubai. For example, last month my cousin sent me photos from his school trip and we ended up chatting for an hour. Having said that, I'm trying to spend less time scrolling, so next month I'm going to turn off my notifications during the exam period.*

  2. *Q: What is your favourite subject?* Weak answer: *Maths.*

    Show answer
    Sample: *I'd say maths, because I enjoy solving problems step by step, and there's always one clear answer, which I find satisfying. When I was in Year 7 I found it really hard, but my teacher gave me extra exercises and my marks improved a lot. If everything goes well, I'd like to study engineering at university, so maths will be really important for me.*

  3. *Q: What did you do last summer?* Weak answer: *I went to the beach.*

    Show answer
    Sample: *Last summer my family rented a flat in Alexandria for two weeks. We went to the beach almost every morning, and in the evenings we walked along the Corniche and ate ice cream. The best day was when my father took us on a boat trip — I'd never done that before. Next summer I'm hoping to go somewhere new, maybe Luxor, because I've never seen the temples.*

  4. *Q: Is it important to protect the environment?* Weak answer: *Yes, it is important.*

    Show answer
    Sample: *Absolutely, because if we don't act now, the next generation will pay the price. In Egypt, for example, the summers are getting hotter every year, and water is becoming more precious. At home we've started to recycle plastic bottles and use less water when we wash the car. If I were the head of my school, I would ban single-use plastic in the canteen.*
