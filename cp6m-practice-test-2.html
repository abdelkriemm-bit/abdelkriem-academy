Checkpoint 6 Maths — Practice Test 2

A full two-paper practice test in Checkpoint style (2 × 45 minutes, 2 × 40 marks; Paper 1 without a calculator, Paper 2 with one), with automatic marking and a full mark scheme. By Mohamed Abdelkriem.

British & IGCSE › Checkpoint 6 › Checkpoint 6 Maths — Complete Course

Before you start

  • What this is: a complete practice test in the style of the Cambridge Primary Checkpoint Mathematics test (0096), which tests what you know at the end of Stage 6. Every question is new. The contexts are a supermarket, a cinema timetable, bus journeys, tile designs, a class survey of pets and a koshari recipe.
  • Two papers, like the real test: Paper 1 — 45 minutes, 40 marks, no calculator. Paper 2 — 45 minutes, 40 marks, calculator allowed. That is 80 marks in total. The timer here runs for 45 minutes for each paper.
  • Equipment: the real paper covers list compasses and a protractor (tracing paper is optional). Write in black or dark blue pen and use an HB pencil for diagrams. Have a ruler, compasses and some squared paper ready for the drawing questions in this test, and a calculator for Paper 2 only.
  • Write on paper first if you like. You can also print the question paper (PDF link above the questions), do it like a real exam, then type in your answers. In the real test you answer on the question paper and show all your working there.
  • Look at the marks: most questions are worth 1 mark. A 2-mark question that says "Show your working" can earn 1 mark for a correct method even if the final answer is wrong — so always write your working.
  • How to type answers: numbers only (for example 46.75 or 1 5/12); fractions with a slash, like 3/4; coordinates like (−3, −2); ratios like 3:2; times like 07:24.
  • How marking works here: answers you type or choose are checked automatically. Drawings and written explanations are marked by you: when you finish a paper, compare your answer with the mark scheme shown and give yourself only the marks you really earned.
  • Your score is a practice score only. It shows your strengths and weaknesses; it is not an official Cambridge result.
قبل ما تبدأ

الامتحان ده تدريب كامل زي Checkpoint Maths الحقيقي: ورقتين، كل ورقة ٤٥ دقيقة و٤٠ درجة. الورقة الأولى من غير آلة حاسبة، والتانية مسموح فيها بالآلة الحاسبة. اكتب خطوات الحل دايمًا، لأن السؤال اللي عليه درجتين ممكن تاخد فيه درجة على الطريقة حتى لو الناتج غلط. الإجابات القصيرة بتتصحح لوحدها، أما الرسم والشرح فإنت اللي هتصححه بنفسك بنموذج الإجابة — كن أمين مع نفسك. الدرجة دي للتدريب بس، مش نتيجة رسمية من كامبريدج.

After the test
  • Mark honestly. For each drawing or explanation, find the marking points in the mark scheme. Give the mark only if your answer says the same mathematics, even in different words.
  • Make a mistakes list. For every lost mark write the question number, the topic and why you lost it: did not know it / misread the question / calculation slip / no working on a 2-mark question / forgot units or the remainder.
  • Number (Paper 1): Q2, Q3 → *Counting On and Back and Negative Numbers*; Q13, Q14, Q15 → *Sequences, Letters and Position-to-Term Rules*; Q6, Q7 → *Order of Operations, Brackets and Laws of Arithmetic*; Q8 → *Multiplying Whole Numbers (up to 10 000 × 2-digit)*; Q9 → *Dividing Whole Numbers (up to 1000 ÷ 2-digit)*; Q10, Q11 → *Factors, Multiples and Tests of Divisibility*; Q12 → *Square Numbers and Cube Numbers*.
  • Number (Paper 1): Q1, Q4, Q5 → *Place Value and Decimals to Thousandths*; Q25 → *Rounding Decimals*; Q16, Q17, Q18 → *Fractions: Division, Operators and Simplest Form*; Q19 → *Adding and Subtracting Fractions*; Q20 → *Multiplying and Dividing Fractions by Whole Numbers*; Q21, Q22 → *Fractions, Decimals and Percentages: Equivalence and Ordering*; Q23, Q24 → *Adding, Subtracting, Multiplying and Dividing Decimals*.
  • Number (Paper 2): Q1 → *Counting On and Back and Negative Numbers*; Q2 → *Multiplying Whole Numbers (up to 10 000 × 2-digit)*; Q3, Q4 → *Factors, Multiples and Tests of Divisibility*; Q5 → *Order of Operations, Brackets and Laws of Arithmetic*; Q6 → *Square Numbers and Cube Numbers*; Q8(a), Q9 → *Adding, Subtracting, Multiplying and Dividing Decimals*; Q9(a), Q10 → *Fractions, Decimals and Percentages: Equivalence and Ordering*; Q8(b), Q11 → *Ratio and Direct Proportion*.
  • Geometry and measure: Paper 1 Q26 and Paper 2 Q7 → *Time: Decimals and Mixed Units*; Paper 1 Q27 and Paper 2 Q13(d) → *Quadrilaterals: Properties and Classification*; Paper 2 Q16 → *Circles: Parts and Constructing Circles*; Paper 1 Q28 and Paper 2 Q17 → *Angles: Measuring, Drawing and Angles in a Triangle*; Paper 2 Q13(a) → *Area of Rectangles and Right-angled Triangles*; Paper 2 Q14, Q15 → *3D Shapes, Compound Shapes and Nets*; Paper 2 Q12, Q13(c) → *Capacity, Volume and Surface Area*; Paper 1 Q29 → *Coordinates in Four Quadrants*; Paper 1 Q30 and Paper 2 Q13(b), Q18, Q19 → *Rotational Symmetry, Translation, Reflection and Rotation*.
  • Statistics and probability: Paper 1 Q31 and Paper 2 Q21, Q22, Q23 → *Collecting and Representing Data*; Paper 1 Q31(b) and Paper 2 Q20 → *Mode, Median, Mean and Range, and Interpreting Data*; Paper 1 Q32 and Paper 2 Q24, Q25 → *Probability*.
  • Then try again: redo every question you lost marks on, without looking at the solution, a few days later. When you are ready, take another practice test under timed conditions.
