Grammar: Narrative tenses · past simple, past continuous and past perfect

To tell a story we use three tenses:

past simple = the main events (he ran away) · past continuous = the background (it was raining) · past perfect (had + past participle) = something that happened BEFORE another past event (the train had left).

«كان قد + فعل» في العربية = had + التصريف الثالث في الإنجليزية: كان القطار قد غادر = The train had left. أكثر خطأين: استخدام الماضي البسيط لكل شيء (✗ When I arrived, the film already started)، أو استخدام had مع التصريف الثاني (✗ I had went). استخدم الماضي التام فقط عندما ترجع بالقصة إلى حدث أقدم.

TenseFormUseExample
Past simpleverb + -ed / irregularmain events, one after anotherHe saw the police and ran away.
Past continuouswas / were + -ingbackground, an action in progressI was walking home when it started to rain.
Past perfecthad + past participlean earlier past actionWhen I got there, the shop had closed.

✗ When I arrived, the film already started. ✓ When I arrived, the film had already started.

When the police arrived, the thieves ran away. (= after the police came) · When the police arrived, the thieves had run away. (= before the police came)

Negative: hadn't + past participle (I hadn't eaten). Question: Had you met her before?

Exercises

Exercise 1 · Choose the correct answer.

  1. 1) I (walked / was walking) home when I saw the accident.
  2. 2) When we arrived, the match (already started / had already started).
  3. 3) She (was reading / had read) the news when the lights went out.
  4. 4) They (didn't / hadn't) eaten anything all day, so they were very hungry.
  5. 5) As soon as he saw the police, he (ran / was running) away.
  6. 6) By the time I noticed, someone (stole / had stolen) my wallet.
Show the answers
1) was walking 2) had already started 3) was reading 4) hadn't 5) ran 6) had stolen

Exercise 2 · Complete with the past perfect.

  1. 1) By the time I got to the bank, it ___ (close).
  2. 2) She was upset because she ___ (fail) her exam.
  3. 3) We ___ never ___ (see) snow before we went to Europe.
  4. 4) When we got home, we saw that the burglar ___ (break) the window to get in.
  5. 5) I ___ (not finish) my work when my manager called.
  6. 6) ___ you ___ (meet) her before the party?
Show the answers
1) had closed 2) had failed 3) had … seen 4) had broken 5) hadn't finished 6) Had … met

Exercise 3 · Join the sentences with the word in brackets.

  1. 1) I was cooking. The phone rang. (while)
  2. 2) He finished his homework. Then he went out. (after)
  3. 3) We got to the station. The train left before that. (by the time)
  4. 4) She saw the police. She ran away immediately. (as soon as)
  5. 5) I was walking in the street. I met an old friend. (when)
Show the answers
1) While I was cooking, the phone rang. 2) After he had finished his homework, he went out. / After he finished his homework, he went out. / He went out after he had finished his homework. 3) By the time we got to the station, the train had left. 4) As soon as she saw the police, she ran away. 5) I was walking in the street when I met an old friend. / When I was walking in the street, I met an old friend. / While I was walking in the street, I met an old friend.

Exercise 4 · Translate into English.

  1. 1) بينما كنت أقود السيارة، رأيت حادثًا.
  2. 2) عندما وصلنا، كان الفيلم قد بدأ.
  3. 3) قبضت الشرطة على اللص أمس.
  4. 4) لم أكن قد زرت الأقصر من قبل.
Show the answers
1) While I was driving (the car), I saw an accident. / I saw an accident while I was driving. 2) When we arrived, the film had (already) started. / The film had (already) started when we arrived. 3) The police arrested the thief yesterday. / The police caught the thief yesterday. 4) I had never visited Luxor before. / I hadn't visited Luxor before. / I had never been to Luxor before.

Do each exercise in pencil first, then open the answers to check.

Write & Speak

Write & Speak — Write a short news story (120–150 words) about something surprising that happened — real or imagined. Use the past continuous for the background, the past simple for the main events and the past perfect for earlier events. Then tell the story aloud like a news reporter.

Example: Last Thursday evening, Ahmed Samir, a 32-year-old engineer from Nasr City, had a very strange experience. He was sitting in a café in Downtown Cairo and chatting with a friend when a young man grabbed his phone from the table and ran away. Ahmed chased him down the street, but the thief had already disappeared into the crowd. Luckily, a woman at the next table had filmed everything on her phone. She shared the video online and it went viral in a few hours. Thousands of people commented, and some of them recognised the thief. The next morning, the police arrested a 19-year-old suspect near Ramses Station. He had stolen four other phones in the same week. In the end, Ahmed got his phone back. “I had almost given up,” he said, “but social media helped me.”
