Grammar: Advanced comparatives — the more … the more, much / far / a bit + comparative, not nearly as … as

Big difference: much / far / a lot + comparative (much bigger, far more expensive). Small difference: a bit / slightly / a little + comparative (a bit cheaper).

the + comparative …, the + comparative … = one change causes another: The more you read, the more you learn.

not nearly as … as = much less · (just) as … as = the same · twice as … as = double.

يقول المتعلم العربي very bigger ترجمةً لـ«أكبر جدًا»، لكن very لا تأتي قبل صيغة المقارنة؛ نستخدم much أو far أو a lot: much bigger. وفي تركيب «كلما… كلما…» نبدأ كل جزء بـ the ولا نترجم «كلما» حرفيًا: The more you practise, the better you speak (وليس Whenever you practise more, more you speak better). وانتبه لترتيب الكلمات: الاسم يأتي بعد more مباشرة: The more money you save, the more freedom you have.

StructureMeaningExample
much / far / a lot + comparativebig differenceCairo is much bigger than Luxor.
a bit / slightly / a little + comparativesmall differenceThis flat is a bit cheaper.
the + comparative, the + comparativeone change causes anotherThe hotter it gets, the more water we use.
comparative + and + comparativecontinuous changePrices are getting higher and higher.
not nearly as … asmuch lessTrains are not nearly as polluting as planes.
twice / three times as … asmultiplesThis car uses twice as much petrol as mine.

Wrong: very more expensive ✔ much / far more expensive.

Short fixed forms: The sooner, the better. · The more, the merrier.

by far + superlative: Climate change is by far the biggest challenge.

Exercises

Exercise 1 · Choose the correct word.

  1. 1) Plastic bags are (very / much) more harmful than paper bags.
  2. 2) Today is (slightly / far) warmer than yesterday — only one degree.
  3. 3) Electric cars are (a lot / very) cleaner than old diesel cars.
  4. 4) The problem is (much / more) worse than we thought.
  5. 5) Life in the city is (far / very) more expensive than in the village.
  6. 6) Water is (by far / very) the most important resource.
Show the answers
1) much 2) slightly 3) a lot 4) much 5) far 6) by far

Exercise 2 · Complete with the … the … .

  1. 1) The more you recycle, ___ (little) waste you produce.
  2. 2) ___ (early) we leave, the sooner we'll arrive.
  3. 3) The hotter the weather, ___ (much) water we need.
  4. 4) ___ (many) people volunteer, the more we can help.
  5. 5) The older he gets, ___ (wise) he becomes.
  6. 6) ___ (soon), ___ (good).
Show the answers
1) the less 2) The earlier 3) the more 4) The more 5) the wiser 6) The sooner · the better

Exercise 3 · Rewrite so the meaning is the same. Use the words in brackets.

  1. 1) Trains are much less polluting than planes. (not nearly as)
  2. 2) My flat is 100 m². Your flat is 200 m². (twice as)
  3. 3) Prices keep going up. (higher and higher)
  4. 4) When you study more, you learn more. (The more)
  5. 5) Hurghada is a little hotter than Alexandria in summer. (slightly)
  6. 6) No problem is as serious as climate change. (by far)
Show the answers
1) Trains are not nearly as polluting as planes. / Trains aren't nearly as polluting as planes. 2) Your flat is twice as big as mine. / Your flat is twice as large as mine. 3) Prices are getting higher and higher. 4) The more you study, the more you learn. 5) Hurghada is slightly hotter than Alexandria in summer. 6) Climate change is by far the most serious problem. / Climate change is by far the most serious problem of all.

Exercise 4 · Translate into English.

  1. 1) كلما قرأت أكثر، تعلمت أكثر.
  2. 2) الحياة في القاهرة أغلى بكثير من الحياة في القرية.
  3. 3) المشكلة تزداد سوءًا يومًا بعد يوم.
  4. 4) القطارات أقل تلويثًا للبيئة بكثير من الطائرات.
  5. 5) يجب أن ننشر الوعي بمشكلة نقص المياه.
Show the answers
1) The more you read, the more you learn. / The more I read, the more I learn. 2) Life in Cairo is much (far) more expensive than life in the village. 3) The problem is getting worse and worse. 4) Trains are not nearly as polluting as planes. / Trains are much less polluting than planes. 5) We must raise awareness of the water shortage. / We must raise awareness about the water shortage.

Do each exercise in pencil first, then open the answers to check.

Write & Speak

Write & Speak — “What is the biggest problem facing the world today, and what can ordinary people do?”: write an opinion essay of 150–200 words. Use at least four advanced comparative structures. Then present your essay aloud as a short talk.

Example: In my view, climate change is by far the biggest problem facing the world today. Summers in Egypt are getting hotter and hotter, and heatwaves are much longer than they were when I was a child. Scientists warn that the higher temperatures rise, the more droughts, floods and food shortages we will see. Countries like ours, which depend on the Nile for fresh water, are especially at risk. Some people think that ordinary people can't do anything and that only governments can solve the problem. I don't agree. Of course, big companies produce far more carbon emissions than individuals, but our daily choices still matter. For example, taking public transport is not nearly as polluting as driving alone, and a fan uses much less electricity than an air conditioner. We can also cut down on single-use plastic, recycle and stop wasting food. Most importantly, we can raise awareness. The more people understand the problem, the more pressure they will put on governments to take action. And the sooner we all start, the better.
