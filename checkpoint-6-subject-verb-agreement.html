Checkpoint 6 · Grammar in Use

Subject-Verb Agreement

How to make the verb match its subject, the tricky cases (each, everyone, neither...nor, one of, there is/are) and the agreement mistakes that lose marks every year.

British & IGCSE › Checkpoint 6 › Checkpoint 6 English (Cambridge Primary 0058)

1. The basic rule

The verb must agree with its subject: a singular subject takes a singular verb, and a plural subject takes a plural verb. First find the subject (who or what does the action), then choose the verb.

VerbSingular (he / she / it / one thing)Plural (we / you / they / many things)
be (present)is (*I am*)are
be (past)waswere
havehashave
dodoes / doesn'tdo / don't
other verbs (present)add -s: *runs, plays, watches*base form: *run, play, watch*
Past tense is easier

In the past simple, most verbs are the same for everyone: *he played, they played*. Only be changes: *he was, they were*.

2. The rules for tricky subjects

RuleCorrect example
Singular subject → singular verb (+s in the present)The dog barks. / She was late.
Plural subject → plural verbThe dogs bark. / They were late.
Two subjects joined by *and* → pluralOmar and Ali are cousins.
*either ... or / neither ... nor* → the verb agrees with the nearer subjectNeither the teacher nor the students were ready. / Either the students or the teacher is coming.
*everyone, everybody, someone, nobody, each, every* → singularEveryone is here. / Each child has a book.
The real subject, not the nearest nounThe box of chocolates is on the table. (the box is)
*one of* + plural noun → singular verbOne of my friends lives in Dubai.
*There is* + singular / *There are* + pluralThere is a cat. / There are many shops.
Collective nouns (team, family, class) usually singularOur team is winning.
Amounts of time, money and distance as one unit → singularTen kilometres is a long way to walk.
Find the real subject

Cover the words between the subject and the verb: *The children in the back row are noisy.* → *The children are noisy.* / *The colour of the flowers is lovely.* → *The colour is lovely.*

3. Uncountable nouns are singular

Words like information, news, advice, furniture, homework, equipment, money, water are uncountable. They always take a singular verb.

  • *The news is good.* (not *are*)
  • *Your advice was very helpful.*
  • *All the equipment has been checked.*

4. Mistakes the examiner sees every year

Watch out for these
  • *we was* ✗ → *we were* ✓
  • *he don't* ✗ → *he doesn't* ✓
  • *there was many people* ✗ → *there were many people* ✓
  • *everyone have* ✗ → *everyone has* ✓
  • *the information were* ✗ → *the information was* ✓
  • *my sister always forget* ✗ → *my sister always forgets* ✓
  • *one of the boys are* ✗ → *one of the boys is* ✓

5. How Checkpoint asks about it

Agreement is mostly tested in your writing. It is part of Grammar and punctuation (7 marks): the examiner looks for sentences that are grammatically accurate. Small agreement errors like *we was* or *there is many* repeated through a piece can pull this mark down quickly.

In the reading section
  • You may be asked to choose the correct verb to complete a sentence, or to spot which sentence is grammatically correct.
  • Read every option with the subject: say the subject + verb together quietly in your head (*Each of the girls ... has*).

When you proofread, check every *was/were*, *is/are*, *has/have* and *does/do*. Ask: what is the subject? Is it one or more than one?

6. Practice

  1. Choose: *Neither my brother nor my sisters (like / likes) fish.*

    Show answer
    like: the verb agrees with the nearer subject, *sisters* (plural).

  2. Choose: *Each of the players (has / have) a shirt.*

    Show answer
    has: *each* is singular.

  3. Correct: *We was waiting at the gate for an hour.*

    Show answer
    *We were waiting at the gate for an hour.*

  4. Choose: *The basket of oranges (is / are) heavy.*

    Show answer
    is: the subject is *the basket* (singular), not *oranges*.

  5. Correct: *There is lots of cars in the street today.*

    Show answer
    *There are lots of cars in the street today.* (*cars* is plural)

  6. Choose: *Salma and her cousin (go / goes) swimming on Fridays.*

    Show answer
    go: two subjects joined by *and* are plural.

  7. Correct: *My dad don't like spicy food.*

    Show answer
    *My dad doesn't like spicy food.*

  8. Choose: *The news about the match (was / were) exciting.*

    Show answer
    was: *news* is uncountable, so it is singular.

  9. Choose: *One of the windows (is / are) broken.*

    Show answer
    is: *one of* takes a singular verb.

  10. Choose: *Everybody in my class (want / wants) a school trip.*

    Show answer
    wants: *everybody* is singular.
