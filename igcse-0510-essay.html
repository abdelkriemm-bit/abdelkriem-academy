IGCSE 0510 · Paper 1 Writing

Writing an Essay (Exercise 6)

How to write a full-mark Exercise 6 essay: for-and-against, opinion, advantages/disadvantages and problem–solution structures, thesis statements, topic sentences, discourse markers, formal register and two annotated model essays.

British & IGCSE › Cambridge IGCSE English as a Second Language (0510)

Many students prepare only for the article, the report and the review – and then panic when Exercise 6 asks for an essay. The syllabus lists four text types for Exercise 6: article, report, essay or review. So the essay must be in your toolkit too. The good news is that an exam essay is the most logical text type of all: a clear position, two or three well-supported paragraphs and a conclusion.

This page follows the same method as the classroom writing book: the step-by-step structure for each type of essay, the commonly used phrases and clauses, and samples with comments.

1. What the task is and where it sits in the paper

ItemWhat you need to know
PaperPaper 1 Reading and Writing (2 hours, 60 marks): four reading exercises (30 marks) + two writing exercises (30 marks).
ExerciseExercise 6 (Question 17). The question tells you which text type to write: article, report, essay or review.
What an essay isA piece of writing on a topic that presents an argument or a point of view. It has an introduction, a main body and a conclusion, and opinions are supported by reasons and examples.
RegisterFormal or semi-formal. An essay for your teacher should be formal: no contractions, no slang, no chatty questions to a friend.
LengthAbout 120–160 words.
Marks15: Content up to 6 + Language up to 9.
What the question gives youUsually a statement or question to discuss, the reader (often your teacher), and some comments with different views. Use some of them and add ideas of your own.
How an essay task can look (original example)
  • Situation: ‘Your class has been discussing whether students should be allowed to use mobile phones during the school day.’
  • Instruction: ‘Your teacher has asked you to write an essay giving your views.’
  • Comments: ‘Phones are useful for research in lessons.’ / ‘People spend break time staring at screens instead of talking.’ / ‘Parents need to be able to contact us.’ / ‘Phones make cheating easier.’
  • ‘The comments above may give you some ideas, and you should also use some ideas of your own. Write about 120 to 160 words.’

Read the instruction carefully: does it ask for your views (opinion essay), both sides (for and against), advantages and disadvantages, or solutions to a problem? That decides which of the four structures below you use.

2. Marks: what each band needs

Content (6 marks)What it means for an essay
5–6Task fully done and completely relevant. Consistently formal essay style. A clear position that suits the question and the reader. Every argument is developed with a reason and an example.
3–4Task generally done. Some arguments are listed rather than explained, the position may be unclear, or the style slips into informal language.
1–2Only part of the task is done (e.g. one side only when both are asked for). Ideas are thin or off the topic.
0Nothing that can be credited.
Language (9 marks)What it means
7–9Skilful communication. Wide range of vocabulary (including abstract words such as ‘benefit’, ‘responsibility’, ‘distraction’) and of structures (complex sentences, conditionals, passives). Highly accurate. Logically organised with a wide range of discourse markers.
4–6Clear. Mostly common vocabulary and simple sentences, with some complex ones. Generally accurate. Some linking, perhaps repetitive (‘also… also… also’).
1–3Meaning not always clear. Very simple language, frequent errors, little organisation.
0Nothing that can be credited.

3. How to get full marks: checklist

  1. A short title (optional but helpful) that states the topic.
  2. An introduction that introduces the topic and ends with a thesis statement (your position, or what the essay will do).
  3. Each body paragraph starts with a clear topic sentence.
  4. Every argument is developed: reason + example or result.
  5. Some ideas from the comments plus at least one or two of your own.
  6. A range of discourse markers: addition, contrast, cause and effect, concession.
  7. Formal register: no contractions (do not, cannot), no slang, no exclamation marks, no ‘you guys’.
  8. A conclusion that sums up and gives a final judgement – no new arguments.
  9. Your position is consistent from the introduction to the conclusion.
  10. About 120–160 words: four short paragraphs.

4. The building blocks: thesis statement and topic sentences

Thesis statement = the last sentence of your introduction. It tells the reader what the essay will argue. Topic sentence = the first sentence of a body paragraph. It tells the reader what this paragraph is about. If the examiner reads only your thesis and your topic sentences, they should understand your whole argument.

Essay typeExample thesis statement
For and against‘There are strong arguments on both sides of this debate.’ / ‘This essay will consider both sides before reaching a conclusion.’
Opinion‘In my opinion, schools should ban phones during lessons, but not during break time.’
Advantages and disadvantages‘Living in a big city has clear benefits, but it also brings serious problems.’
Problem and solution‘Fortunately, there are several practical ways in which this problem could be reduced.’

Topic sentences

  • The main argument in favour of… is that…
  • Another important reason why… is…
  • Opponents of this idea point out that…
  • One clear disadvantage of… is…
  • The most effective solution would be to…
  • Perhaps the strongest argument against… is…

5. The four types of essay and their structures

All four have the same frame – introduction, main body, conclusion – but the main body is organised differently.

Paragraph(a) For and against(b) Opinion (agree/disagree)(c) Advantages / disadvantages(d) Problem and solution
1 IntroductionIntroduce the topic, say people disagree. Neutral thesis.Introduce the topic, state your opinion clearly.Introduce the topic. Thesis: it has benefits and drawbacks.Describe the problem briefly and say why it matters.
2 BodyArguments FOR (2 points, each with an example).Reason 1 with example.Advantages (2 points with examples).Cause(s) of the problem, or Solution 1 + how it works.
3 BodyArguments AGAINST (2 points, each with an example).Reason 2 with example + concession (‘Admittedly…, however…’).Disadvantages (2 points with examples).Solution 2 (+ Solution 3) + expected result.
4 ConclusionWeigh up both sides. Your opinion appears here for the first time.Restate your opinion in new words.Overall judgement: do advantages outweigh disadvantages?The best solution and a hopeful final sentence.

Type (a): For and against (balanced) essay

  • Use it when the question says ‘discuss both sides’, ‘give the arguments for and against’ or ‘consider both points of view’.
  • Keep the introduction neutral. Do not give your opinion until the conclusion.
  • Give both sides roughly equal space. One weak sentence on the other side is not enough.
  • Signal the change of side clearly: ‘On the one hand…’ / ‘On the other hand…’.
  • Conclusion: weigh up both sides and say which you find stronger, or suggest a balanced compromise.

Type (b): Opinion essay (agree or disagree)

  • Use it when the question asks ‘Do you agree?’ or ‘What is your opinion?’.
  • This is a persuasive essay, so each body paragraph can follow PEEL: Point (topic sentence), Explain, Example, Link back to your opinion.
  • State your opinion in the introduction and keep it the same until the end.
  • Add a concession: admit one argument on the other side and answer it (‘Admittedly… However…’). This makes your argument stronger, not weaker.
  • Conclusion: restate your opinion in different words and end with a strong final sentence.

Type (c): Advantages and disadvantages essay

  • Use it when the question asks about ‘the advantages and disadvantages’ or ‘the benefits and drawbacks’ of something (online shopping, living in a city, social media).
  • Paragraph 2 = advantages, paragraph 3 = disadvantages. Do not mix them.
  • Use abstract nouns: benefit, drawback, convenience, risk, impact.
  • Conclusion: say whether the advantages outweigh the disadvantages, or under what conditions they do.

Type (d): Problem and solution essay

  • Use it when the question asks what can be done about a problem (litter, traffic, too much screen time, bullying).
  • Introduction: describe the problem and its effects in one or two sentences.
  • Body: two or three realistic solutions. For each, say who should do it, how, and what the result would be.
  • Useful grammar: modals (should, could, ought to), the second conditional (‘If…, … would…’), passives (‘more bins should be provided’).
  • Conclusion: the most effective solution and a hopeful final thought.

6. Discourse markers

Discourse markers are the signposts of an essay. Using a range of them correctly is part of the Language mark. Learn at least three from each row.

FunctionDiscourse markersExample
Additionfurthermore, moreover, in addition, what is more, not only… but alsoFurthermore, no time is wasted travelling.
Contrasthowever, on the other hand, whereas, while, in contrastTeachers can see confusion at once, whereas online it is easy to hide.
Cause and effectas a result, therefore, consequently, because of, this means that, due toConsequently, many students arrive tired.
Concessionalthough, even though, despite, admittedly, it is true that… howeverAdmittedly, volunteering takes time. However, two hours a month is enough.
Examplefor example, for instance, such as, a good example of this isA student who finds algebra difficult, for example, can replay a lesson.
Sequencefirstly, secondly, the main reason, another reason, finallyThe main reason is that…
Conclusionin conclusion, to sum up, on balance, all things considered, overallOn balance, the advantages outweigh the disadvantages.
Use ‘however’ correctly

‘However’ starts a new sentence and is followed by a comma: ‘Phones are useful. However, they are also distracting.’ Do not join two sentences with a comma before ‘however’ (‘Phones are useful, however they are distracting’ is a punctuation error). ‘Although’ is different: it joins two clauses in one sentence (‘Although phones are useful, they are distracting.’).

8. How to start: openings

Introducing the topic

  • In recent years, [topic] has become a subject of heated debate.
  • It is often said that… but is this really true?
  • Nowadays, more and more [young people] are…
  • Few issues divide [parents and teenagers] as much as…

Thesis statements

  • There are strong arguments on both sides.
  • In my opinion, … because…
  • This essay will examine the main advantages and disadvantages of…
  • However, there are several practical ways to tackle this problem.

9. Commonly used phrases and clauses for the body

Giving arguments

  • The main argument in favour of… is that…
  • Supporters of this idea claim that…
  • It could be argued that…
  • This is particularly true of…, who…

Developing an argument

  • This means that…
  • As a result, …
  • A good example of this is…
  • If…, … would…

Opposing views and concession

  • On the other hand, opponents point out that…
  • Admittedly, … However, …
  • Although it is true that…, …
  • Despite this, …

Solutions

  • One effective solution would be to…
  • The government / the school / parents should…
  • If…were introduced, …would…
  • This would encourage… to…

10. How to end: conclusions

  • In conclusion, although online learning is a useful tool, it should support school rather than replace it.
  • To sum up, the benefits of volunteering far outweigh the small amount of time it requires.
  • On balance, city life offers more opportunities, but only to those who can cope with its pressures.
  • All things considered, the problem can be reduced if schools, families and students work together.
  • For these reasons, I firmly believe that…
The golden rule of conclusions

No new arguments in the conclusion. Sum up, give your final judgement, and finish with one strong sentence (a recommendation, a hope for the future, or a balanced compromise).

12. Timing

Paper 1 lasts 2 hours. We suggest (as advice, not a rule) about 25 minutes for Exercise 6: 1 minute to read the question and decide the essay type, 2–3 minutes to plan the thesis and topic sentences, about 18 minutes to write, and 3–4 minutes to check register, discourse markers and spelling.

13. Model answer 1 – For and against essay

For and against essay150 words · Content 6/6 + Language 9/9 = 15/15
Task: Your class has been discussing whether students can learn as well at home with online lessons as they can at school. Your teacher has asked you to write an essay giving the arguments for and against. Comments from students: ‘At home I can study at my own speed.’ / ‘I’d really miss my friends.’ / ‘There are fewer distractions at home.’ / ‘Online, teachers can’t see if you’re lost.’ The comments above may give you some ideas, and you should also use some ideas of your own. Write about 120 to 160 words.

Learning Online at Home: A Step Forward?

Since online lessons became common, many families have wondered whether learning at home could replace traditional schooling. There are strong arguments on both sides.

On the one hand, studying at home allows students to work at their own speed. A student who finds algebra difficult, for example, can replay a recorded lesson as many times as necessary. Furthermore, no time is wasted travelling, which is a real advantage in a crowded city like Cairo.

On the other hand, school offers far more than lessons. Students learn to cooperate, make friends and solve problems together, and a screen cannot teach these skills. In addition, teachers in a classroom notice immediately when someone is confused, whereas online it is easy to hide behind a switched-off camera.

In conclusion, although online learning is a useful tool, I believe it should support school rather than replace it.

Why this gets the marks
  • Text type and register (Content): a real essay – introduction, two body paragraphs, conclusion – in formal English with no contractions, written for a teacher.
  • Neutral introduction (Content): the thesis ‘There are strong arguments on both sides’ is right for a for-and-against essay. The writer’s opinion appears only in the conclusion.
  • Balance (Content): one full paragraph for each side, each with two developed arguments.
  • Prompts + own ideas (Content): ‘own speed’, ‘friends’ and ‘teachers can’t see’ are used in new words. Own ideas include the algebra example, travelling time in Cairo, cooperation skills and the switched-off camera.
  • Topic sentences (Language): ‘studying at home allows students to work at their own speed’ and ‘school offers far more than lessons’ announce each paragraph clearly.
  • Discourse markers (Language): On the one hand, for example, Furthermore, On the other hand, In addition, whereas, In conclusion, although – addition, contrast, example and concession are all covered.
  • Structures and vocabulary (Language): relative clauses (‘who finds…’, ‘which is a real advantage…’), passive (‘no time is wasted’), less common vocabulary (traditional schooling, replay, cooperate, confused, switched-off).
  • Conclusion (Content): sums up both sides in one sentence with a clear, balanced judgement and no new arguments.
  • Word count: 150 words including the title – inside the 120–160 guide.

14. Model answer 2 – Opinion essay

Opinion essay (agree/disagree)160 words · Content 6/6 + Language 9/9 = 15/15
Task: Your teacher has asked you to write an essay giving your opinion on this statement: ‘All young people should do some unpaid volunteer work in their community.’ Comments: ‘It teaches you to be responsible.’ / ‘We already have too much homework.’ / ‘Charities really need the help.’ / ‘It looks good when you apply to university.’ The comments above may give you some ideas, and you should also use some ideas of your own. Write about 120 to 160 words.

Should Volunteering Be Part of Growing Up?

It is often said that teenagers are too busy with screens to care about their communities. In my opinion, every young person should do some volunteer work, as it benefits both society and the volunteers themselves.

The most obvious reason is that volunteers meet real needs. Teenagers who help at an animal shelter or teach younger children to read are giving time that many charities could never afford to pay for, so the whole community benefits.

Moreover, volunteering builds character. When students organise a food collection for poorer families, for instance, they learn responsibility, teamwork and patience. These are qualities that no textbook can provide.

Admittedly, some people argue that students already have too much homework. However, even two hours a month would make a difference without affecting their studies.

To sum up, volunteering should be encouraged in every school, because those who give their time often gain far more than they expect.

Why this gets the marks
  • Clear position (Content): the thesis in the introduction (‘every young person should do some volunteer work’) is repeated in new words in the conclusion. The opinion never changes.
  • PEEL in the body (Content): this is persuasive writing, so each body paragraph makes a Point (‘volunteers meet real needs’), Explains it, gives an Example (animal shelter, food collection) and Links back to the opinion (‘so the whole community benefits’, ‘These are qualities that no textbook can provide’).
  • Concession (Content and Language): the homework argument is admitted and answered (‘Admittedly… However…’), which makes the essay more convincing.
  • Prompts + own ideas (Content): ‘responsible’, ‘homework’ and ‘charities need help’ are used in new words. Own ideas include the screens hook, animal shelters, teaching younger children and the ‘two hours a month’ compromise.
  • Register (Content): formal throughout – no contractions, no slang, no exclamation marks.
  • Structures (Language): relative clauses (‘who help…’, ‘that many charities could never afford…’, ‘who give their time’), a time clause (‘When students organise…’), passive (‘should be encouraged’), a second-conditional idea (‘would make a difference’).
  • Vocabulary (Language): meet real needs, builds character, responsibility, qualities, affecting their studies, gain far more than they expect.
  • Discourse markers (Language): It is often said that, In my opinion, The most obvious reason, Moreover, for instance, Admittedly, However, To sum up, because.
  • Word count: 160 words including the title – inside the 120–160 guide.

15. Plans for the other two types

(c) Advantages and disadvantages – ‘Write an essay for your teacher about the advantages and disadvantages of living in a big city.’Plan
IntroductionMore and more families are moving to big cities such as Cairo. Thesis: city life has clear benefits but also serious drawbacks.
AdvantagesBetter schools, universities and hospitals nearby. Wide choice of jobs. Entertainment: museums, sports clubs, cinemas.
DisadvantagesTraffic jams waste hours every day. Noise and air pollution affect health. Higher rents and prices.
ConclusionOn balance, the advantages outweigh the disadvantages for young people, but only if they live near their school or work.
(d) Problem and solution – ‘Many teenagers spend too much time on screens. Write an essay for your teacher suggesting what could be done.’Plan
IntroductionThe problem: many teenagers spend over six hours a day on phones. Effects: poor sleep, less exercise, weaker concentration. Thesis: several practical solutions exist.
Solution 1: FamiliesPhone-free family meals and no phones in bedrooms after 10 p.m. Result: better sleep.
Solution 2: SchoolsMore after-school clubs and sports. Lessons on healthy technology use. Result: real-life alternatives.
Solution 3: Teenagers themselvesUse screen-time limits built into phones. Turn off notifications while studying.
ConclusionNo single solution is enough. If families, schools and teenagers work together, the problem can be greatly reduced.

16. Practice tasks

  1. Task 1: ‘Some people think that school uniforms should be abolished.’ Your teacher has asked you to write an essay giving the arguments for and against. Write a neutral thesis statement and two topic sentences.

    Show answer
    Thesis: ‘There are convincing arguments both for and against keeping school uniforms.’ Topic sentence 1: ‘The main argument in favour of uniforms is that they make all students equal, whatever their family’s income.’ Topic sentence 2: ‘On the other hand, opponents claim that uniforms prevent young people from expressing their personality.’

  2. Task 2: ‘Homework should be banned in secondary schools.’ Do you agree? Make a PEEL plan for one body paragraph of an opinion essay that disagrees with the statement.

    Show answer
    Point: homework helps students remember what they learnt in class. Explain: practising at home moves information into long-term memory. Example: a student who practises ten new words each evening will remember them before a test far better than one who never reviews. Link: therefore, banning homework would make learning less effective, so it should be kept but limited.

  3. Task 3: Rewrite this informal paragraph in formal essay style: ‘Loads of kids don’t do sport anymore. It’s really bad cos they get unhealthy. Schools should do something!!’

    Show answer
    ‘A large number of young people no longer take part in regular sport. This is a serious problem, as a lack of exercise can lead to health problems such as weight gain. Schools should therefore take action, for example by offering more after-school sports clubs.’

  4. Task 4: Choose the correct discourse marker: (a) ‘___ public transport is cheap, many people still prefer to drive.’ (However / Although) (b) ‘Plastic bags harm sea animals. ___, many countries have banned them.’ (As a result / Whereas) (c) ‘City life is exciting, ___ village life is peaceful.’ (whereas / moreover)

    Show answer
    (a) Although – it joins two clauses in one sentence. (b) As a result – it shows the effect. (c) whereas – it shows contrast inside one sentence.

  5. Task 5: ‘Many students say there is too much litter in and around the school.’ Write a problem–solution essay plan for your head teacher’s essay competition.

    Show answer
    Introduction: litter in the playground and outside the gate. Effects: insects, a bad image of the school, danger to animals. Thesis: practical solutions exist. Solution 1: more bins, including recycling bins, at the places where students eat. Solution 2: a weekly class cleaning rota with points for the cleanest class. Solution 3: a ban on single-use plastic in the canteen. Conclusion: if students take responsibility, the school can become cleaner and greener.
