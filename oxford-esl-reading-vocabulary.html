Reading: Vocabulary in Context

How to answer every vocabulary question without a dictionary: explain-in-your-own-words, context clues, prefixes and suffixes, synonyms, idioms and figurative language, with worked examples and 20 practice items.

British & IGCSE › Oxford International GCSE English as a Second Language

Vocabulary questions appear on every text of the Reading paper, and they become more valuable as the texts get harder. In Text 4 one sentence to explain can be worth 3 marks. You cannot use a dictionary, so you need a method for working out meaning from the text itself. This page teaches that method, the six types of context clues, how words are built, idioms and figurative language, and gives you lots of worked examples and practice.

What vocabulary questions look like

Form of the questionUsual marksWhat you do
In paragraph 2, the writer says, 'given a weekly allowance'. Explain in your own words what 'allowance' means.1Give one synonym or a short explanation: money given regularly (e.g. by parents) to spend.
Explain in your own words what the phrase 'a hectic schedule' means.2Explain both ideas: hectic (very busy, rushed) + schedule (timetable/plan).
Explain in your own words what this sentence means.3Explain three ideas, usually three key words or parts of the sentence.
What does the phrase '...' mean? (A, B or C)1Choose the option that matches the meaning in context, often for figurative phrases.
How these questions are marked
  • One mark = one idea. For a 2-mark phrase the examiner looks for two ideas, usually one for each key word. Explaining only one gives 1 mark; explaining one part partly can also give 1.
  • Synonyms and explanations are both accepted. Many different correct words can score, so you do not need the 'perfect' word.
  • Your English does not need to be perfect. The examiner rewards the meaning you show.
  • Repeating the word from the text does not show understanding, so it does not score. 'Roofless means it has no roof' is fine, but 'roofless means roofless' is not.

The 5-step method for 'Explain in your own words'

  1. Count the marks. That is the number of ideas you need.
  2. Split the phrase into its meaning parts. Usually each important word (noun, adjective, verb) is one idea: 'a | breathtaking | view'.
  3. Read the sentence before and after. These are your clues: examples, contrast words, causes, results.
  4. Find a simpler word or a short explanation for each part. Check it fits the context.
  5. Write one clear sentence that contains all the ideas, then test it: put your explanation in place of the phrase in the original sentence. Does the sentence still make sense?

Worked example (3 marks). Text: 'High on the hill, the old fort gazes down on a restless harbour.' Question: Explain in your own words what this sentence means.

PartIdeaYour words
the old fortidea 1: the ancient castle / old military building'The ancient castle'
gazes down onidea 2: looks at it from above; it stands higher and faces it'stands above and looks over'
a restless harbouridea 3: a port that is always busy and moving'a port which is always busy, full of moving boats'

Full-mark answer: The ancient castle stands high above and looks over a port that is always busy and full of movement. (3 ideas = 3 marks.)

The six types of context clues

When you meet an unknown word, the text around it often explains it. Learn to spot these six clue types.

Clue typeSignal wordsExampleMeaning you can work out
1 Definitionis, means, is called, that is, commas or dashes'The town is famous for its souk, a traditional open-air market.'souk = a traditional open-air market
2 Synonym / restatementor, in other words, also, similarly'The trip was arduous, or in other words, really exhausting.'arduous = very hard and tiring
3 Examplesuch as, for example, like, including, a colon'Pack your toiletries, such as toothpaste, soap and shampoo.'toiletries = things for washing and cleaning yourself
4 Contrastbut, however, although, unlike, whereas, instead of'Karim is talkative, but his brother is taciturn and rarely speaks.'taciturn = quiet and does not talk much
5 Cause and effectbecause, so, as a result, therefore, due to'The road was impassable because of the flood, so the bus turned back.'impassable = impossible to travel along
6 General sense / moodthe whole sentence and the writer's feelings'The children squealed with delight when they saw the clowns.'squealed = made high, excited sounds
Use what you already know
  • Theme knowledge: if the text is about charity, 'sponsor', 'donate' and 'raise funds' are likely; see the Themes and topics page.
  • Grammar clues: is the word a noun (after 'a/the'), a verb (after 'to' or a subject), or an adjective (before a noun)? This narrows the meaning.
  • Positive or negative? Even if you only know that a word is positive, you can usually choose the right MCQ option or write a sensible explanation.

How words are built: suffixes

A suffix comes at the end of a word. It often tells you the word class (noun, adjective, verb, adverb) and sometimes changes the meaning.

SuffixMeaning / word classExamples
-lesswithout (adjective)roofless, homeless, endless, careless, spotless
-fulfull of (adjective)peaceful, colourful, stressful
-able / -iblecan be done (adjective)affordable, reachable, visible
-ous / -ive / -alhaving the quality of (adjective)dangerous, creative, cultural
-ness / -ity / -ment / -ionturns words into nounskindness, popularity, achievement, pollution
-er / -or / -ist / -eea person whovolunteer, visitor, tourist, employee
-ise / -en / -ifyto make (verb)modernise, widen, simplify
-lyin this way (adverb)quickly, gracefully, gently

Worked example (1 mark). 'After the earthquake, a row of roofless houses stood by the river.' Explain 'roofless'. → roof + -less (without) = without a roof / with no roof on top.

Worked example (1 mark). 'The prices at the new café are surprisingly affordable.' Explain 'affordable'. → afford (have enough money for) + -able (can be) = cheap enough for people to pay / not too expensive.

Synonyms: choosing the right one

A synonym is a word with the same or a similar meaning. But many words have several meanings, and only one fits the text. Always use the substitution test: put your synonym in the sentence and read it.

SentenceWordWrong synonymRight synonym (in context)
'The school has seen a sharp increase in student numbers.'sharppointedsudden and large
'The museum is a must for any visitor.'mustan obligation by lawsomething you should not miss
'She is a bright student.'brightfull of lightclever, intelligent
'The new rule will affect the whole school.'affectfeel emotionchange, have an influence on
'He runs a small bakery.'runsmoves fast on footmanages, is in charge of

Phrases, idioms and figurative language

Text 4 (literary non-fiction) is full of descriptive and figurative language. You must explain what it means, not what the words say literally.

TypeWhat it isExampleMeaning
Similecompares using 'like' or 'as''The streets were as quiet as a library.'very quiet, almost silent
Metaphorsays something IS something else'The city was an oven in August.'the city was extremely hot
Personificationgives human actions to things'The wind howled through the empty streets.'the wind made a loud, long, sad sound
Idioma fixed phrase with a special meaning'Learning to drive was a piece of cake.'very easy
Phrasal verbverb + small word with a new meaning'The trip was called off because of the storm.'cancelled
Hyperboledeliberate exaggeration'I have told you a million times.'very many times

Do not explain figurative language literally

'The city was an oven' does NOT mean there was an oven. Ask: what do the two things have in common? (An oven is very hot.) Then explain that shared quality in context: the city was extremely hot.

MCQ meaning questions (and a 'find a word' drill)

  • For 'find a word that means...' (a useful practice drill rather than a usual exam question): check the part of speech. If the meaning is 'very old' (adjective), look for an adjective: ancient. Copy it exactly with correct spelling.
  • For MCQ meaning questions: one option is usually a literal meaning (the 'word-for-word' trap), one is the opposite or unrelated, and one is the meaning in context. Choose the one that fits the whole paragraph.
  • Substitute each option into the sentence. The right one keeps the meaning of the paragraph.

Worked example (MCQ). 'Years after the factory closed, its noise still echoes in the memories of the villagers.' What does 'still echoes in the memories' mean? A The factory is still noisy. B The villagers still remember the factory clearly. C The villagers cannot hear well. → B. A is the literal trap: the factory has closed. C is unrelated.

Phrase (marks)0 marks1 markFull marks

How to answer without a dictionary: a checklist

  • Do not panic at one unknown word: you rarely need every word to answer.
  • Look at the word parts: prefix, root, suffix.
  • Read one sentence before and one sentence after.
  • Look for signal words: such as, but, because, in other words.
  • Decide: positive or negative? noun, verb or adjective?
  • Make a guess and test it by substitution.
  • Write the meaning simply. Simple words are safer than impressive ones.

Practice: 20 vocabulary questions

Cover the answers. For each question, count the marks, find the ideas, then check.

  1. 'Volunteers at the clinic receive no payment.' Explain 'volunteers'. [1]

    Show answer
    People who work/help without being paid (because they want to).

  2. 'The festival attracts thousands of spectators every year.' Explain 'spectators'. [1]

    Show answer
    People who watch (an event/show).

  3. 'Our journey was arduous: twelve hours on a bumpy road with no air conditioning.' Explain 'arduous'. [1]

    Show answer
    Very difficult and tiring. Clue: the example after the colon.

  4. 'The beach was spotless; not a single plastic bottle could be seen.' Explain 'spotless'. [1]

    Show answer
    Completely clean (spot = dirty mark + -less = without). Clue: the restatement after the semicolon.

  5. 'Tickets are non-refundable.' Explain 'non-refundable'. [1]

    Show answer
    You cannot get your money back (non- = not, refund = give money back, -able = can be).

  6. Explain 'a hectic schedule'. [2]

    Show answer
    A timetable/plan of activities (schedule) that is extremely busy and rushed (hectic).

  7. 'The old souk is a maze of narrow alleys.' Explain 'a maze of narrow alleys'. [2]

    Show answer
    A confusing network where it is easy to get lost (maze) of small, thin streets/paths (narrow alleys).

  8. 'The coach was a tower of strength for the team.' Explain 'a tower of strength'. [2]

    Show answer
    Someone who gives a lot of support (strength) and whom others can depend on, like something strong and solid (tower).

  9. 'Staying up all night to revise is a false economy.' What does 'a false economy' mean? A a way to save money B something that seems to help but actually causes problems C a shop with fake products [1]

    Show answer
    B. You think you are saving time, but tiredness makes your exam worse.

  10. Find a word in this sentence that means 'very old': 'Near the ancient temple, children sold modern souvenirs.' [1]

    Show answer
    ancient

  11. 'Rents in the city centre have skyrocketed.' Explain 'skyrocketed'. [1]

    Show answer
    Increased very quickly and by a large amount.

  12. 'Although the hotel looked modest from outside, the rooms were luxurious.' Explain 'modest'. [1]

    Show answer
    Simple / plain / not grand or impressive. Clue: contrast with 'luxurious' after 'although'.

  13. 'The teacher's praise boosted my self-confidence.' Explain 'boosted my self-confidence'. [2]

    Show answer
    Increased/raised (boosted) my belief in my own abilities (self-confidence).

  14. 'Waves of tourists wash over the town every summer.' Explain this sentence. [3]

    Show answer
    Very large numbers of visitors (waves of tourists) arrive and fill/flood (wash over) the town every summer (time). Three ideas: large numbers, they fill the town, every summer.

  15. 'The village seemed frozen in time.' Explain 'frozen in time'. [2]

    Show answer
    It has not changed at all (frozen) for many years, so it still looks as it did in the past (in time).

  16. 'She bit off more than she could chew when she joined four clubs.' What does the idiom mean? [1]

    Show answer
    She tried to do more than she was able to manage.

  17. 'The plan was put on hold.' Explain 'put on hold'. [1]

    Show answer
    Stopped/delayed for a period of time (to continue later).

  18. 'The desert stretched out, vast and silent, beneath a merciless sun.' Explain 'a merciless sun'. [2]

    Show answer
    Sunshine/heat (sun) that is extremely strong, harsh and never stops (merciless = showing no kindness or pity).

  19. Which prefix means 'wrongly', and which suffix means 'without'? Give an example of each.

    Show answer
    mis- (misunderstand) and -less (homeless).

  20. 'The museum's interactive displays let visitors touch and try things.' Explain 'interactive'. [1]

    Show answer
    Allowing people to take part / do things with it, not just look (inter- = between, act = do).
