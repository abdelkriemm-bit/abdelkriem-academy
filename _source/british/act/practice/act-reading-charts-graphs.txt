Each was planted in two fields: one with normal soil and one with salty soil.

**Table: Wheat yield in normal and salty soil (one season)**
Variety | Yield in normal soil (tonnes per hectare) | Yield in salty soil (tonnes per hectare) | Share of normal yield kept in salty soil

The first lesson of the trial is that no single variety was best in every situation. In normal soil, the standard variety remained the clear winner; the new lines all gave up some yield in exchange for their tolerance. In salty soil, however, every new line did better than the standard variety.

The breeders have set themselves a practical target: a line that produces at least 3.2 tonnes per hectare in salty soil without falling below 5.0 tonnes per hectare in normal soil, so that farmers whose fields are only partly salty do not lose too much on their better land. The breeders also know the limits of their evidence. One season in two fields cannot capture the range of soils, weather and water across the Delta.
