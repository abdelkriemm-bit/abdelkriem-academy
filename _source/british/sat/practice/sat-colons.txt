STEM: Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

## Practice 1 | Foundation
### Boundaries (colons)
P: The class needed three things for the experiment ______ a magnet, a compass, and a length of copper wire.
A) experiment; || What follows is a list, not a complete sentence.
*B) experiment: || Correct: a colon after a complete sentence introduces a list.
C) experiment || Without punctuation, the list runs into the sentence.
D) experiment. || The list would be a fragment.

P: The ingredients in the dish ______ rice, lentils, pasta, and fried onions.
A) include: || A colon cannot come between a verb and its objects; "The ingredients in the dish include" is not a complete sentence.
*B) include || Correct: no punctuation between the verb "include" and the list that completes it.
C) include; || A semicolon would leave the list as a fragment.
D) include, || A comma cannot separate a verb from its objects.

P: The museum has objects from many countries, such ______ Japan, Brazil, and Kenya.
A) as: || Never put a colon after "such as".
B) as; || A semicolon cannot follow "such as".
*C) as || Correct: no punctuation after "such as".
D) as, || A comma cannot separate "such as" from the examples.

P: The village is famous for one thing ______ its handmade pottery.
*A) thing: || Correct: a colon after a complete sentence introduces the thing it announces.
B) thing; || "its handmade pottery" is not a complete sentence.
C) thing. || "Its handmade pottery" would be a fragment.
D) thing, which is: || A colon cannot follow "which is".

P: The reasons for the delay ______ bad weather, a broken engine, and a missing pilot.
A) were: || "The reasons for the delay were" is not a complete sentence, so no colon.
B) were; || A semicolon would leave the list as a fragment.
C) were, || A comma cannot separate a verb from its complement.
*D) were || Correct: no punctuation.

P: Hiba packed only what she needed for the trip ______ a passport, a phone charger, and two books.
A) trip; || What follows is a list, not a complete sentence.
B) trip, including: || A colon cannot follow "including".
*C) trip: || Correct: colon after a complete sentence to introduce a list.
D) trip || The list runs into the sentence.

P: The scientists faced a difficult question ______ how could a plant survive with almost no water?
A) question, and || "and" cannot introduce the question that explains "a difficult question"; the result is ungrammatical.
*B) question: || Correct: a colon introduces the question that the first clause announces.
C) question, || A comma creates a comma splice before the complete question.
D) question || Run-on.

P: The tour visits several famous sites, including ______ the Citadel, and the Hanging Church.
A) Al-Azhar Mosque: || A colon cannot separate list items.
B) Al-Azhar Mosque; || A semicolon cannot separate simple list items.
*C) Al-Azhar Mosque, || Correct: comma between list items (no colon after "including").
D) Al-Azhar Mosque || Two items run together.

P: My grandfather's advice was always the ______ work hard, be honest, and help your neighbours.
A) same; || The commands that follow explain "the same"; a semicolon does not introduce an explanation like this.
B) same, || A comma does not introduce the list of advice.
*C) same: || Correct: a colon after a complete sentence introduces the explanation.
D) same || Run-on.

P: The new phone comes in three colours ______ black, silver, and green.
*A) colours: || Correct.
B) colours; || The list is not a complete sentence.
C) colours, such as: || A colon cannot follow "such as".
D) colours. || Fragment.
