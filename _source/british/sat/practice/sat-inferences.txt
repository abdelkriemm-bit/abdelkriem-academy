## Practice 1 | Foundation
### Inferences
P: Most plants need sunlight to make food. Mushrooms, however, are not plants; they are fungi and get their food from dead leaves and wood. This explains why mushrooms ______
A) grow only in bright sunlight. || They do not need sunlight to make food.
*B) can grow well in dark, shady places. || Correct: they do not depend on sunlight.
C) are always green. || Not suggested; green colour comes from sunlight-using chlorophyll.
D) make food in the same way as trees. || They get food differently.

P: The café near the university is always crowded during exam weeks, but it is almost empty in August. This pattern suggests that most of the café's customers ______
*A) are students. || Correct: busy during exams, empty in the summer holiday.
B) are tourists. || Tourists would come in August.
C) live far from the university. || Not supported.
D) dislike coffee. || Not supported.

P: In the experiment, plants in pots with holes in the bottom grew well, while identical plants in pots without holes developed rotten roots. The researchers concluded that the plants were damaged by ______
A) too little water. || Pots without holes keep more water, not less.
B) too much sunlight. || Light was not different.
*C) water that could not drain away. || Correct.
D) the colour of the pots. || Not suggested.

P: Hana's grandmother keeps every jar, box, and piece of string, saying that "everything has a second life." It is likely that Hana's grandmother ______
A) buys new things whenever she can. || The opposite.
B) dislikes her house being tidy. || Not supported.
*C) believes in reusing things rather than throwing them away. || Correct.
D) collects valuable antiques. || Jars and string are not antiques.

P: The town was built on a hill above the river. When the river flooded in 1950, the fields below were covered in water, but the town itself ______
A) was completely destroyed. || Being above the river suggests it was safe.
*B) stayed dry. || Correct.
C) moved to the valley. || Not supported.
D) was built in 1950. || Not supported.

P: Penguins cannot fly, but their wings are shaped like flippers and their bodies are smooth and streamlined. These features suggest that penguins are well adapted for ______
A) climbing trees. || Not supported.
B) running across snow. || Not supported.
C) flying long distances. || They cannot fly.
*D) swimming. || Correct.

P: Every shop on the street sold the same cheap souvenirs, except one small shop that sold handmade pottery made by local families. Tourists who wanted something unique ______
A) avoided the small shop. || Unique items were there.
*B) were likely to visit the small shop. || Correct.
C) could find unique items in every shop. || The other shops sold the same items.
D) did not buy any souvenirs. || Not supported.

P: Omar studied for his maths test for two weeks but did not study at all for his history test. When the results came, his maths score was much higher. This suggests that, for Omar, ______
*A) studying improved his results. || Correct.
B) history is easier than maths. || The higher score was in maths.
C) studying made no difference. || The studied subject scored higher.
D) he dislikes maths. || Not supported.

P: The museum's ancient papyrus scrolls are kept in dark rooms with no windows, and visitors may not use flash photography. These rules suggest that strong light ______
A) helps preserve papyrus. || The rules avoid light.
B) has no effect on papyrus. || Then the rules would be unnecessary.
*C) can damage papyrus. || Correct.
D) makes papyrus easier to read. || Not supported.

P: Farmers in the valley plant their seeds in October, after the summer heat ends and the first rains arrive. If the rains are late one year, farmers are likely to ______
A) plant in July instead. || That is the hottest time.
*B) delay planting until the rain comes. || Correct.
C) stop farming permanently. || Too extreme.
D) plant twice as many seeds in October. || Not logical without rain.

## Practice 2 | Foundation
### Inferences
P: Camels can close their nostrils and have two rows of long eyelashes. These features are especially useful in the desert because they help camels ______
A) find water underground. || Not related to nostrils and eyelashes.
*B) keep out blowing sand. || Correct.
C) see in the dark. || Not supported.
D) run faster. || Not supported.

P: The restaurant's owner noticed that customers ordered more desserts when the dessert menu had pictures than when it had only words. To sell more desserts, the owner will most likely ______
*A) use a dessert menu with pictures. || Correct.
B) remove desserts from the menu. || Opposite of the goal.
C) use a menu with only words. || That sold fewer desserts.
D) raise dessert prices. || Not suggested by the observation.

P: In the story, Karim always walks the long way home to avoid passing the old house on the corner. Karim most likely ______
A) lives in the old house. || Then he would not avoid it.
*B) feels uneasy about the old house. || Correct.
C) does not know where the old house is. || He knows and avoids it.
D) enjoys walking past the old house. || The opposite.

P: Ice floats on water because it is less dense than liquid water. If ice were denser than water, lakes in cold countries would likely ______
A) never freeze. || Density does not stop freezing.
B) freeze only at the surface. || That is what happens now.
*C) freeze from the bottom up, as ice sank. || Correct.
D) become warmer in winter. || Not supported.

P: The bus to the village leaves only once a day, at 6 a.m. A visitor who arrives at the bus station at 7 a.m. will most likely ______
A) catch the bus easily. || It left an hour earlier.
B) find the bus waiting. || Not supported.
*C) have to wait until the next morning or find another way. || Correct.
D) find a bus every hour. || Only once a day.

P: The two sisters argued about everything—food, music, even the colour of the sky at sunset—yet each called the other her best friend. This suggests that their arguments ______
*A) did not damage their close relationship. || Correct.
B) ended their friendship. || They still call each other best friends.
C) were about serious matters only. || Some were trivial.
D) made them dislike each other. || The opposite.

P: Researchers found that seeds of a desert plant sprouted only after being soaked in water for several days. This may help the plant because it prevents seeds from sprouting after ______
A) a long rainy season. || A long rainy season would be good for sprouting.
*B) a single light shower that would not provide enough water. || Correct.
C) any rain at all. || They do sprout after enough rain.
D) a period of strong sunlight. || Not supported.

P: In the old photographs, the street is lined with tall palm trees. Today, there are no trees, only parked cars. The photographs suggest that the street ______
A) has never had trees. || The old photographs show trees.
B) has more trees today than in the past. || The opposite.
*C) has changed considerably over time. || Correct.
D) was built recently. || The old photographs show it existed earlier.

P: The library allows students to borrow up to five books at a time. Salma already has four books from the library and wants to borrow three more. She will most likely be allowed to borrow ______
A) all three books. || That would make seven.
*B) only one more book. || Correct: 4 + 1 = 5.
C) no more books. || She can have one more.
D) ten more books. || Far over the limit.

P: Elephants in the reserve often walk many kilometres to reach a particular hill, where they lick the soil. Scientists found that this soil is rich in salt. This suggests that the elephants ______
A) are lost. || They return deliberately.
B) are avoiding predators. || Not supported.
C) dislike the taste of salt. || The opposite.
*D) visit the hill to get salt their diet lacks. || Correct.

## Practice 3 | Exam level
### Inferences
P: Historians once believed that the medieval city traded mainly with nearby towns. However, archaeologists recently found Chinese porcelain and Indian beads in the city's markets, items that could have arrived only through long-distance routes. These findings suggest that the city ______
A) never traded with nearby towns. || The finds add to, not erase, local trade.
*B) was connected to trade networks that reached much farther than historians had thought. || Correct.
C) produced porcelain and beads itself. || The items came from China and India.
D) was abandoned before trade began. || Not supported.

P: In a study, people who were told that a medicine would make them sleepy reported feeling sleepier after taking it, even though the pills contained no medicine at all. This suggests that people's expectations ______
*A) can influence how they feel. || Correct.
B) have no effect on how they feel. || The study shows an effect.
C) make medicines more effective in every case. || Too broad.
D) depend only on the colour of pills. || Not supported.

P: The novelist wrote her first three books in Arabic. After moving to London, she wrote her fourth in English, saying she wanted to "reach readers who had never heard of my village." Her decision to switch languages was most likely motivated by a wish to ______
A) leave her village behind forever. || Not supported.
B) stop writing about her village. || She wants to share it.
*C) share her stories with a wider audience. || Correct.
D) prove that English is easier than Arabic. || Not supported.

P: In one coastal town, sea turtles stopped nesting on a popular beach after bright lights were installed along the shore. On a nearby beach that remained dark, nesting continued as before. This suggests that the turtles ______
A) prefer crowded beaches. || Not supported.
*B) avoid nesting on brightly lit beaches. || Correct.
C) nest only on beaches with lights. || The opposite.
D) have stopped nesting everywhere. || They continued on the dark beach.

P: The company's profits rose sharply in the year it began selling its products online, even though sales in its physical shops fell slightly. It can most reasonably be concluded that ______
*A) online sales more than made up for the drop in shop sales. || Correct.

C) the company closed all its shops. || Not supported.
D) online sales were smaller than shop sales. || Not supported.

P: Some birds that live on islands without predators have lost the ability to fly. Flying uses a great deal of energy. This suggests that, in the absence of predators, ______
A) flying became more useful. || The opposite.
*B) the advantages of flight may not have been worth its energy cost. || Correct.
C) the birds stopped needing food. || Not supported.
D) the birds grew smaller. || Not supported.

P: The poet's early poems are full of city noise: car horns, crowded markets, shouting traders. Her later poems, written after she moved to a village in the Delta, describe birdsong and wind in the trees. This change suggests that her poetry ______
A) never changed during her career. || It changed.
*B) was shaped by the places where she lived. || Correct.
C) became more critical of village life. || Not supported.

P: A study found that students who took handwritten notes understood lectures better than students who typed notes, even though the typists wrote down more words. The researchers suggested that handwriting forces students to summarise ideas. This suggests that understanding depends more on ______
A) how many words students write down. || The typists wrote more but understood less.
*B) processing ideas than on recording every word. || Correct.
C) how fast students can type. || Not supported.
D) the length of the lecture. || Not supported.

P: During the 2020 lockdowns, levels of air pollution in many cities fell sharply. Traffic was far lighter than usual during this time. This suggests that a major source of air pollution in these cities is ______
*A) road traffic. || Correct.
B) cooking at home. || More people were at home, yet pollution fell.
C) natural dust storms. || Not linked to the lockdowns.
D) forest fires. || Not supported.

P: The archaeologist noticed that the pottery at the lower levels of the site was plain and roughly made, while the pottery at higher levels was finely decorated. Because lower levels are usually older, this suggests that ______
A) the people at the site stopped making pottery. || They kept making it.

## Practice 5 | Challenge
### Inferences
P: For years, the city measured the success of its bus service by counting tickets sold. When it switched to tracking how long passengers waited at stops, it discovered that although ticket sales were high, many passengers waited more than thirty minutes. This suggests that ticket sales alone ______
A) were an accurate measure of passenger satisfaction. || The waiting data reveal a problem that ticket sales hid.
*B) gave an incomplete picture of how well the service worked. || Correct.
C) were lower than the city believed. || Sales were high.
D) caused the long waiting times. || Not supported.

P: A species of moth in industrial areas of England was mostly light-coloured in the early 1800s. After factories covered tree trunks with soot, dark-coloured moths became far more common. When air pollution laws later cleaned the trees, light moths increased again. This pattern suggests that the moths' colour ______
*A) affected how easily birds and other predators could see them against the trees. || Correct: the better-camouflaged colour became more common each time.
B) was caused directly by breathing polluted air. || The change followed the colour of tree trunks, not air.
C) did not affect their survival. || The changes suggest it did.
D) changed within the life of each moth. || The text describes changes in the population.

P: In two neighbouring villages, families have similar incomes. Children in Village A, which has a public library, read on average twice as many books a year as children in Village B, which does not. However, researchers caution against concluding that the library alone explains the difference, because ______
A) children in both villages read books. || True but not a reason for caution.
B) libraries are expensive to build. || Not relevant to the cause.
*C) other differences between the villages, such as their schools, might also affect reading. || Correct: other factors could explain the difference.
D) families in the two villages have similar incomes. || This removes one alternative explanation rather than raising one.

P: The poet published her first collection under a man's name. Only after it won a national prize did she reveal her identity. Her choice suggests that she believed ______
A) men's names were easier to spell. || Not supported.
*B) her work might be judged unfairly if readers knew she was a woman. || Correct.
C) the prize was only open to men. || Not stated.
D) her poems were not good enough. || The prize suggests otherwise.

P: When a dam was built upstream, the river delivered far less sediment to the coast. Over the following decades, the beaches near the river mouth became narrower each year, even though sea levels rose only slightly. This suggests that the beaches had previously been ______

B) protected mainly by the dam. || The dam came later and the beaches shrank afterward.
C) unaffected by the river. || The shrinking after the dam suggests a link.
D) growing because of rising sea levels. || Not supported.

P: In a survey, 80 percent of residents said they wanted more cycle lanes. Yet when the city built new lanes, few residents used them. Interviews later revealed that most residents feared riding on busy roads, even in lanes. This suggests that, for many residents, ______

*C) feeling safe mattered more than simply having cycle lanes. || Correct.
D) cycling was more dangerous in lanes than on pavements. || Not supported.
