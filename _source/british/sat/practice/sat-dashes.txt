## Practice 1 | Foundation
### Boundaries (dashes)
P: The old lighthouse — built by Italian engineers in ______ still guides ships into the harbour.
A) 1848, || The extra information opens with a dash, so it must close with a dash, not a comma.
*B) 1848 — || Correct: a pair of dashes surrounds the extra information.
C) 1848 || The extra information is never closed.
D) 1848; || A semicolon cannot close extra information.

P: The festival's star ______ a twelve-year-old violinist from Assiut — received a standing ovation.
*A) performer — || Correct: the extra information closes with a dash, so it must open with a dash.
B) performer, || Mismatched marks (comma … dash).
C) performer || The extra information is never opened.
D) performer; || A semicolon cannot open extra information.

P: After months of training, Salma finally reached her ______ a place on the national swimming team.
A) goal; || "a place on the national swimming team" is not a complete sentence.
B) goal. || Fragment.
*C) goal — || Correct: a single dash introduces the goal for emphasis at the end of the sentence.
D) goal — and || "and a place…" leaves the phrase without a verb and makes no sense after the dash.

P: The scientists who discovered the new species of ______ spent three years searching the rainforest.
A) frog — || A single dash cannot separate the subject from its verb.
*B) frog || Correct: no punctuation between the subject ("The scientists who discovered…") and the verb "spent".
C) frog, || A single comma cannot separate the subject from its verb.
D) frog; || A semicolon cannot separate the subject from its verb.

P: Three cities — Alexandria, Rosetta, and ______ were once the main ports of Egypt's Mediterranean coast.
A) Damietta, || Mismatched marks: the list was opened with a dash.
B) Damietta || The extra information is never closed.
*C) Damietta — || Correct: dash … dash around the list.
D) Damietta; || A semicolon cannot close the extra information.

P: My grandmother's ______ a mix of lentils, rice, pasta, and fried onions — is the best I have ever tasted.
*A) koshari — || Correct: the dash opens the extra information that closes with a dash.
B) koshari, || Mismatched marks.
C) koshari: || A colon cannot open extra information inside a subject.
D) koshari || Not opened.

P: There was only one problem with the new ______ it was far too heavy to carry.
A) laptop, || Comma splice: "it was far too heavy to carry" is a complete sentence.
B) laptop || Run-on.
*C) laptop — || Correct: a dash can introduce an explanation that completes the first clause.
D) laptop — that || "that it was far too heavy" leaves a fragment after the dash.

P: The museum's newest ______ display a collection of ancient Nubian jewellery.
A) rooms — || A single dash cannot separate the subject from its verb.
B) rooms, || A single comma cannot separate the subject from its verb.
C) rooms; || A semicolon cannot separate the subject from its verb.
*D) rooms || Correct.

P: The documentary's director — a former ______ spent two years living with the fishing families of Lake Burullus.
A) journalist, || Mismatched marks.
*B) journalist — || Correct.
C) journalist || Not closed.
D) journalist; || A semicolon cannot close extra information.

P: The ancient library held thousands of ______ scrolls on medicine, mathematics, poetry, and law.
A) texts; || The list is not a complete sentence.
*B) texts — || Correct: a dash introduces the extra information at the end of the sentence.
C) texts. || Fragment.
D) texts — and || "and scrolls…" leaves the phrase without a verb.
