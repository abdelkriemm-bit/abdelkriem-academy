STEM: Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

## Practice 1 | Foundation
### Form, Structure, and Sense (finite and non-finite verbs)
P: The Great Library of Alexandria, one of the largest libraries of the ancient world, ______ hundreds of thousands of scrolls.
A) holding || "-ing" form alone is not a main verb; the sentence would have no main verb.
B) to hold || An infinitive is not a main verb.
*C) held || Correct: the subject "The Great Library" needs a finite (main) verb.
D) having held || Not a main verb.

P: ______ the race, the runner collapsed on the grass and smiled.
*A) Having finished || Correct: a participial phrase opens the sentence; the main verb is "collapsed".
B) She finished || Creates a comma splice: "She finished the race, the runner collapsed…".
C) Finishes || A finite verb with no subject.
D) Finished || "Finished the race" would mean the race finished her; it does not describe the runner's action.

P: The museum hopes ______ more young visitors with its new interactive rooms.
A) attracting || "hopes" is followed by an infinitive, not an "-ing" form.
*B) to attract || Correct.
C) attracts || Two finite verbs in a row ("hopes attracts").
D) attracted || Not a correct form after "hopes".

P: Scientists studying the desert ______ that some plants can survive for years without rain.
A) discovering || No main verb.
B) to discover || No main verb.
*C) have discovered || Correct: the subject "Scientists" needs a finite verb ("studying the desert" only describes them).
D) having discovered || No main verb.

P: The festival, ______ every spring in the old city, attracts musicians from across the region.
*A) held || Correct: a past participle introduces the extra information about the festival.
B) is held || A second finite verb inside the commas; it would need "which" ("which is held").
C) it is held || Creates a sentence inside a sentence.
D) holds || The festival does not hold itself; the passive meaning is needed.

P: The farmers began ______ the wheat early because of the hot weather.
A) harvested || A past participle cannot follow "began" here.
*B) harvesting || Correct: "began harvesting".
C) harvests || A finite verb cannot follow "began".
D) they harvested || "began they harvested" is ungrammatical.

P: The bridge ______ by Italian engineers in 1905, is still in use today.
A) designed || Without a comma before "designed", the phrase is not opened, so the single comma before "is" wrongly separates the subject from its verb.
B) was designed || A second finite verb; the sentence already has "is".
C) designs || The bridge did not design anything.
*D) , designed || Correct: a comma opens the participial phrase that closes with the comma before "is".

P: My goal for the summer is ______ two novels in English.
A) reads || A finite verb cannot follow "is" this way.
*B) to read || Correct: "is to read" names the goal.
C) read || "is read" would be passive and change the meaning.
D) having read || Does not fit after "is" to state a goal.

P: Walking along the Corniche, ______ the sun set over the Mediterranean.
A) the sea was golden as || The sea was not walking; the opening phrase must describe the person who walked.
*B) we watched || Correct: "we" did the walking; "watched" is the main verb.
C) watching || No main verb.
D) to watch || No main verb.

P: The documentary, which ______ two years to film, follows a family of camel herders.
A) taking || "which" needs a finite verb in its clause.
*B) took || Correct.
C) to take || Not a finite verb.
D) having taken || Not a finite verb.

## Practice 2 | Foundation
### Form, Structure, and Sense (finite and non-finite verbs)
P: The students ______ to the planetarium every year as part of their science course.
*A) go || Correct: the subject needs a finite verb.
B) going || No main verb.
C) to go || No main verb.
D) gone || No main verb.

P: ______ by the noise, the cat ran under the bed.
A) Frightening || "Frightening by the noise" is ungrammatical; the cat was frightened (passive).
*B) Frightened || Correct: past participle phrase describing the cat.
C) It was frightened || Comma splice.
D) Frightens || A finite verb with no subject.

P: Learning a new language ______ patience and daily practice.
A) requiring || No main verb.
*B) requires || Correct: the gerund subject "Learning a new language" needs a finite verb.
C) to require || No main verb.
D) required being || Ungrammatical.

P: The company plans ______ two new factories in the coming year.
A) opening || "plans" takes an infinitive.
B) opens || Two finite verbs in a row.
*C) to open || Correct.
D) opened || Wrong form.

P: The village, ______ on a hill above the river, is famous for its pottery.
*A) located || Correct.
B) is located || A second finite verb inside the commas.
C) it is located || A full sentence inside a sentence.
D) locating || The village is not doing the locating.

P: The teacher asked the students ______ their phones away during the exam.
A) putting || "asked" + person takes an infinitive.
*B) to put || Correct.
C) put || "asked the students put" is ungrammatical.
D) they put || Ungrammatical.

P: The old photographs, ______ in a box in the attic, show the city in the 1930s.
A) were found || A second finite verb inside the commas.
B) they were found || A sentence inside a sentence.
*C) found || Correct.
D) finding || The photographs did not find anything.

P: The researchers spent three months ______ the behaviour of the dolphins.
*A) observing || Correct: "spent time" + "-ing".
B) observed || Wrong form.
C) to observe || "spent three months to observe" is not standard.
D) they observed || Ungrammatical.

P: The bakery ______ fresh bread every morning at six o'clock.
A) baking || No main verb.
B) to bake || No main verb.
C) baked being || Ungrammatical.
*D) bakes || Correct.

P: Hoping ______ the train, Karim ran all the way to the station.
A) catching || "hoping" takes an infinitive.
*B) to catch || Correct.
C) catches || A finite verb cannot follow "Hoping" here.
D) caught || Wrong form.

## Practice 3 | Exam level
### Form, Structure, and Sense (finite and non-finite verbs)
P: Hatshepsut, one of the few women to rule ancient Egypt as pharaoh, ______ a famous temple at Deir el-Bahari.
A) building || No main verb.
*B) built || Correct: the subject "Hatshepsut" needs a finite verb.
C) having built || No main verb.
D) to build || No main verb.

P: The research team, after testing more than two hundred samples of river water, ______ that the pollution came from a single factory.
A) concluding || No main verb.
B) to conclude || No main verb.
*C) concluded || Correct.
D) having concluded || No main verb.

P: Discovered in 1799 near the town of Rashid, ______ helped scholars read Egyptian hieroglyphs.
*A) the Rosetta Stone || Correct: the opening participial phrase describes the stone, and the main verb is "helped".
B) scholars used the Rosetta Stone, which || The scholars were not discovered; also the structure becomes confusing.
C) the Rosetta Stone, which || "which helped…" leaves the sentence without a main verb.
D) helping || No subject and no main verb.

P: The novel's main character, a young doctor ______ in a village in Upper Egypt, struggles to win the trust of the local people.
A) works || A second finite verb; it would need "who works".
*B) working || Correct: a present participle phrase describes the doctor.
C) she works || A sentence inside a sentence.
D) to work || Changes the meaning (purpose) and is awkward here.

P: The coral reefs of the Red Sea, home to more than a thousand species of fish, ______ thousands of divers every year.
A) attracting || No main verb.
B) to attract || No main verb.
C) having attracted || No main verb.
*D) attract || Correct.

P: ______ the experiment several times, the students were confident in their results.
*A) Having repeated || Correct: the students repeated it before becoming confident.
B) They repeated || Comma splice.
C) Repeats || Finite verb with no subject.
D) Repeated || "Repeated the experiment several times" would describe the students as repeated.

P: The new law, which ______ last month, bans plastic bags in all supermarkets.
A) passing || "which" needs a finite verb.
*B) was passed || Correct.
C) to pass || Not finite.
D) having passed || Not finite.

P: The architect wanted the building ______ like a ship sailing on the desert.
A) looks || Finite verb after "wanted the building".
B) looking || "wanted the building looking" is not standard.
*C) to look || Correct.
D) it looks || Ungrammatical.

P: Founded in 970, Al-Azhar ______ one of the oldest universities in the world still operating today.
A) being || No main verb.
*B) is || Correct.
C) to be || No main verb.
D) having been || No main verb.

P: The children, ______ by the music, began to dance in the street.
A) were delighted || A second finite verb inside the commas.
*B) delighted || Correct.
C) delighting || The children were the ones delighted (passive), not delighting someone.
D) they were delighted || A sentence inside a sentence.

## Practice 4 | Exam level
### Form, Structure, and Sense (finite and non-finite verbs)
P: A small team of volunteers ______ more than ten thousand trees along the edge of the desert since the project began.
*A) has planted || Correct: the subject "team" needs a finite verb; "since" calls for the present perfect.
B) planting || No main verb.
C) to plant || No main verb.
D) having planted || No main verb.

P: The festival, ______ in 2008 by a group of local artists, now draws visitors from around the world.
A) it was started || A sentence inside a sentence.
B) was started || A second finite verb inside the commas.
*C) started || Correct.
D) starting || The festival did not start anyone; passive meaning is needed.

P: Unable to find the old map, ______ had to plan the route from memory.
A) the route || The route was not "unable to find the map".
*B) the explorers || Correct: the opening phrase describes the explorers, and "had to plan" is the main verb.
C) planning || No main verb and no subject.
D) it || Vague; the opening phrase cannot describe "it".

P: The scientists hope that the new vaccine ______ millions of lives.
A) saving || "that" clause needs a finite verb.
B) to save || Not finite.
*C) will save || Correct.
D) having saved || Not finite.

P: The old lighthouse, damaged by a storm in 2015 and ______ two years later, is now a small museum.
A) was rebuilt || A finite verb breaks the parallel participial phrase ("damaged … and rebuilt").
*B) rebuilt || Correct: parallel with "damaged".
C) rebuilding || Not parallel, and wrong meaning.
D) it was rebuilt || A sentence inside a sentence.

P: Reading the letter for the third time, ______ finally understood what her grandfather had meant.
*A) Salma || Correct: Salma was reading, and "understood" is the main verb.
B) the meaning was clear to Salma, who || The meaning was not reading the letter.
C) understanding || No main verb or subject.
D) to understand || No main verb.

P: The bees, which ______ pollen from more than fifty kinds of flowers, produce a dark, strong honey.
A) collecting || "which" needs a finite verb.
*B) collect || Correct.
C) to collect || Not finite.
D) having collected || Not finite.

P: Built of mud brick more than seven hundred years ago, ______ still provides shelter for travellers.
A) travellers find that the old inn || Travellers were not built of mud brick.
B) providing || No main verb.
*C) the old inn || Correct: the inn was built, and "provides" is the main verb.
D) the old inn, which || "which still provides…" turns the rest into a describing clause and leaves the sentence without a main verb.

P: The goal of the project is ______ clean water to every school in the district.
A) brings || Finite verb after "is".
*B) to bring || Correct.
C) brought || "is brought" changes the meaning (passive) and leaves "clean water" without a role.
D) they bring || Ungrammatical.

P: Having lived in Alexandria for most of her life, ______ the sea more than anything when she moved to Cairo.
A) missing || No main verb or subject.
*B) the writer missed || Correct: the writer had lived there; "missed" is the main verb.
C) the sea was missed by the writer || The sea did not live in Alexandria.
D) to miss || No main verb.
