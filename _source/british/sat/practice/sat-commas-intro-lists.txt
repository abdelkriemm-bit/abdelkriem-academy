STEM: Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

## Practice 1 | Foundation
### Boundaries (commas: introductions and lists)
P: After the long drought finally ______ farmers across the region planted their wheat again.
*A) ended, || Correct: a comma follows the introductory dependent clause "After the long drought finally ended".
B) ended || Without a comma, the long introductory clause runs into the main clause.
C) ended; || The opening clause is dependent, so a semicolon is wrong.
D) ended. || The opening clause would be a fragment.

P: The science fair included projects on robotics ______ recycling, and space travel.
A) robotics; || A semicolon cannot separate simple list items.
*B) robotics, || Correct: commas separate the items in a list.
C) robotics || "robotics recycling" runs two list items together.
D) robotics: || A colon cannot separate list items.

P: In the Middle ______ Cairo was one of the largest cities in the world.
A) Ages; || "In the Middle Ages" is a phrase, not a complete sentence.
B) Ages: || A colon must follow a complete sentence.
*C) Ages, || Correct: a comma after the introductory phrase.
D) Ages. || Fragment.

P: ______ dates, and figs were among the sweet foods eaten in ancient Egypt.
*A) Honey, || Correct: a comma separates the first list item from the second.
B) Honey; || A semicolon cannot separate simple list items.
C) Honey: || A colon cannot separate list items.
D) Honey — || A dash cannot separate list items.

P: ______ the smallest planet in the solar system is not the coldest.
*A) Surprisingly, || Correct: a comma follows the introductory word that comments on the whole sentence.
B) Surprisingly; || A semicolon cannot follow a single introductory word.
C) Surprisingly: || A colon cannot follow a single introductory word.
D) Surprisingly — || A single dash is not used after an introductory word.

P: To prepare for the ______ Nour solved one full practice test every Friday.
A) exam; || The infinitive phrase is not a complete sentence.
B) exam. || Fragment.
*C) exam, || Correct: a comma after the introductory infinitive phrase.
D) exam: || A colon must follow a complete sentence.

P: The recipe needs ______ sugar, eggs, and butter.
A) flour; || A semicolon cannot separate simple list items.
B) flour: || A colon cannot come between items of a list.
*C) flour, || Correct: commas separate the list items.
D) flour || "flour sugar" runs two items together.

P: Walking slowly through the old ______ the tourists stopped to watch a man making copper trays.
A) market; || The participial phrase is not a complete sentence.
*B) market, || Correct: a comma after the introductory participial phrase.
C) market. || Fragment.
D) market: || A colon must follow a complete sentence.

P: The library opens at nine on ______ but it closes early on Fridays.
A) weekdays || Run-on: two complete sentences joined by "but" need a comma before "but".
B) weekdays; but || A semicolon plus "but" doubles the joiner.
*C) weekdays, || Correct: comma + "but" joins two complete sentences.
D) weekdays: || A colon cannot be followed by "but" like this.

P: The bus stops at the ______ the hospital, and the train station.
*A) library, || Correct: commas separate the list items.
B) library || "the library the hospital" runs two items together.
C) library; || Semicolon between simple list items.
D) library: || Colon between list items.
