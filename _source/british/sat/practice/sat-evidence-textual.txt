STEM: Which finding, if true, would most directly support the hypothesis?

## Practice 1 | Foundation
### Command of Evidence — Textual
P: Researchers hypothesise that students who eat breakfast pay more attention in morning lessons than students who skip breakfast.
*A) In a study, students who ate breakfast answered more questions correctly in a 9 a.m. attention test than students who did not. || Correct: it directly links breakfast to better morning attention.
B) Most students prefer cereal to eggs for breakfast. || About preferences, not attention.
C) Students who skip breakfast often eat a large lunch. || About lunch, not attention.
D) Afternoon lessons are usually shorter than morning lessons. || Unrelated.

P: A biologist hypothesises that a certain species of desert beetle collects drinking water from morning fog.
A) The beetle is black and about two centimetres long. || Describes the beetle, not water collection.
B) The beetle is most active at night. || Unrelated to fog.
*C) Water droplets form on the beetle's back during foggy mornings and roll down toward its mouth. || Correct.
D) Fog is common in the desert where the beetle lives. || Fog being present does not show the beetle uses it.

P: Researchers hypothesise that city parks reduce air temperatures in the streets around them.
A) Many people visit parks on weekends. || About visitors, not temperature.
B) Parks are more common in older neighbourhoods. || Unrelated.
C) Some parks have playgrounds and cafés. || Unrelated.
*D) Streets next to large parks were 2°C cooler on summer afternoons than similar streets far from parks. || Correct.

P: A team of historians hypothesises that the ancient town was an important trading centre.
*A) Excavations uncovered coins and pottery from several distant regions. || Correct: goods from far away point to trade.
B) The town was built near a small hill. || Unrelated to trade.
C) Most houses in the town had two rooms. || Unrelated.
D) The town was abandoned around 500 CE. || About its end, not its trade.

P: Scientists hypothesise that dolphins can recognise themselves in a mirror.
A) Dolphins live in groups called pods. || Unrelated.
*B) When marked with a spot of paint, dolphins swam to a mirror and turned to look at the marked part of their bodies. || Correct.
C) Dolphins make clicking sounds to find food. || Unrelated.
D) Dolphins can swim faster than most fish. || Unrelated.

P: Researchers hypothesise that playing music to tomato plants makes them grow faster.
A) Tomato plants need sunlight to grow. || Does not test music.
B) Tomatoes are one of the most popular vegetables in the world. || Unrelated.
*C) Tomato plants exposed to music for two hours a day grew taller than identical plants kept in silence. || Correct.
D) Many farmers play the radio while they work. || Does not show an effect on the plants.

P: An economist hypothesises that lowering bus fares will increase the number of people who use buses.
A) Bus drivers asked for higher wages. || Unrelated.
B) Most buses in the city are painted blue. || Unrelated.
C) Many residents own cars. || Does not test the fare change.
*D) After fares were cut by half in a nearby city, bus use rose by 40 percent. || Correct.

P: A linguist hypothesises that children learn new words faster when they hear them in stories than when they see them in lists.
*A) Children who heard new words in stories remembered more of them a week later than children who studied the same words in lists. || Correct.
B) Children enjoy stories more than lists. || Enjoyment is not the same as learning speed.
C) Lists are often used in language textbooks. || Unrelated.
D) Some children cannot read yet. || Unrelated.

P: Researchers hypothesise that wearing a helmet reduces head injuries among cyclists.
A) Helmets come in many colours. || Unrelated.
B) Cycling is popular among teenagers. || Unrelated.
C) Helmets are required by law in some countries. || Laws do not show the effect.
*D) Among cyclists in accidents, those wearing helmets were much less likely to suffer serious head injuries. || Correct.

P: An astronomer hypothesises that a distant star has a planet orbiting it.
A) The star is brighter than the Sun. || Not evidence of a planet.
*B) The star's light dims slightly at regular intervals, as if something were passing in front of it. || Correct.
C) The star is about 300 light-years from Earth. || Distance is not evidence of a planet.
D) The star was discovered in 1990. || Unrelated.

B) Wheat is grown in many countries. || Unrelated.

P: An education researcher hypothesises that smaller classes improve students' reading scores.
A) Smaller classes are more expensive to run. || Cost, not scores.

A) Crows eat a wide variety of foods. || Unrelated.

*C) Crows scolded and dived at a person wearing a mask that had been worn by someone who once trapped them, but ignored people in other masks. || Correct.

P: A doctor hypothesises that drinking more water reduces headaches in office workers.
*A) Workers who increased their daily water intake reported fewer headaches over three months than workers who did not. || Correct.
B) Most office workers drink coffee. || Unrelated.
C) Headaches are common in many jobs. || Does not test water.
D) Water is cheaper than juice. || Unrelated.

P: Archaeologists hypothesise that people at the site kept cattle.
A) The site is near a river. || Does not show cattle.
B) The site's houses were made of stone. || Unrelated.
C) Paintings at a site 500 kilometres away show cattle. || A different site.
*D) Many cattle bones with cut marks were found in the site's rubbish pits. || Correct.

P: Engineers hypothesise that a new road surface reduces traffic noise.
A) The new surface is darker than the old one. || Colour is not noise.
*B) Noise levels measured beside roads with the new surface were lower than beside roads with the old surface, under the same traffic. || Correct.
C) The new surface takes two days to install. || Unrelated.
D) Many drivers complain about traffic. || Unrelated.

P: A psychologist hypothesises that people make more careful decisions when they are not hungry.
A) Most people eat three meals a day. || Unrelated.
B) Hungry people are more likely to buy snacks. || About snacks, not carefulness generally.
*C) Participants who had just eaten took longer and made fewer errors on a decision-making test than participants who had not eaten for six hours. || Correct.
D) Decision-making tests are often used in research. || Unrelated.

P: Researchers hypothesise that a certain type of coral recovers well after high water temperatures.
A) The coral is found in the Red Sea. || Location does not show recovery.
B) The coral is bright orange. || Unrelated.
C) The coral grows slowly. || Unrelated to recovery after heat.
*D) Six months after a heatwave turned the coral white, most colonies had regained their colour and were growing again. || Correct.

## Practice 3 | Exam level
### Command of Evidence — Textual
P: In her novel The Salt Road, a writer portrays the narrator's grandmother as someone who hides her feelings behind practical tasks.
Q: Which quotation from the novel most effectively illustrates the claim?
A) "My grandmother was born in a village near the sea and never learned to read." || Background, not hiding feelings.
*B) "When the news came, she said nothing; she only began to scrub the clean floor again, harder than before." || Correct: she reacts to news by working instead of showing emotion.
C) "Her kitchen smelled of cumin and bread." || Description of setting.
D) "She liked to sing old songs while she cooked." || Shows her expressing herself, not hiding feelings.

P: An essayist claims that the city's old market is valued by residents more for its social life than for its shopping.
Q: Which quotation from the essay most effectively illustrates the claim?
A) "Prices in the market are often lower than in the new shopping centres." || About shopping, the opposite focus.
B) "The market opens at seven every morning." || A detail.
*C) "Many people come without buying anything at all; they come to drink tea with the stallholders and hear the news." || Correct.
D) "The market sells fruit, spices, and fabric." || About goods.

P: Researchers hypothesise that the spread of a plant disease is slowed when different crops are planted together rather than in large fields of a single crop.
*A) In mixed fields, the disease reached 15 percent of plants after a month; in single-crop fields, it reached 60 percent. || Correct.
B) The disease is caused by a fungus carried by the wind. || Describes the cause, not the effect of mixing.
C) Single-crop fields are easier to harvest by machine. || Unrelated to disease spread.
D) The disease was first reported in 2015. || Unrelated.

P: In a poem, the speaker describes a city at night as both beautiful and lonely.
Q: Which quotation from the poem most effectively illustrates the claim?
A) "The buses stop at midnight, one by one." || Neither beauty nor loneliness clearly.
B) "I walk home quickly through the cold." || Loneliness is not clear; beauty absent.
C) "The bakery opens before the dawn." || Neither.
*D) "A thousand windows shine like gold, / and no one at them knows my name." || Correct: beauty (shining windows) and loneliness (no one knows the speaker).

P: Researchers hypothesise that children who learn a musical instrument develop better memory for sequences of sounds.
A) Children who play instruments often enjoy concerts. || Unrelated to memory.
B) Many schools offer music lessons. || Unrelated.
*C) Children with two years of instrument lessons repeated longer sequences of tones correctly than children with no lessons. || Correct.
D) Musical instruments are expensive. || Unrelated.

P: A critic argues that the playwright uses humour to make serious points about social inequality.
Q: Which quotation from the play most effectively illustrates the critic's claim?
A) "Act 1 takes place in a small apartment in Shubra." || Setting.
*B) "The servant, laughing, tells his master: 'You own the house, sir, but I'm the only one who knows where the door is.'" || Correct: a joke that points to the master's dependence on his servant.
C) "The play lasts about two hours." || A fact about length.
D) "The master and his wife argue about dinner." || Not clearly about inequality or humour.

P: An ecologist hypothesises that wolves returning to a national park have helped riverside trees recover by changing where deer feed.
A) Wolves were absent from the park for seventy years. || Background.
B) Deer populations vary from year to year. || Does not link wolves to trees.
C) Riverside trees provide shade for fish. || About fish, not the hypothesis.
*D) After wolves returned, deer spent less time near rivers, and young trees there grew much taller than before. || Correct.

P: In her memoir, an engineer describes her childhood curiosity about how machines work.
Q: Which quotation from the memoir most effectively illustrates the claim?
*A) "By the age of nine, I had taken apart my father's radio twice, and put it back together once." || Correct.
B) "My father worked at the post office for thirty years." || About her father.
C) "Our house was near the railway station." || Setting.
D) "I studied engineering at Cairo University." || About her education, not childhood curiosity.

P: A team hypothesises that a newly found fossil belongs to an animal that could fly.
A) The fossil was found in a layer of rock about 150 million years old. || Age, not flight.
B) The fossil is about the size of a crow. || Size alone does not show flight.
*C) The fossil shows long feathers arranged like those on the wings of modern flying birds. || Correct.
D) The fossil was found by a farmer. || Unrelated.

P: An author claims that a historical novel captures the uncertainty people felt during the 1919 revolution.
Q: Which quotation from the novel most effectively illustrates the claim?
A) "The revolution began in March 1919." || A fact, not uncertainty.
B) "Students marched through the streets of Cairo." || An event.
*C) "No one in the café knew whether tomorrow would bring freedom or prison, and so they spoke in whispers." || Correct.
D) "Her brother had studied law." || Unrelated.

## Practice 5 | Challenge
### Command of Evidence — Textual
P: Researchers hypothesise that a medicine reduces headaches by improving sleep, rather than by acting directly on pain.
Q: Which finding, if true, would most directly support the researchers' hypothesis?
A) Patients who took the medicine reported fewer headaches. || This shows the medicine works but not how.
*B) The medicine reduced headaches only in patients whose sleep improved; patients whose sleep did not improve saw no change. || Correct: it links the effect to sleep.
C) The medicine is cheaper than other headache medicines. || Unrelated.
D) Most patients took the medicine at night. || Timing alone does not show the mechanism.

P: A historian claims that a nineteenth-century traveller's account of Egypt exaggerated the dangers of the journey to make his book more exciting.
Q: Which finding, if true, would most directly support the historian's claim?
A) The traveller's book sold thousands of copies. || Sales do not show exaggeration.
B) The traveller visited Egypt twice. || Unrelated.
*C) Letters the traveller wrote during the trip describe the same journey as calm and comfortable. || Correct: private letters contradict the book's dangers.
D) Other travellers also wrote books about Egypt. || Unrelated.

P: Researchers hypothesise that the decline of a fish species in a lake is caused mainly by a newly introduced predator, not by pollution.
Q: Which finding, if true, would most directly weaken the researchers' hypothesis?
A) The predator was introduced to the lake in 2005. || Background, neither supports nor weakens.
*B) The fish species declined just as quickly in a nearby polluted lake where the predator has never lived. || Correct: decline without the predator points to pollution.
C) The predator eats several kinds of fish. || Consistent with the hypothesis.
D) Pollution levels in the lake have stayed the same since 2000. || This would support, not weaken, the hypothesis.

P: In a novel, the narrator gradually comes to admire the strict teacher she disliked at first.
Q: Which quotation from the novel most effectively illustrates the change described?
A) "Miss Samia never smiled and never allowed us to speak without raising our hands." || Shows strictness, not a change.
B) "I hated her lessons and counted the minutes until the bell." || Shows only the early dislike.
*C) "Years later, when I stood in front of my own class, I found myself using her exact words—and I was proud of it." || Correct: it shows later admiration.
D) "The school was a grey building at the end of our street." || Setting.

P: A scientist argues that a certain bird learns its song from older birds rather than being born knowing it.

C) The species' song is louder than that of most birds. || Unrelated.
D) Female birds of this species do not sing. || Unrelated.
