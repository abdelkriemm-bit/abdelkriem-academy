STEM: Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

## Practice 1 | Foundation
### Boundaries (sentence boundaries)
P: The first public library in Egypt opened in Cairo in 1870 ______ it began with a collection of manuscripts gathered from mosques and schools.
A) 1870, || Comma splice: two complete sentences joined by a comma alone.
*B) 1870. || Correct: a period ends the first complete sentence; "It began…" starts the next one.
C) 1870 || Run-on: two sentences fused with no punctuation.
D) 1870, and, || The comma after "and" is wrong.

P: The giant panda eats bamboo for up to fourteen hours a day ______ because bamboo contains very little energy.
A) day. || "Because bamboo contains very little energy" would be a fragment on its own.
B) day; || A semicolon needs a complete sentence after it, but "because…" is dependent.
*C) day || Correct: a "because" clause at the end of a sentence usually takes no punctuation before it.
D) day: || A colon cannot introduce a dependent "because" clause here.

P: Cyclists in Amsterdam outnumber cars ______ the city has built more than 700 kilometres of bicycle paths.
*A) cars, and || Correct: comma + "and" joins two complete sentences.
B) cars and, || The comma belongs before "and", not after it.
C) cars, || Comma splice.
D) cars || Run-on.

P: When the sun sets over the Western Desert ______ the temperature can drop by more than twenty degrees in a few hours.
A) Desert. || The "When…" clause would become a fragment.
*B) Desert, || Correct: an introductory dependent clause is followed by a comma.
C) Desert; || A semicolon needs complete sentences on both sides.
D) Desert, and || "and" cannot join a dependent clause to a main clause.

P: Leonardo da Vinci filled thousands of notebook pages with drawings ______ many of them show inventions that were never built.
A) drawings, || Comma splice.
B) drawings || Run-on.
*C) drawings, and || Correct: comma + coordinating conjunction joins two independent clauses.
D) drawings and, || The comma is in the wrong place.

P: The new railway will connect Cairo and the Red Sea coast. Travellers ______ will reach Ain Sokhna in about one hour.
*A) on the high-speed trains || Correct: no punctuation is needed between the subject and its verb "will reach".
B) on the high-speed trains, || A single comma must never separate a subject from its verb.
C) on the high-speed trains; || A semicolon cannot split a subject from its verb.
D) on the high-speed trains: || A colon cannot split a subject from its verb.

P: Some scientists study the oldest trees on Earth ______ by counting their growth rings, they can learn about the climate of past centuries.
A) Earth, || Comma splice: "by counting… they can learn…" is a complete sentence.
*B) Earth. || Correct: the period ends the first sentence.
C) Earth || Run-on.
D) Earth, by || This repeats "by" and creates "by by counting", and the comma splice remains.

P: The writer Naguib Mahfouz won the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1988 ______ he was the first Arabic-language writer to receive it.
A) 1988, || Comma splice.
B) 1988 || Run-on.
C) 1988 and, || The comma after "and" is wrong and the comma before it is missing.
*D) 1988; || Correct: a semicolon joins two closely related complete sentences.

P: The students measured the temperature of the water every ten minutes ______ recording each result in a table.
A) minutes. || "Recording each result in a table" would be a fragment.
*B) minutes, || Correct: a comma introduces the participial phrase that describes how they measured.
C) minutes; || What follows is not a complete sentence.
D) minutes, they were || This creates a comma splice ("they were recording…" is a complete sentence).

P: Coffee was first grown in Yemen ______ from there, it spread to Egypt, Turkey, and eventually Europe.
A) Yemen, || Comma splice.
B) Yemen || Run-on.
*C) Yemen; || Correct: both sides are independent clauses.
D) Yemen; and, || Doubled joiner and an extra comma.

## Practice 2 | Foundation
### Boundaries (sentence boundaries)
P: Sea turtles return to the beach where they hatched ______ even after travelling thousands of kilometres across the ocean.
A) hatched. || "Even after travelling…" would be a fragment.
B) hatched; || A semicolon needs a full sentence after it.
*C) hatched, || Correct: a comma sets off the final phrase that adds information.
D) hatched: and || A colon followed by "and" is never correct.

P: The ancient Egyptians made paper from the papyrus plant ______ they cut its stems into thin strips and pressed them together.
A) plant, || Comma splice.
*B) plant. || Correct: two separate complete sentences.
C) plant || Run-on.
D) plant, which || "which they cut its stems" is ungrammatical (two objects).

P: Although the storm had passed ______ the roads remained closed until the next morning.
*A) passed, || Correct: introductory dependent clause + comma.
B) passed. || The "Although…" clause would be a fragment.
C) passed; || A semicolon needs complete sentences on both sides.
D) passed, and || "and" cannot follow a dependent clause like this.

P: The director of the museum ______ that the exhibition would travel to five countries.
*A) announced || Correct: the subject "The director of the museum" is followed directly by its verb.
B) , announced || A single comma cannot separate a subject from its verb.
C) announced: || A colon cannot separate a verb from its object clause.
D) announcing || Without the finite verb "announced", the sentence has no main verb.

P: Many plants close their leaves at night ______ scientists still debate exactly why they do this.
A) night, || Comma splice.
B) night || Run-on.
*C) night, but || Correct: comma + "but" joins two complete sentences.
D) night but, || The comma is in the wrong place.

P: The festival takes place every October ______ it attracts musicians from across North Africa.
A) October, || Comma splice.
*B) October. || Correct: two sentences separated by a period.
C) October || Run-on.
D) October, it, || A comma splice with an extra comma after "it".

P: In 2019, a team of divers found a shipwreck off the coast of Alexandria ______ the ship had been carrying jars of olive oil.
A) Alexandria, || Comma splice.
B) Alexandria || Run-on.
C) Alexandria and, || The comma is in the wrong place.
*D) Alexandria; || Correct: two independent clauses joined by a semicolon.

P: The recipe calls for three main ingredients ______ rice, lentils, and fried onions.
A) ingredients; || What follows is a list, not a complete sentence.
*B) ingredients: || Correct: a colon after a complete sentence introduces a list.
C) ingredients, which || "which rice, lentils…" has no verb and is ungrammatical.
D) ingredients. || The list would become a fragment.

P: The engineers tested the bridge with heavy trucks ______ the bridge did not bend at all.
*A) trucks, and || Correct: comma + "and" joins two complete sentences.
B) trucks, || Comma splice.
C) trucks and, || The comma is in the wrong place.
D) trucks || Run-on.

P: Students who read for twenty minutes every day ______ tend to build a much larger vocabulary.
A) day, || A comma would separate the subject ("Students who read… every day") from its verb "tend".
B) day; || A semicolon cannot separate a subject from its verb.
C) day. || The first part would be a fragment with no main verb.
*D) day || Correct: no punctuation between the long subject and its verb.

## Practice 3 | Exam level
### Boundaries (sentence boundaries)
P: The geologist Yara Mansour has spent a decade mapping the caves beneath the Western Desert ______ her maps reveal tunnels that run for more than eight kilometres.
A) Desert, || Comma splice.
B) Desert || Run-on.
*C) Desert; || Correct: the second clause is complete and explains the first.
D) Desert, which || "which her maps reveal…" is ungrammatical.

P: Spending most of his life in the small village of Deir el-Medina ______ the craftsman decorated the tombs of several pharaohs.
A) Deir el-Medina; || The opening is a participial phrase, not a complete sentence, so a semicolon is wrong.
B) Deir el-Medina. || The opening phrase would become a fragment.
*C) Deir el-Medina, || Correct: an introductory participial phrase is followed by a comma.
D) Deir el-Medina || Without a comma, the long opening phrase runs into the main clause.

P: The author's second novel was rejected by eleven publishers ______ it later sold more than a million copies.
A) publishers, || Comma splice.
*B) publishers; || Correct: two complete sentences joined by a semicolon.
C) publishers || Run-on.
D) publishers, however, || "however" is not a conjunction, so commas alone create a comma splice.

P: The researchers' main finding ______ that bees can recognise human faces.
A) was: || A colon cannot come between the verb "was" and its complement.
*B) was || Correct: subject + verb + complement with no punctuation.
C) , was || A single comma cannot separate the subject from its verb.
D) being || Without a finite verb, the sentence has no main verb.

P: Desert foxes have unusually large ears ______ the ears release heat and help the animals stay cool.
A) ears, || Comma splice.
B) ears || Run-on.
C) ears; and, || Doubled joiner with an extra comma.
*D) ears. || Correct: two complete sentences.

P: The city of Fez in Morocco is home to the University of al-Qarawiyyin ______ which many historians consider the oldest university still operating.
*A) al-Qarawiyyin, || Correct: a comma introduces the non-essential "which" clause.
B) al-Qarawiyyin; || What follows is dependent, so a semicolon cannot be used.
C) al-Qarawiyyin. || "Which many historians consider…" would be a fragment.
D) al-Qarawiyyin: || A colon must introduce a list, explanation or extra information after a complete sentence, not a "which" clause.

P: In the early twentieth century, travel between Cairo and Luxor took several days by boat ______ today, the same journey takes about an hour by plane.
A) boat, || Comma splice.
B) boat || Run-on.
*C) boat; || Correct: two independent clauses.
D) boat, today || Comma splice.

P: The goal of the program ______ to give every student in the district access to a laptop.
A) is: || A colon cannot come between a verb and its complement.
*B) is || Correct: no punctuation is needed between "is" and the infinitive phrase.
C) is, || A comma should not separate a verb from its complement.
D) is; || A semicolon would leave "to give every student…" as a fragment.

P: Most of the paintings in the exhibition are small ______ the largest is only about the size of a school notebook.
A) small, || Comma splice.
B) small || Run-on.
C) small, and, || The comma after "and" is wrong.
*D) small; || Correct: a semicolon joins the two related clauses.

P: By the time the rescue team reached the mountain village ______ most of the snow had already melted.
A) village; || The "By the time…" clause is dependent, so a semicolon is wrong.
*B) village, || Correct: introductory dependent clause + comma.
C) village. || Fragment.
D) village || Without a comma, the long opening clause runs into the main clause.

## Practice 4 | Exam level
### Boundaries (sentence boundaries)
P: Few visitors realise that the Great Pyramid was once covered in polished white limestone ______ most of these casing stones were removed centuries ago to build other structures.
A) limestone, || Comma splice.
*B) limestone; || Correct: two complete sentences.
C) limestone || Run-on.
D) limestone, which || "which most of these casing stones were removed" is ungrammatical.

P: Each spring, millions of birds fly over Egypt on their way north ______ storks, pelicans, and eagles are among the most common.
*A) north; || Correct: the second part is a complete sentence ("storks, pelicans, and eagles are…").
B) north, || Comma splice.
C) north || Run-on.
D) north: and || A colon followed by "and" is never correct.

P: The team's analysis of the satellite images ______ several ancient roads hidden beneath the sand.
A) revealing || Without a finite verb ("revealed"), the sentence has no main verb.
B) , which revealed || A comma would separate the subject from the rest and leave no main verb.
*C) revealed || Correct: the subject "analysis" needs the finite verb "revealed" with no punctuation before it.
D) ; it revealed || A semicolon would leave "The team's analysis of the satellite images" as a fragment.

P: Fatima al-Fihri founded the university in 859 ______ using money she had inherited from her father.
A) 859. || "Using money…" would be a fragment.
B) 859; || A semicolon needs a complete sentence after it.
*C) 859, || Correct: a comma introduces the participial phrase.
D) 859: || A colon needs an explanation, list or extra information, and "using money…" is a descriptive phrase that a comma introduces.

P: The orchestra rehearsed for months before the premiere ______ the performance received a standing ovation.
A) premiere, || Comma splice.
B) premiere || Run-on.
*C) premiere, and || Correct: comma + "and".
D) premiere and, || Misplaced comma.

P: A recent survey of teenagers in five countries ______ that most of them check their phones within ten minutes of waking up.
*A) found || Correct: the subject "survey" needs its verb with no punctuation between them.
B) found: || A colon cannot separate a verb from its object clause.
C) , found || A comma cannot separate a subject from its verb.
D) finding || There would be no main verb.

P: Owls can turn their heads about 270 degrees ______ this flexibility comes from extra bones in their necks.
A) degrees, || Comma splice.
*B) degrees. || Correct: two sentences.
C) degrees || Run-on.
D) degrees, and, || Extra comma after "and".

P: The architect's plan included two features that were rare at the time ______ large windows facing north and a garden on the roof.
A) time; || What follows is a list, not a complete sentence.
B) time, || A comma is too weak to introduce this list; a colon is the convention after a complete sentence.
*C) time: || Correct: a colon after a complete sentence introduces the list.
D) time || Without punctuation, the list runs into the sentence.

P: Because the river changed course several times over the centuries ______ some ancient towns that were once on its banks now lie far from the water.
*A) centuries, || Correct: introductory dependent clause + comma.
B) centuries; || The first part is dependent, so a semicolon is wrong.
C) centuries. || Fragment.
D) centuries: || A colon must follow a complete sentence.

P: The film was shot entirely on a phone camera ______ yet critics praised its rich colours and sharp images.
A) camera; || A semicolon before "yet" is unusual here; "yet" is a coordinating conjunction and takes a comma.
B) camera || Run-on: a comma is needed before "yet" joining two independent clauses.
C) camera: || A colon cannot be followed by the conjunction "yet" here.
*D) camera, || Correct: comma + coordinating conjunction "yet" joins two complete sentences.

## Practice 5 | Challenge
### Boundaries (sentence boundaries)
P: The economist argues that cities grow fastest when they invest in public transport ______ her critics, however, point out that several fast-growing cities have almost no buses or trains.
A) transport, || Comma splice: "however" is not a conjunction.
*B) transport; || Correct: the second clause is complete, with "however" inside it.
C) transport || Run-on.
D) transport, however; || Moves "however" into the wrong clause and splits the second sentence.

P: What the historians found most surprising about the letters ______ how often the merchant wrote about his garden.
A) , was || A single comma cannot separate the long subject from its verb.
B) was: || A colon cannot come between "was" and its complement.
*C) was || Correct: the whole clause "What the historians found most surprising about the letters" is the subject, so nothing comes before or after "was".
D) was, || A comma cannot separate the verb from its complement.

P: The sculptor worked with three unusual materials ______ melted glass from old bottles, wire taken from broken fences, and sand from the beach near her studio.
A) materials; || A semicolon needs a complete sentence after it; this is a list.
B) materials, which were: || A colon cannot follow "which were".
*C) materials: || Correct: colon after a complete sentence to introduce the list.
D) materials || Without punctuation, the list runs on confusingly.

P: Although the experiment did not prove the hypothesis ______ it gave the researchers several new ideas for future studies.
A) hypothesis; || The "Although…" clause is dependent.
B) hypothesis. || Fragment.
C) hypothesis, but || "Although" and "but" together double the contrast and are ungrammatical.
*D) hypothesis, || Correct: introductory dependent clause + comma.

P: Tea plantations cover the hills of Sri Lanka's central highlands ______ the cool, wet climate there is ideal for the plants.
*A) highlands; || Correct: two complete sentences.
B) highlands, || Comma splice.

P: The documentary follows a family of fishermen on Lake Nasser ______ over a single year, it shows how the rising and falling water shapes their lives.
A) Nasser, || Comma splice.
B) Nasser || Run-on.
*C) Nasser. || Correct: two separate sentences.

P: Born in Baghdad in 965 ______ the scientist Ibn al-Haytham later wrote a famous book about light and vision.
A) 965; || The opening phrase is not a complete sentence.
*B) 965, || Correct: introductory phrase + comma.

D) 965 || The opening phrase needs a comma to separate it from the main clause.

P: The report did not blame any single company for the pollution ______ instead, it called on every factory along the river to reduce its waste.
A) pollution, || Comma splice: "instead" is not a conjunction.
B) pollution || Run-on.
C) pollution, instead; || Places the semicolon inside the second clause.
*D) pollution; || Correct: semicolon + "instead," starts the second clause.

P: The students' presentation on renewable energy ______ how a small solar farm could power their entire school.
*A) explained || Correct: the subject "presentation" is followed directly by its verb.
B) , explained || A single comma cannot separate a subject from its verb.
C) explaining || Without a finite verb, the sentence has no main verb.
D) explained: || A colon cannot separate a verb from its object.

P: Some of the oldest known board games come from Mesopotamia ______ the Royal Game of Ur, for instance, is more than 4,000 years old.
