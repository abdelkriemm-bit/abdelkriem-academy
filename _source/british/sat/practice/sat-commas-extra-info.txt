## Practice 1 | Foundation
### Boundaries (extra information)
P: The Nile, the longest river in Africa ______ flows north through eleven countries before reaching the Mediterranean Sea.
*A) Africa, || Correct: the extra information "the longest river in Africa" opens with a comma, so it must close with a comma.
B) Africa || The extra information must be closed; without a comma, it runs into the verb.
C) Africa; || A semicolon cannot close extra information.
D) Africa — || A dash cannot close information that was opened with a comma; the marks must match.

P: My cousin ______ a nurse at a hospital in Tanta, volunteers at a children's library every Friday.
A) Hoda || The extra information "a nurse at a hospital in Tanta" is closed by a comma, so it must also be opened by one.
*B) Hoda, || Correct: a pair of commas surrounds the extra information.
C) Hoda; || A semicolon cannot open extra information.
D) Hoda: || A colon cannot be used in the middle of a subject like this.

P: The bridge ______ which opened in 1932, is still used by thousands of cars every day.
A) bridge || A non-essential "which" clause must be set off by commas on both sides.
*B) bridge, || Correct: the comma opens the "which" clause, matching the comma that closes it.
C) bridge; || A semicolon cannot open a "which" clause.
D) bridge — || The clause is closed with a comma, so it must be opened with a comma, not a dash.

P: Students ______ who arrive late must sign in at the main office.
*A) Students || Correct: "who arrive late" tells us WHICH students; it is essential information, so no commas are used.
B) Students, || A comma would make the essential "who" clause look like extra information and would separate the subject from its verb.
C) Students; || A semicolon cannot come between a subject and its describing clause.
D) Students: || A colon cannot be used here.

P: Ancient Egyptian scribes wrote on papyrus ______ a paper-like material made from reeds.
A) papyrus; || What follows is not a complete sentence.
B) papyrus. || "A paper-like material made from reeds" would be a fragment.
*C) papyrus, || Correct: a comma introduces the extra information at the end of the sentence.
D) papyrus || Without punctuation, the description runs into "papyrus" confusingly.

P: The novel's main character ______ a young engineer named Karim, returns to his village after twenty years abroad.
A) character || Extra information closed by a comma must be opened by a comma.
B) character; || A semicolon cannot open extra information.
C) character — || A dash opening must match a dash closing, but the information closes with a comma.
*D) character, || Correct: a pair of commas surrounds the extra information.

P: The pyramids at Giza ______ were built about 4,500 years ago.
*A) Giza || Correct: there is no extra information here; the subject goes straight to the verb.
B) Giza, || A single comma cannot separate the subject from its verb.
C) Giza; || A semicolon cannot separate a subject from its verb.
D) Giza — || A single dash cannot separate a subject from its verb.

P: Dr. Ahmed Zewail ______ won the Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 1999.
A) , an Egyptian-American scientist || The extra information is opened but never closed.
*B) , an Egyptian-American scientist, || Correct: the extra information is set off by a matching pair of commas.
C) an Egyptian-American scientist, || The extra information is closed but never opened.
D) , an Egyptian-American scientist — || The marks do not match (comma … dash).

P: The city's oldest café (which opened in 1898 ______ still serves tea in the original glasses.
A) 1898, || Brackets must be closed with a bracket, not a comma.
B) 1898; || A semicolon cannot close a bracket.
*C) 1898) || Correct: an opening bracket needs a closing bracket.
D) 1898 — || Brackets must be closed with a bracket, not a dash.

P: The fennec fox ______ the smallest fox in the world, lives in the Sahara Desert.
A) fox — || The information is closed with a comma, so it must be opened with a comma.
*B) fox, || Correct: matching pair of commas.
C) fox || The extra information must be opened.
D) fox; || A semicolon cannot open extra information.
