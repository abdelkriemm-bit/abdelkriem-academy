STEM: Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

## Practice 1 | Foundation
### Boundaries (semicolons)
P: The ancient city of Petra was carved directly into rose-coloured sandstone cliffs ______ its builders, the Nabataeans, controlled important trade routes across the desert.
A) cliffs, || Comma splice: two complete sentences cannot be joined by a comma alone.
*B) cliffs; || Correct: "The ancient city … cliffs" and "its builders … controlled … routes" are both complete sentences, so a semicolon joins them.
C) cliffs || Run-on: two complete sentences are fused with no punctuation at all.
D) cliffs, and, || "and" works only with a single comma before it; the comma after "and" breaks the sentence.

P: Honeybees communicate the location of flowers through a movement called the waggle dance ______ the angle of the dance shows the direction of the food.
*A) dance; || Correct: both sides are independent clauses, so a semicolon is the right joiner.
B) dance, || Comma splice: a comma alone cannot join two complete sentences.
C) dance || Run-on: nothing separates the two complete sentences.
D) dance; and || A semicolon already joins the clauses; adding "and" doubles the joiner.

P: Chef Amira Saleh opened her first restaurant in Alexandria in 2015 ______ by 2021 she was running three kitchens along the coast.
A) 2015, || Comma splice: "by 2021 she was running…" is a full sentence, so a comma is not enough.
B) 2015 || Run-on: two complete sentences with no punctuation.
*C) 2015; || Correct: the semicolon links two closely related complete sentences.
D) 2015: and || A colon followed by "and" is never correct; the colon must follow a complete sentence and introduce something, not a conjunction.

P: The documentary was filmed over three years in the Sinai Peninsula ______ which allowed the crew to record the full cycle of the seasons.
A) Peninsula; || "which allowed the crew…" is not a complete sentence, so it cannot stand after a semicolon.
*B) Peninsula, || Correct: "which…" is a dependent clause adding extra information, so a comma introduces it.
C) Peninsula. || The second part would become a fragment starting with "Which".
D) Peninsula; this || "this allowed" would make a complete sentence, but the choice leaves "which" in the text, giving "this which", which is ungrammatical.

P: Many deep-sea fish produce their own light ______ scientists call this ability bioluminescence.
A) light, || Comma splice: "scientists call this ability bioluminescence" is a complete sentence.
B) light || Run-on: no punctuation between two independent clauses.
C) light; and || Doubled joiner: a semicolon does not take "and" after it in this sentence.
*D) light; || Correct: two complete, related sentences joined by a semicolon.

P: The museum's new wing will display objects from three periods ______ the Old Kingdom, the New Kingdom, and the Ptolemaic era.
A) periods; || What follows is a list, not a complete sentence; a semicolon needs a complete sentence on both sides.
*B) periods: || Correct: a colon after a complete sentence introduces the list of the three periods.
C) periods; such as || "such as" after a semicolon still leaves no independent clause after it.
D) periods, such as: || Never put a colon after "such as"; the colon must follow a complete sentence.

P: The novel's first chapter is set in a busy Cairo market ______ the second takes place in a silent village in Upper Egypt.
*A) market; || Correct: "the second takes place…" is a complete sentence, so the semicolon is right.
B) market, || Comma splice between two complete sentences.
C) market || Run-on with no punctuation.
D) market, however, || "however" is not a conjunction; with commas only, it still creates a comma splice.

P: Solar panels work best in direct sunlight ______ cloudy weather can cut their output sharply.
A) sunlight, || Comma splice.
B) sunlight || Run-on.
*C) sunlight; || Correct: two independent clauses joined by a semicolon.
D) sunlight, so, || The comma after "so" splits "so cloudy weather…" incorrectly; "so" needs only one comma, before it.

P: Before the invention of the printing press, books were copied by hand ______ a single copy could take months to finish.
A) hand, || Comma splice.
*B) hand; || Correct: both sides are complete sentences.
C) hand, and; || A comma and a semicolon cannot both appear around "and".
D) hand || Run-on.

P: Marine biologist Hana Fawzy studies coral reefs in the Red Sea ______ her research focuses on how the corals survive high water temperatures.
A) Sea, || Comma splice.
B) Sea || Run-on.
C) Sea; and || Semicolon plus "and" is a doubled joiner.
*D) Sea; || Correct: two related complete sentences joined by a semicolon.

## Practice 2 | Foundation
### Boundaries (semicolons)
P: The Aswan High Dam controls the yearly flood of the Nile ______ before it was built, the river often rose high enough to damage villages.
*A) Nile; || Correct: "before it was built, the river often rose…" is a complete sentence, so a semicolon joins the two clauses.
B) Nile, || Comma splice.
C) Nile || Run-on.
D) Nile: and || A colon cannot be followed by a coordinating conjunction like this.

P: Origami artists fold a single square of paper into complex shapes ______ they never cut or glue the paper.
A) shapes, || Comma splice.
*B) shapes; || Correct: two complete sentences joined by a semicolon.
C) shapes || Run-on.
D) shapes; because || "because they never cut…" is a dependent clause, so it cannot follow a semicolon.

P: Although the mountain trail is steep ______ thousands of hikers climb it every spring.
A) steep; || "Although the mountain trail is steep" is a dependent clause, not a complete sentence, so a semicolon cannot follow it.
B) steep. || The first part would be a fragment.
*C) steep, || Correct: an introductory dependent clause is followed by a comma.
D) steep: || A colon must follow a complete sentence; this part is dependent.

P: The early Arab astronomer al-Battani measured the length of the solar year with great accuracy ______ his tables were used by scholars in Europe for centuries.
A) accuracy, || Comma splice.
B) accuracy || Run-on.
*C) accuracy; || Correct: two independent clauses.
D) accuracy; and, || Semicolon plus "and" plus comma is a doubled joiner and an extra comma.

P: Electric cars produce no exhaust while driving ______ the electricity that charges them may still come from power plants that burn fuel.
A) driving, || Comma splice.
B) driving || Run-on.
C) driving; but, || The comma after "but" is wrong, and a semicolon with "but" doubles the joiner here.
*D) driving; || Correct: a semicolon joins two complete sentences.

P: The team spent weeks testing different bridge designs ______ the final model could carry twenty times its own weight.
*A) designs; || Correct: two complete, related sentences.
B) designs, || Comma splice.
C) designs || Run-on.
D) designs, it || "it" adds a new subject before "the final model", which makes the clause ungrammatical, and the comma would still splice.

P: Several animals in the Sahara rarely drink water ______ the fennec fox, for example, gets most of its water from food.
A) water, || Comma splice.
*B) water; || Correct: both sides are independent clauses.
C) water || Run-on.
D) water: and || A colon followed by "and" is incorrect.

P: The photographer waited for hours in the cold ______ wanting to capture the moment when the sun first touched the pyramids.
A) cold; || "wanting to capture…" is a participial phrase, not a complete sentence, so a semicolon cannot come before it.
*B) cold, || Correct: a comma introduces the descriptive phrase that begins with "wanting".
C) cold. || "Wanting to capture…" would become a fragment.
D) cold: and || A colon followed by "and" is never correct, and the phrase after it is not a list or explanation.

P: Most volcanoes form where tectonic plates meet ______ Hawaii's volcanoes are an exception because they sit above a hot spot in the middle of a plate.
A) meet, || Comma splice.
B) meet || Run-on.
*C) meet; || Correct: a semicolon joins the two related complete sentences.
D) meet; but, || The comma after "but" is wrong, and "but" is not needed after a semicolon.

P: The library extended its opening hours during exam season ______ students could now study there until midnight.
A) season, || Comma splice.
B) season || Run-on.
C) season; and || Doubled joiner.
*D) season; || Correct: two complete sentences.

## Practice 3 | Exam level
### Boundaries (semicolons)
P: Researchers once believed that octopuses lived completely solitary lives. Recent observations off the coast of Australia have challenged that view ______ divers recorded dozens of octopuses sharing dens and even interacting.
A) view, || Comma splice: "divers recorded dozens…" is a full sentence.
*B) view; || Correct: the second clause explains the first, and both are complete, so a semicolon fits.
C) view || Run-on.
D) view, and, || The comma after "and" is incorrect.

P: The festival featured musicians from Tunis, Tunisia; Beirut, Lebanon ______ and Rabat, Morocco.
A) Lebanon, || The first two items are already separated by a semicolon because each item contains a comma; the list must keep using semicolons.
*B) Lebanon; || Correct (SAT/ACT extra rule): when list items already contain commas, semicolons separate the items.
C) Lebanon: || A colon does not separate list items.
D) Lebanon || Without punctuation the place names run together.

P: The bakery's most popular item is not bread or cake ______ it is a small date-filled biscuit that the owner's grandmother first made in the 1960s.
A) cake, || Comma splice.
B) cake || Run-on.
*C) cake; || Correct: two complete sentences; the second explains the first.
D) cake; which || "which it is" is ungrammatical; the second part must be an independent clause.

P: In her study of desert plants, botanist Leila Haddad found that some seeds can wait decades before sprouting ______ the seeds remain dormant until a heavy rain finally soaks the soil.
A) sprouting, || Comma splice.
*B) sprouting; || Correct: both sides are independent clauses.
C) sprouting, which, || "which" would need a verb of its own, and "which the seeds remain" is ungrammatical.
D) sprouting || Run-on.

P: The committee received more than four hundred proposals for the new park ______ only six, however, met all of the city's requirements.
A) park, || Comma splice: "however" does not make the comma enough.
B) park || Run-on.
C) park, but; || A semicolon cannot directly follow "but" here.
*D) park; || Correct: the second clause is complete, and "however" sits inside it between commas.

P: The ancient Egyptians used a calendar of 365 days ______ the year was divided into twelve months of thirty days each, plus five extra days at the end.
*A) days; || Correct: two complete sentences.
B) days, || Comma splice.
C) days || Run-on.
D) days; because || "because" would turn the second part into a dependent clause, which cannot follow a semicolon.

P: Pianist Omar Khairat often blends Western classical forms with Arabic melodies. His soundtrack for one popular film begins with a simple oud theme ______ by the end, a full orchestra has joined in.
A) theme, || Comma splice.
B) theme || Run-on.
*C) theme; || Correct: two independent clauses.
D) theme: and || A colon followed by "and" is incorrect.

P: The robotics club met every Thursday ______ during the week before the competition, however, its members met every day.
A) Thursday, || Comma splice.
*B) Thursday; || Correct: the second part is a complete sentence with "however" inside it.
C) Thursday || Run-on.
D) Thursday, however; || This attaches "however" to the wrong clause and puts a semicolon in the middle of the second sentence.

P: Tea first reached Britain in the seventeenth century ______ at first, it was so expensive that only wealthy families could buy it.
A) century, || Comma splice.
B) century || Run-on.
C) century; and, || The comma after "and" is incorrect.
*D) century; || Correct: a semicolon joins the two complete sentences.

P: The study included participants from three cities ______ Giza, where the climate is hot and dry; Mansoura, where it is humid; and Alexandria, where sea breezes cool the air.
A) cities; || What follows is a list, not a complete sentence, so a semicolon is wrong here.
B) cities, || A comma is too weak to introduce a list whose items are already separated by semicolons; it also makes the sentence confusing.
*C) cities: || Correct: a colon after a complete sentence introduces the list.
D) cities || Without punctuation, the list runs straight into the sentence.

## Practice 4 | Exam level
### Boundaries (semicolons)

D) Kings, and; || A semicolon cannot follow "and" like this.

*A) fast; || Correct: the second clause, a complete sentence, gives evidence for the first.

P: For the experiment, students tested three liquids ______ salt water, which conducted electricity well; distilled water, which barely conducted it at all; and vinegar, which fell in between.

P: Ancient Greek theatres were built into hillsides ______ the curved shape of the seating carried the actors' voices to the back rows.

P: In the novel, Nadia rarely speaks about her childhood in the village ______ when she finally does, the memories pour out in a single long letter to her brother.

P: Wind turbines are often placed far out at sea ______ there, the wind is stronger and more constant than on land.

P: The city council approved the plan for new bicycle lanes ______ construction will begin in March and should finish before the summer.

## Practice 5 | Challenge
### Boundaries (semicolons)
P: The poet's early collections, written while she was still a student in Damascus, focus on the city's crowded streets and markets ______ her later work, by contrast, turns almost entirely to the quiet of the countryside.
A) markets, || Comma splice: the long first clause still ends before a new complete sentence.
B) markets || Run-on.
*C) markets; || Correct: despite the long phrases, both sides are independent clauses ("her later work … turns…").
D) markets, whereas, || "whereas" should not be followed by a comma, and "whereas her later work, by contrast" doubles the contrast.

P: The conference included speakers from Amman, Jordan ______ Doha, Qatar; and Muscat, Oman.
A) Jordan, || The other items are separated by a semicolon; the list must use the same separator throughout, and commas already appear inside each item.
*B) Jordan; || Correct (SAT/ACT extra rule): items that contain commas are separated by semicolons, matching "Qatar; and".
C) Jordan: || A colon cannot separate two list items.
D) Jordan || Without punctuation, the items run together.

P: What surprised the researchers most was not the size of the colony ______ it was how quickly the ants rebuilt their nest after the flood.
A) colony, || Comma splice.
*B) colony; || Correct: "What surprised the researchers most was not the size of the colony" and "it was how quickly…" are both complete sentences.
C) colony || Run-on.
D) colony; and || Doubled joiner.

P: Because the manuscript had been stored in a dry desert cave for nearly two thousand years ______ its ink remained clear enough for scholars to read most of the text.
*A) years, || Correct: the long opening is a dependent "Because…" clause, so a comma separates it from the main clause.
B) years; || The part before the blank is dependent (it starts with "Because"), so a semicolon is wrong.
C) years. || The first part would be a fragment.
D) years: || A colon must follow a complete sentence.

P: The architect designed the school around a central courtyard ______ every classroom opens onto it, so students can move between lessons without walking through long corridors.
A) courtyard, || Comma splice.
B) courtyard || Run-on.
*C) courtyard; || Correct: the second clause is complete and closely related.
D) courtyard; that || "that every classroom opens onto it" is dependent and cannot follow a semicolon.

P: In the Middle Ages, the House of Wisdom in Baghdad attracted scholars of many backgrounds ______ translators there turned Greek, Persian, and Indian works into Arabic.
A) backgrounds, || Comma splice.
B) backgrounds || Run-on.
C) backgrounds; where || "where translators…" is dependent; it cannot follow a semicolon.
*D) backgrounds; || Correct: two independent clauses.

P: The documentary's director chose not to use narration ______ instead, she let the fishermen describe their own lives in their own words.
A) narration, || Comma splice: "instead" is not a conjunction, so a comma cannot join the clauses.
*B) narration; || Correct: a semicolon joins the clauses, and "instead," opens the second one.
C) narration || Run-on.
D) narration, instead; || This puts "instead" in the first clause and the semicolon in the wrong place.

P: The data were clear ______ in every region studied, rainfall had declined over the previous twenty years.
A) clear; and || Doubled joiner.
B) clear, || Comma splice.
*C) clear: || Correct: a colon can follow a complete sentence to introduce an explanation; "the data were clear" is explained by what follows.
D) clear || Run-on.

P: Many of the city's oldest houses have thick mud-brick walls ______ walls that keep the rooms cool even in August.
A) walls; || "walls that keep the rooms cool…" has no main verb of its own, so it is not a complete sentence and cannot follow a semicolon.
*B) walls, || Correct: the repeated noun "walls" begins a phrase that adds extra information, so a comma (or a dash) introduces it.
C) walls. || "Walls that keep the rooms cool…" would be a fragment.
D) walls, and || "and walls that keep…" leaves the second part without a verb and makes the sentence ungrammatical.

P: Some readers find the novel's ending disappointing ______ others consider it the most honest part of the book.
A) disappointing, || Comma splice.
B) disappointing || Run-on.
C) disappointing; while || "while others consider…" is dependent and cannot follow a semicolon.
*D) disappointing; || Correct: two independent clauses.
