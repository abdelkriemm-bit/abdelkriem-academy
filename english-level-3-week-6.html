Grammar: The passive · present simple and past simple (is made / was built)

اقرا الشرح بالعربي الأول، وبعدين بص على الجدول والأمثلة. لو مش فاهم، اسمع درس الجرامر اللي في خطة الأسبوع.

Passive = be + past participle (made, built, celebrated). We use it when the action is more important than the person who does it, or when we don't know who did it.

Present: am / is / are + past participle · Past: was / were + past participle · by + the doer (only if it is important).

المبني للمجهول في العربية (بُني، يُصنع، اكتُشف) = be + التصريف الثالث. أشهر خطأين: حذف الفعل be (✗ The pyramids built 4,500 years ago) أو استخدام التصريف الثاني (✗ It was wrote / ✗ It was builded). تذكّر: is / are للمضارع، و was / were للماضي، ثم التصريف الثالث دائمًا.

ActivePassive
People make koshari with rice.Koshari is made with rice.
They speak Arabic in Egypt.Arabic is spoken in Egypt.
Howard Carter discovered the tomb in 1922.The tomb was discovered (by Howard Carter) in 1922.
Present simple passivePast simple passive
PositiveIt is made. They are sold.It was built. They were found.
NegativeIt isn't made. They aren't sold.It wasn't built. They weren't found.
QuestionIs it made…? Where are they sold?When was it built? Were they found…?

✗ The museum opened by the president. ✓ The museum was opened by the president.

made of = you can still see the material (made of wood) · made from = the material changes (cheese is made from milk).

Use by only when the doer is important: This novel was written by Naguib Mahfouz.

Exercises · تمارين

حل كل تمرين في يومه بالقلم الرصاص، وبعدين دوس «Show the answers» وصحّح لنفسك. اكتب أخطاءك في الكشكول وراجعها في اليوم السادس.

Exercise 1 · Complete with the present simple passive.

  1. 1) Arabic ___ (speak) in 22 countries.
  2. 2) Feteer ___ (bake) in a very hot oven.
  3. 3) These carpets ___ (make) by hand.
  4. 4) Coffee ___ (not grow) in Egypt.
  5. 5) How ___ this word ___ (pronounce)?
  6. 6) Lanterns ___ (sell) before Ramadan.
Show the answers
1) is spoken 2) is baked 3) are made 4) isn't grown 5) is … pronounced 6) are sold

Exercise 2 · Complete with the past simple passive.

  1. 1) The High Dam in Aswan ___ (build) in the 1960s.
  2. 2) The Rosetta Stone ___ (find) in 1799.
  3. 3) We ___ (invite) to an engagement party.
  4. 4) The match ___ (not cancel).
  5. 5) When ___ the Suez Canal ___ (open)?
  6. 6) Their wedding photos ___ (take) on a boat on the Nile.
Show the answers
1) was built 2) was found 3) were invited 4) wasn't cancelled 5) was … opened 6) were taken

Exercise 3 · Rewrite in the passive.

  1. 1) People celebrate Sham El-Nessim in spring.
  2. 2) They serve tea after dinner.
  3. 3) Someone stole my bag.
  4. 4) Workers built the bridge in 2019.
  5. 5) Naguib Mahfouz wrote this novel.
Show the answers
1) Sham El-Nessim is celebrated in spring. 2) Tea is served after dinner. 3) My bag was stolen. / My bag was stolen by someone. 4) The bridge was built in 2019. / The bridge was built by workers in 2019. 5) This novel was written by Naguib Mahfouz.

Exercise 4 · Translate into English.

  1. 1) يُصنع هذا الطبق من الأرز واللحم.
  2. 2) بُني هذا المسجد منذ مئتي عام.
  3. 3) يُحتفل بالعيد مع العائلة.
  4. 4) سُرقت سيارته الأسبوع الماضي.
Show the answers
1) This dish is made with rice and meat. / This dish is made from rice and meat. 2) This mosque was built two hundred years ago. / This mosque was built 200 years ago. 3) Eid is celebrated with the family. / Eid is celebrated with family. 4) His car was stolen last week.

Do each exercise in pencil first, then open the answers to check.

Write & Speak · اكتب واتكلم

اكتب الموضوع في الكشكول بنفسك، وبص على المثال لو احتجت، وبعدين اقراه بصوت عالي أو سجّل نفسك.

Write & Speak — Write 120–150 words: a short guide to an Egyptian tradition or celebration for a foreign visitor. Use at least five passive verbs (is celebrated, is made, are sold, was started…). Then record it as if you were a tour guide.

Example: Ramadan is one of the most special times of the year in Egypt. It is celebrated by millions of people across the country. During the holy month, Muslims don't eat or drink from dawn until sunset. The streets are decorated with colourful lights, and traditional lanterns, called fawanees, are sold everywhere. They are often given to children as presents. The fast is broken at sunset with dates and water, and then a big family meal is served. Popular dishes like stuffed vine leaves and kunafa are made at home or bought from famous shops. In the past, people were woken up for suhoor by a man called the mesaharaty, who walked through the streets with a drum. This custom is still followed in some neighbourhoods today. If you visit Egypt in Ramadan, you will probably be invited to iftar by people you have just met!
