Speaking Part 2: General Conversation

Every topic in all three themes with the questions you are most likely to be asked, developed model answers, phrases for opinions, examples and the future, and a two-week practice plan.

British & IGCSE › Oxford International GCSE English as a Second Language

Part 2 of the Speaking test is the General conversation: about 6-7 minutes, worth 25 marks (Communication 10 + Knowledge and use of language 5 + Spontaneity and fluency 5 + Pronunciation and intonation 5). It covers the two themes that were not on your photocard, with roughly equal time — about three minutes — on each. Your teacher chooses the topics inside each theme and follows up on what you say, so you will not know the exact questions. That is why this page gives you every topic in all three themes, the questions you are most likely to hear, and model answers to learn from.

Learn ideas, not speeches

Do not memorise these model answers word for word. Examiners notice recited answers, and they lower your mark for Spontaneity and fluency. Instead, take the ideas, vocabulary and structures from each model, then answer in your own words about your own life. Practise with different questions each time.

What the examiner rewardsWhat it looks like in your answers
Developed answers (Communication)3-5 sentences for most questions: answer, reason, example (A-R-E).
Narrating eventsWhen asked *Tell me about…* or *What did you do…?*, tell a short story: when, where, who, what happened, how you felt.
Convincing opinionsAn opinion + reasons + an example, and sometimes the other side.
Past, present and future (Language)Every topic: one past event, one present habit, one future plan or *if* sentence.
Natural reactions (Spontaneity)Respond to what the teacher actually asked; use natural starters like *Well…*, *Actually…*, *To be honest…*
Clear pronunciation and intonationSteady speed, clear endings (*-ed, -s*), natural rise and fall.

Useful phrases for the conversation

Giving opinions

  • In my opinion, … / Personally, I think …
  • As far as I'm concerned, …
  • I'm convinced that …
  • I'd say that … / It seems to me that …
  • What I like most about … is …
  • I'm not a big fan of … because …

Agreeing and disagreeing

  • I completely agree, because …
  • That's true, but …
  • I see your point, but I'm not sure that …
  • I don't think so, actually.
  • It depends on …
  • Yes and no. On the one hand … on the other hand …

Giving examples

  • For example, … / For instance, …
  • Take my cousin, for example: …
  • A good example of this is …
  • such as … / like …
  • I remember once when …
  • This happened to me last year, when …

Buying time

  • Well, let me think …
  • That's an interesting question.
  • Hmm, I've never really thought about that, but …
  • It's hard to say, but I suppose …
  • How can I put it? …

Talking about the past

  • When I was younger, I used to …
  • Last summer, … / A few years ago, …
  • The best/worst … I've ever … was …
  • I'll never forget the time when …
  • Before I started …, I had never …

Talking about the future

  • Next year I'm going to …
  • I'm planning to … / I'm hoping to …
  • I'd love to … one day.
  • If everything goes well, I'll …
  • In ten years' time, I think I'll be …
  • If I could …, I would …

Theme 1: Identity and culture · Me, my family, friends and people I know

Sub-topic: Relationships with family

  1. Tell me about your family. (present)
  2. Who are you closest to in your family, and why? (opinion)
  3. What did you do with your family last weekend / during the last holiday? (past)
  4. Do you get on well with your parents? Do you ever argue? (present / opinion)
  5. How is your family life different from your grandparents' when they were young? (past / comparison)
  6. What kind of parent would you like to be in the future? (future / hypothetical)
  7. Should teenagers help with housework? (opinion)
Model answer82 words · Top-band quality: Communication 9-10 · Language 5 · Spontaneity & fluency 5
Task: Who are you closest to in your family, and why?

I'd say I'm closest to my older brother, Ahmed, even though we're very different. He's calm and organised, while I'm a bit chaotic! The main reason is that he always listens without judging me. For example, when I failed a chemistry test last year, I was too scared to tell my parents, so he helped me explain it and then studied with me every evening. Next year he's going to university in Alexandria, and I'm honestly going to miss him a lot.

Why this gets the marks
  • Contrast (*even though…*, *while…*) and a clear reason: an explained opinion.
  • Narrates a past event with detail, then a future reference: all three time frames.
Model answer81 words · Top-band quality: Communication 9-10 · Language 5 · Spontaneity & fluency 5
Task: How is your family life different from your grandparents' when they were young?

It's very different. My grandfather grew up in a village with eight brothers and sisters, and they all slept in two rooms. He started working on the farm when he was only twelve, so he left school early. These days, families are smaller and children spend much longer in education. On the other hand, I think my grandparents' generation spent more time together, because there were no phones. If I could change one thing, I'd bring back those long family evenings.

Why this gets the marks
  • Comparison of past and present with *These days* and *On the other hand*.
  • Second conditional (*If I could…, I'd…*) adds complex language naturally.

The best holiday I've ever had was a trip to Luxor and Aswan with my family two years ago. We took the overnight train from Cairo, which was an adventure in itself. In Luxor we visited the Valley of the Kings, and I couldn't believe how bright the colours in the tombs still were after thousands of years. In Aswan we sailed on a felucca at sunset. I'd love to go back one day with my own children.

Why this gets the marks
  • Superlative with present perfect (*The best holiday I've ever had*) then a past narrative.
  • Specific, vivid details and a future wish at the end.
Model answer76 words · Top-band quality: Communication 9-10 · Language 5 · Spontaneity & fluency 5
Task: What are the advantages and disadvantages of tourism for a country?

Tourism brings a lot of benefits. It creates jobs for hotel staff, guides and drivers, and it brings foreign currency into the country. It also helps people from different cultures understand each other. On the other hand, too many tourists can damage historical sites, and some popular beaches become very polluted. In my view, the solution is sustainable tourism, for example limiting the number of visitors to certain tombs and teaching tourists to respect local customs.

Why this gets the marks
  • Advantages, disadvantages and a solution: a fully developed, convincing opinion.
  • Topic vocabulary: *foreign currency, historical sites, sustainable tourism*.

  1. What subjects are you studying this year? (present)
  2. What is your favourite subject, and why? (opinion)
  3. Which subject do you find most difficult? How do you deal with it? (present)
  4. Tell me about a project or piece of work you were proud of. (past)
  5. How do you prepare for exams? (present)
  6. Which subjects will be most useful for your future? (future / opinion)
  7. If you could add a new subject to the timetable, what would it be? (hypothetical)
Model answer77 words · Top-band quality: Communication 9-10 · Language 5 · Spontaneity & fluency 5
Task: Which subject do you find most difficult? How do you deal with it?

Physics is definitely the hardest for me, especially when there are lots of formulas to remember. When I started Year 10, I got quite low marks in the first test, and I felt really discouraged. Since then, I've changed the way I study: I make flashcards for every formula and I practise past-paper questions every weekend. My marks have improved a lot. I'm planning to ask my teacher for extra help before the final exams as well.

Why this gets the marks
  • Past problem → present perfect change → future plan.
  • Precise study vocabulary: *formulas, flashcards, past-paper questions*.
Model answer80 words · Top-band quality: Communication 9-10 · Language 5 · Spontaneity & fluency 5
Task: If you could add a new subject to the timetable, what would it be?

I'd add a subject about practical life skills, like managing money, cooking and basic first aid. At the moment, we learn a lot of theory, but nobody teaches us how to open a bank account or what to do if someone faints. My older sister told me that when she went to university, she had no idea how to budget, and she ran out of money in the first month! A subject like that would prepare us for real life.

Why this gets the marks
  • Hypothetical answer with *I'd add…* and *would prepare*.
  • Reported speech (*my sister told me that…*) adds variety.

Theme 3: Current and future study and employment · Life at school/college

Model answer83 words · Top-band quality: Communication 9-10 · Language 5 · Spontaneity & fluency 5
Task: Tell me about a school trip or event you enjoyed.

In Year 8 we went on a school trip to the Grand Egyptian Museum, and it was one of the best days I've had at school. We travelled by coach, and a guide showed us the huge statue of Ramses II in the entrance hall. What impressed me most was the Tutankhamun collection, because I'd only seen it in books before. We also had to complete a worksheet in groups, which made it more fun. I'd love to go back with my family.

Why this gets the marks
  • Narration with a cleft sentence (*What impressed me most was…*).
  • Past perfect (*I'd only seen it*) and a future wish.
Model answer78 words · Top-band quality: Communication 9-10 · Language 5 · Spontaneity & fluency 5
Task: Do you think school rules are fair? Which rule would you change?

Most of the rules at my school are fair, for example the rules about respecting teachers and arriving on time, because without them the school would be chaotic. However, I'd change the rule about uniforms in summer. We have to wear long trousers and a jacket even when it's thirty-five degrees outside, which makes it very hard to concentrate. If students could wear polo shirts in hot weather, I think everyone would feel more comfortable and learn better.

Why this gets the marks
  • Agrees in part, then disagrees with a precise reason: a convincing opinion.
  • Second conditional with a result (*everyone would feel…*).

Model answer79 words · Top-band quality: Communication 9-10 · Language 5 · Spontaneity & fluency 5
Task: What are you going to do after your International GCSEs?

After my International GCSEs, I'm going to stay at my school to do A levels in maths, physics and chemistry, because I want to study engineering at university. I chose these subjects after a long conversation with my careers adviser last term, who explained exactly what universities require. To be honest, I'm a bit worried about the workload, as my cousin told me A levels are much harder. But if I organise my time well, I think I'll cope.

Why this gets the marks
  • *going to* for plans and *because* for reasons.
  • Past event (*I chose… last term*), feelings and a first conditional.

I'd love to study abroad, maybe in Germany, because it has excellent engineering universities. The biggest advantage is independence: you learn to cook, manage money and solve problems on your own. Task: Have you ever had a part-time job or done work experience?

Yes, last summer I did two weeks of work experience at my uncle's printing company. I mostly helped in the office, answering emails and checking orders, but one day they let me operate a small printing machine, which was exciting. I learnt that working life is much more tiring than school, because you're on your feet all day. It also showed me how important it is to be organised. Model answer78 words · Top-band quality: Communication 9-10 · Language 5 · Spontaneity & fluency 5
Task: How do you think jobs will change because of technology?

I think technology will change almost every job. Many routine tasks, like checking documents or working in warehouses, will probably be done by computers and robots. That worries me a little, because some people will lose their jobs. On the other hand, new jobs will appear — twenty years ago, nobody had heard of app developers or drone pilots. So I think the most important thing for my generation is to keep learning new skills throughout our careers.

Why this gets the marks
  • Future predictions with *will* and *probably*, plus a past comparison (*twenty years ago*).
  • Balanced and convincing, with a clear conclusion.

Two-week speaking practice plan

Practise 15-20 minutes a day, out loud. Record yourself on a phone (audio only) and listen back. Work with a partner, a parent or a classmate when you can: one asks the questions on this page in a random order, the other answers without notes.

DayFocusWhat to do
1The test formatRead the Speaking overview page. Learn the 40-mark breakdown and the A-R-E method. Answer 3 easy questions (family, school, free time) using A-R-E.
2Theme 1: family and friendsPrepare key words for each question above. Answer 5 questions aloud; record and check: did you use past, present and future?
3Theme 1: technologyLearn 10 words (e.g. *notifications, screen time, influencer, addiction, privacy*). Answer 4 questions; include one *If…* sentence in each.
4Theme 1: free timeMusic, cinema, sport, festivals: prepare one past story for each. Tell each story in 45-60 seconds.
5Photocard practiceUse a practice card. Follow the 10-minute routine with a timer, then answer all 5 questions aloud.
6Theme 2: home town and social issuesAnswer questions on your area, volunteering and health. Focus on giving two reasons for every opinion.
7Mock conversation 1Partner asks questions from Themes 1 and 2 for 3 minutes each, following up with *Why? Can you give an example?* Listen back and note three improvements.
8Theme 2: global issues and travelEnvironment, poverty, tourism: learn 10 topic words. Practise *On the one hand… on the other hand…* in every answer.
9Theme 3: studies and schoolAnswer questions on subjects, exams and school life. Add one past event and one future plan to each.
10Theme 3: post-16 and careersPrepare your real plans: subjects, university, job. Practise explaining why with *because, since, so that*.
11Pronunciation dayRead your recorded answers back. Practise *th, p/b, v/f*, word stress (*tech-NOL-o-gy, en-VI-ron-ment*) and *-ed* endings.
12Spontaneity dayPartner asks unexpected follow-up questions. Answer straight away using a natural filler. No notes at all.
13Mock testFull test: 10 minutes' preparation, photocard 3-4 minutes, then conversation 6-7 minutes on the two other themes. Record it.
14ReviewListen to your mock test. Check: developed answers? opinions explained? past/present/future? natural reactions? Fix your three biggest weaknesses.
