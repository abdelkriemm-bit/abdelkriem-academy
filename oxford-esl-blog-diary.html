Oxford ESL 9280 · Writing

Question 3: Blog and Diary Entry

How to score 16/16 in Question 3: blog vs diary, the right tenses for recounts and opinions, a paragraph plan, openings and endings, and two full-mark models with notes for Content 8 + Language 8.

British & IGCSE › Oxford International GCSE English as a Second Language

Question 3 is a longer, form-specific piece of 70-90 words worth 16 marks (Content 8 + Language 8). The task gives you 3-4 compulsory bullet points, usually four. The form can change, but in recent papers it has most often been a blog, and a diary entry is the other classic choice (the two are even offered together in the specimen paper). This pack teaches you both, shows how to recount an experience, give opinions and look ahead, and gives two full-mark models with notes for 8 + 8.

What the task is and where it sits

ItemDetails
PositionQuestion 3, the third of four compulsory questions
FormsMost often a blog post. It can also be a diary entry, an email, a report or an article.
Words70-90
Marks16 = Content and Communication 8 + Language 8
Bullet points3-4, all compulsory ("You must include:"). Usually four.
Skills testedW2 (clear, coherent longer text), W3 (accurate vocabulary, grammar, spelling, punctuation), W4 (fluency and creativity)
Typical topicsPersonal experiences: an activity with friends, an event or festival, a person who inspires you, a sport, music, a trip, a celebration, work experience
Suggested timeAbout 20 minutes: 4 to plan, 13 to write, 3 to check
Why Q3 matters

Q3 is worth 16 marks, almost as much as Q1 and Q2 together (21). It is the first question with four levels for language, so the top band asks for a wide variety of effective vocabulary and structures, not just correct ones. Think of it as the bridge between the short tasks and the long Q4.

The golden rule of Q3

If one bullet is missing, the examiner calls it partial coverage, and the answer cannot enter the top content band, however beautiful the English is. Examiners tick the bullets one by one as they read. The bullet students forget most is a short one, such as "where".

How to get 16/16: checklist

  • Underline the form (blog or diary) and number every bullet ①②③④.
  • Deal with the short bullets (what, where, who, when) quickly, in one clear sentence each.
  • Spend most of your words on the "why" or opinion bullet: a reason + an example + a feeling or result.
  • Use the right tenses: past for the experience, present for opinions and facts, future or conditional for plans and recommendations.
  • Use at least four different complex structures (when, because, which, where, although, if, so that), plus a past perfect where it fits.
  • Use effective, precise vocabulary: "thrilled", "crowded", "breathtaking" only if they fit and you can spell them.
  • Link your ideas with time linkers (first, later, by the evening) and logic linkers (because, however, as a result).
  • Use a friendly, personal voice: you are talking to readers (blog) or to yourself (diary).
  • Keep to about 70-90 words. Around 80-88 is ideal.
  • Check spelling, full stops, commas, apostrophes (can't, it's) and past-tense verb forms.
كيف تحصل على 16/16 في الـ Blog أو الـ Diary

  • النقاط القصيرة (أين – مع مَن – متى) تكفيها جملة واحدة لكل نقطة، أما النقطة الأخيرة (لماذا / رأيك) فاكتب فيها سبباً ومثالاً وإحساساً.

Blog or diary: what is the difference?

FeatureBlog postDiary entry
ReaderOther people online (your followers, students, the public)Yourself (private)
PurposeShare an experience, opinion or recommendationRecord your day and how you feel about it
VoiceFriendly and chatty, speaks to the reader with "you"Personal and honest, speaks to yourself
Typical openingHi everyone! / Welcome back to my blog!Dear Diary, / What a day!
Typical endingA recommendation or a question to readersA reflection, a feeling or a hope for tomorrow
TensesPast (experience) + present (opinion) + future/conditional (advice)Past (today's events) + present (feelings now) + future (tomorrow)
QuestionsQuestions to readers: Have you ever...?Questions to yourself: What was I thinking?
MarksSame: 16 marks, 8 + 8Same: 16 marks, 8 + 8

Remember the Lead Examiner's advice: these differences are on the surface and earn no marks by themselves. "Hi everyone!" or "Dear Diary," is a nice touch that shows you know your reader, but marks come from covering the bullets with detail and using accurate, varied English. Do not spend more than two or three words on the greeting.

Tone

  • Informal but correct. Contractions (I'm, can't, we'd) are fine. Text-speak (u, gonna, lol) is not.
  • Personal. Use "I" and "we", and say how you felt: nervous, proud, amazed, disappointed.
  • Lively. One exclamation and one question are enough to make it sound real. Do not put "!!!" after every sentence.
  • Clear. A blog for strangers needs a little explanation: "Fayoum, which is only two hours from Cairo".

Tenses for recounts, opinions and the future

Job in your answerTenseExample
Set the scenePast simple / past continuousLast Friday we went to the stadium. It was raining when we arrived.
Tell the events in orderPast simple + time linkersFirst we queued for tickets. Then we found our seats.
Show something happened earlierPast perfectBy half-time, our team had scored twice.
Describe a habitPresent simple / used toWe usually meet at a café. I used to hate swimming.
Give an opinion nowPresent simpleI think it is the best way to relax.
Talk about experiencePresent perfectI have been to three concerts this year.
Recommend or adviseModal / imperative / conditionalYou should try it. If you visit, go early.
Plans and hopesFuture (will / going to) / hope toNext year I am going to enter the competition.

Structure: paragraph by paragraph

Most Q3 tasks give four bullets that move from simple facts to a reason or opinion. Follow their order unless combining two is more natural. Short paragraphs make the coverage easy for the examiner to see.

PartWhat to writeWords
Greeting (optional)Hi everyone! / Dear Diary,2
Paragraph 1Bullets 1 and 2 (usually what + where / who / when) in one or two sentences, with one extra detail20-25
Paragraph 2Bullet 3 (what you did / what happened): a sequence of 2-3 actions with time linkers and specific details25-30
Paragraph 3Bullet 4 (why / opinion): a reason, an example and a feeling or result20-25
Closing lineBlog: a recommendation or question to readers. Diary: a feeling or a look ahead.5-10

How to start

Blog openings

  • Hi everyone! Last weekend, I finally...
  • Welcome back to my blog! Today I want to tell you about...
  • If you have never tried..., this post is for you.
  • Have you ever wondered what it is like to...?
  • I've been looking forward to writing this post since...

Diary openings

  • Dear Diary, What a day!
  • Dear Diary, I can't believe what happened today.
  • Today was my first day at...
  • I'm writing this in bed because I'm too excited to sleep.
  • Dear Diary, Today didn't go exactly as I had planned.

How to end

Blog closings

  • I would definitely recommend it to anyone who loves...
  • Have you been there? Let me know in the comments!
  • If you get the chance, don't miss it.
  • Next time, I'm going to bring my whole family.
  • Thanks for reading, and see you in my next post!

Diary closings

  • I can't wait for tomorrow!
  • I'm exhausted, but it was worth every minute.
  • Tomorrow I'll try to...
  • I hope next week will be just as good.
  • Time to sleep now. Goodnight!

Useful phrases

Time and sequence

  • At first,
  • After that,
  • Later that afternoon,
  • As soon as we arrived,
  • By the end of the day,
  • Meanwhile,

Feelings

  • I was thrilled when...
  • I felt a bit nervous at first, but...
  • It was the most exciting moment of...
  • I was surprised to find that...
  • I'll never forget...

Opinions and reasons

  • What I enjoyed most was...
  • The main reason I love it is that...
  • In my opinion,
  • It was worth it because...
  • Not only was it..., but it was also...

Detail builders

  • such as
  • for example,
  • which is...
  • where we...
  • who has...
  • so that...

Model answers

Word counts include the greeting. Each paragraph is shown separately.

Model 1: Blog post about a day trip88 words · 16/16 (Content 8/8 + Language 8/8)
Task: Write a blog post about a day trip you took to a place in your country. You must include: • where you went • who you went with • what you did there • why you would recommend it. Your blog should be between 70 and 90 words.

Hi everyone!

Last weekend, my cousins and I took a day trip to Fayoum, which is only two hours from Cairo.

In the morning, we visited Wadi El Rayan, where we saw the famous waterfalls and climbed a huge sand dune. Then we had a delicious lunch of grilled fish beside Lake Qarun.

I would definitely recommend Fayoum because it is cheap, peaceful and surprisingly beautiful. If you need a break from city noise, this is the perfect escape. Have you been there? Tell me in the comments!

Why this gets the marks
  • Bullet 1 (where) ✓: "Fayoum", with an extra detail for readers who do not know it ("only two hours from Cairo").
  • Bullet 2 (who) ✓: "my cousins and I", in the same first sentence. Two short bullets are covered quickly and naturally.
  • Bullet 3 (what you did) ✓: three specific actions in order (visited the waterfalls, climbed a sand dune, lunch beside the lake) with a time linker ("In the morning", "Then").
  • Bullet 4 (why recommend) ✓: three reasons (cheap, peaceful, beautiful) plus a condition for the reader ("If you need a break..."). This is the developed detail that lifts content to 8.
  • Content 8/8: fully relevant, a wide range of detail, clear and coherent. The blog voice (greeting, question to readers) shows awareness of the audience without wasting words.
  • Language 8/8, variety: relative clauses with which and where, past simple recount, modal + adverb ("would definitely recommend"), a conditional sentence ('If you need a break…, this is…'), rhetorical question, a list of three adjectives.
  • Language 8/8, accuracy and effect: precise vocabulary (escape, surprisingly, grilled), correct capitals for place names, commas after starting phrases, no errors.
Model 2: Diary entry about work experience84 words · 16/16 (Content 8/8 + Language 8/8)
Task: Write a diary entry about your first day of work experience. You must include: • where you did your work experience • what tasks you did • the people you worked with • what you learned about the job. Your diary entry should be between 70 and 90 words.

Dear Diary,

Today was my first day of work experience at a veterinary clinic near our house, and I am exhausted but happy!

At first, I only cleaned cages and fed the cats. Later, Dr Samy let me help while he examined a puppy that had hurt its paw.

The nurses were patient and answered all my questions.

I learned that being a vet is much harder than I expected, because you need patience as well as knowledge. Still, I can't wait for tomorrow!

Why this gets the marks
  • Bullet 1 (where) ✓: "a veterinary clinic near our house" in the opening sentence.
  • Bullet 2 (tasks) ✓: a sequence from simple to exciting tasks (cleaned cages, fed the cats, helped with a puppy) using "At first" and "Later".
  • Bullet 3 (people) ✓: Dr Samy and the nurses, each with a detail about how they treated the writer.
  • Bullet 4 (what you learned) ✓: a real insight with a reason ("harder than I expected, because you need patience as well as knowledge").
  • Content 8/8: all four bullets, a wide range of detail, a clear personal voice. Feelings at the start and end give the diary a natural shape.
  • Language 8/8, variety: past simple recount, present for feelings now ("I am exhausted"), past perfect in a relative clause ("that had hurt its paw"), time clause ("while he examined"), comparison ("much harder than I expected"), "as well as", contraction suited to a diary ("can't").
  • Language 8/8, accuracy: harder words spelt correctly (veterinary, experience, exhausted, patience), correct capital letters for the title "Dr Samy", accurate punctuation.

What makes these 8 + 8: the pattern

  • The first two bullets are covered in the first sentence or two.
  • The action bullet has a clear sequence with time linkers.
  • The last bullet has a reason and an example or result, not just "it was fun".
  • Each answer uses at least three tenses correctly (past, present, future, past perfect).
  • Each answer uses relative clauses, time clauses and "because" clauses.
  • Vocabulary is precise and fits the topic, and every word is spelt correctly.
  • The greeting and ending are short and natural, so almost every word works for the bullets.

Practice

  1. Practice task 1 (blog): Write a blog post about a family celebration you enjoyed. You must include: • what the celebration was • where it took place • what you did • why it was special. 70-90 words. Make a plan.

    Show answer
    Plan: ① my grandparents' 50th wedding anniversary ② my uncle's garden in Mansoura, decorated with lights ③ cooked together in the morning. My brother and I made a short video of old family photos. Dinner for thirty relatives ④ the whole family together for the first time in three years. My grandfather was moved and thanked everyone. Language targets: "which" + "where" clauses, past perfect ("had not seen each other for three years"), an opinion in the present, a question to readers at the end.

  2. Practice task 2 (diary): Write a diary entry about a day when something went wrong. You must include: • where you were • what went wrong • how you solved the problem • how you feel now. 70-90 words. Make a plan.

    Show answer
    Plan: ① at the train station in Cairo, going to Alexandria ② I left my bag with my ticket and phone in a taxi ③ the station manager let me call the taxi company. The driver returned the bag 40 minutes later. I caught the next train ④ relieved and grateful. Next time I will check before I get out. Language targets: past continuous ("While I was paying..."), past perfect ("I had left"), "so that", a future promise, a feeling adjective (relieved).

  3. Which tense fits each job: (a) what happened yesterday (b) your opinion now (c) your plan for next month?

    Show answer
    (a) past simple (b) present simple (c) future: going to / will

  4. Correct this sentence: "Last summer we go to the beach and we swimmed every day, it was really nice."

    Show answer
    Last summer we went to the beach and swam every day. It was really relaxing. (past forms, a full stop instead of a comma splice, a more precise adjective)

  5. A blog covers three of the four bullets perfectly with excellent English. What is the highest content level it can reach, and why?

    Show answer
    It cannot reach the top level (7-8), because a missing bullet means partial coverage. Add one sentence for the missing bullet.

  6. Upgrade: "I liked the trip because it was good."

    Show answer
    Possible answer: "What I enjoyed most was the boat ride, because we could see the sunset over the river." (a specific detail and a real reason)
