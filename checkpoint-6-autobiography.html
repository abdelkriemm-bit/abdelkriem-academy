Checkpoint 6 · Paper 1 writing (non-fiction)

Autobiography

How to write your own life story for Paper 1: what it is, features, the four-paragraph structure, phrases, vocabulary, a strong sample and a practice task.

British & IGCSE › Checkpoint 6 English (Cambridge Primary 0058)

1. What is an autobiography?

An autobiography is the story of a person's life, written by that person. The word explains itself: *auto* = self, *bio* = life, *graphy* = writing. A good autobiography does not just list facts (birthday, school, favourite food). It shares the writer's thoughts, feelings and reflections about the important events of their life, so the reader gets to know the writer's personality.

QuestionAnswer
PurposeTo recount the key events of your own life and reflect on how they shaped you. It often also entertains the reader and tells them who you are.
AudienceUsually readers who do not know you well: a class book, a school website, a competition, a display for parents.
VoiceFirst person: I, me, my, we, our. You are telling your own story.
Where in the examPaper 1 (Non-fiction), Section B Writing, 25 marks, about 30 minutes. Autobiography is not one of the most common tasks, but it is one of the recount-type texts in the curriculum and can appear as 'a chapter of your life story' or 'write about yourself for…'. Its skills are also used in recounts, diaries and personal letters.
Text typeWho writes about whom?PersonMain focus
AutobiographyYou write about your own lifeFirst person (I)Several key parts of your life + reflection on who you are
BiographyYou write about someone else's lifeThird person (he/she)Facts, dates and achievements of another person
RecountYou retell one event or experienceUsually first personOne event in time order + a reflection
DiaryYou write privately about your dayFirst personToday's events and feelings, informal, with a date

What earns marks in an autobiography

CriterionMarksWhat a top answer looks like in an autobiography
Creation of texts5Every bullet covered and developed. A clear, personal voice. Feelings and reflection, not just facts. The reader feels they know you.
Vocabulary and language3Precise words for feelings and memories (treasure, influence, determined, unforgettable) and vivid description of your key moment.
Structure of texts7A title, clear paragraphs (sub-headings help), a logical order from past to future, time connectives, and an ending that links back.
Grammar and punctuation7Past tense for your story, present tense for 'now', future for your dreams, all used correctly. Varied sentences and a range of punctuation.
Word structure (spelling)3Ambitious words and tricky everyday words spelt correctly.

3. How to structure an autobiography

This is the teacher's four-paragraph structure. You may give each paragraph a sub-heading to show your organisation clearly. If the task gives bullet points, put each bullet into the matching paragraph.

PartWhat to writeTips
TitleA short, interesting title that captures your storyUse an image or a key idea: 'The Boy Who Drew Lighthouses'.
Paragraph 1: About myselfWhen and where you were born · your city or country · your family and what they are like · early childhood · friends and schoolDo not just list facts. Give each one a detail or a feeling. Start with one strong sentence.
Paragraph 2: Key aspects of my lifeThe person who has influenced you most · the object you value most · something that worries or frightens you · your hobbiesExplain WHY each one matters, with a small memory or example.
Paragraph 3: My most unforgettable momentWhat happened, in order · your thoughts and feelings at the time · how it changed youThis is a 'mini recount'. Slow it down, use your senses and end with the lesson you learned.
Paragraph 4: Reflection and future dreams(a) Describe yourself: a strength and a weakness, each with an example. (b) Your lifelong dreamEnd with a sentence that links back to your title or your first sentence.
Turn a fact into a strong sentence

Fact only: "I have one sister." Developed: "My little sister, Nour, is six and asks more questions than a teacher." Every fact you include should carry a detail, a feeling or a picture.

4. Useful phrases and sentence starters

How to start (openings)

  • I was born on [date] in [place], a place that has shaped who I am today.
  • Every story has a beginning, and mine starts in…
  • If you met me today, you would see…, but there is much more to my story.
  • My story begins in a small flat above a bakery, where the smell of fresh bread woke me every morning.
  • People say you can learn a lot about someone from their family. Let me introduce mine.

Childhood and family

  • As a young child, I spent most of my days…
  • My parents have always taught me the importance of…
  • Our home is never quiet, because…
  • I remember my early years as a time of…

Important people and objects

  • The person who has influenced me most is…
  • I will never forget the day she told me…
  • The object I treasure most is…, because…
  • Whenever I hold it, I remember…

Fears and challenges

  • If I am honest, the thing I fear most is…
  • One of the hardest moments of my life was when…
  • At first I struggled with…, but…

The unforgettable moment

  • My most unforgettable moment happened…
  • At first, I felt…
  • Suddenly, …
  • For one moment, I could not believe…
  • At that moment, I realised that…

Describing myself

  • I would describe myself as… because…
  • My friends say I am…, and I think they are right because…
  • However, I can sometimes be…, especially when…

How to end (closings)

  • My lifelong dream is to…
  • When I grow up, I hope to…
  • Looking back, I can see how much I have grown.
  • My story is only just beginning, and I cannot wait to see the next chapter.
  • Whatever happens, I will always carry [person/object/lesson] with me.
Word / phraseMeaning / use
memory (n.)something you remember: my happiest memory

influence (n./v.)the power to change someone: My grandfather has influenced me more than anyone.
treasure (v.)to love and keep something very carefully: I treasure the notebook she gave me.
upbringing (n.)the way you were raised as a child
milestone (n.)an important stage or event in a life: starting school was a milestone
turning point (n.)the moment when everything changed
ambition (n.)a strong wish to achieve something
determined (adj.)not giving up, even when it is hard
confident (adj.)sure of yourself
stubborn (adj.)not willing to change your mind
grateful (adj.)thankful
reflect (v.)to think carefully about the past
achievement (n.)something good you did after a lot of effort
cherish (v.)to hold something dear: I cherish those summers by the sea

5. Strong sample

Sample: 'Small Steps, Big Dreams'325 words · Strong answer: Creation 5/5 · Vocabulary 3/3 · Structure 7/7 · Grammar & punctuation 7/7 · Spelling 3/3
Task: Your school library is making a book called 'Who We Are', with one chapter written by every Year 6 pupil. Write your chapter: a short autobiography. Think about: • where you come from and your family • the people and things that matter most to you • your most unforgettable moment • the person you hope to become.

Small Steps, Big Dreams

About Myself

I was born on a rainy morning in March 2015 in Alexandria, a city where the sea is never far away. I live with my parents, my grandmother and my little sister, Nour, who is six and asks more questions than a teacher. Our flat is small, but it is always full of laughter and the smell of my grandmother's cooking. I go to a school near the Corniche, where my two best friends, Adam and Ziad, sit next to me in every lesson.

What Matters Most

The person who has influenced me most is my grandmother. Every evening, she tells me stories about her childhood, and she always ends with the same words: 'Be kind first, and clever second.' The object I treasure most is the blue notebook that she gave me on my ninth birthday. I fill it with drawings of buildings. If I am honest, I am frightened of deep water. That is a strange fear for someone from Alexandria!

My Most Unforgettable Moment

My most unforgettable moment happened last year at our school art competition. My hands were shaking as I handed in my drawing of a lighthouse. When the headteacher read out my name as the winner, I froze. I could not move. My heart was pounding like a drum. Then the whole hall started clapping, and I saw my grandmother wiping her eyes. At that moment, I realised that hard work really does pay off.

The Person I Hope to Become

I would describe myself as patient and creative, because I can spend hours on one drawing until it is perfect. However, I can be stubborn, especially when someone tries to change my ideas. My lifelong dream is to become an architect and design a library by the sea, where children like me can read, dream and draw. My story is only just beginning, and I cannot wait to see the next chapter.

Why this gets the marks
  • Word count: the figure counts the whole piece, including the title and the four sub-headings.
  • Creation of texts 5/5: all four bullets are covered and developed, one section each: family and where I come from (P1), people and things that matter: grandmother, notebook, a fear (P2), the unforgettable moment (P3), the person I hope to become (P4). The voice is personal and honest, and the notebook of drawings in P2 prepares the reader for the art competition in P3 and the dream of being an architect in P4.
  • Text-type features: title, sub-headings, first person all the way through, past tense for the story, present tense for 'now' (I live…, I would describe myself…), future dream at the end, remembered words in inverted commas, a key moment and an honest strength AND weakness with examples.
  • Vocabulary and language 3/3: precise, age-appropriate choices ('influenced', 'treasure', 'unforgettable', 'stubborn', 'lifelong dream') · a simile ('pounding like a drum') · humour ('asks more questions than a teacher', 'a strange fear for someone from Alexandria').
  • Structure of texts 7/7: the teacher's four-part structure with clear sub-headings · time phrases ('Every evening', 'last year', 'At that moment') · the last sentence ('the next chapter') links back to the idea of a book of stories and the title's 'big dreams'.
  • Grammar and punctuation 7/7: tenses controlled · varied sentences (short 'I could not move.' for drama after a longer sentence) · a colon before the grandmother's words · a pair of commas for extra information ('…my little sister, Nour, who is six…') · relative clauses with 'who', 'that' and 'where' · an exclamation mark used once for humour · apostrophes for possession ('grandmother's').
  • Word structure (spelling) 3/3: 'competition', 'headteacher', 'architect', 'influenced', 'creative', 'unforgettable' all correct.

6. Common mistakes

  • Writing a list of facts with no feelings or reflection. It sounds like a form, not a life story.
  • Trying to tell your whole life in tiny pieces instead of slowing down at ONE key moment.
  • Slipping into the third person ('Youssef was born…'). That turns it into a biography.
  • Mixing tenses without a reason. Use past for the story, present only for what is true now.
  • Skipping a bullet, often 'the person you hope to become'.
  • Describing only strengths. One honest weakness with an example shows maturity.
  • Ending suddenly ('That's all. The end.') instead of a reflective last sentence.
  • Using too much chatty language ('it was so cool', 'loads of') for a school book.
Autobiography checklist
  • Title + four sub-headings (optional but helpful).
  • First person from start to finish.
  • P1: birth, place, family, school, each with a detail.
  • P2: influential person, treasured object, a fear or a hobby, each explained.
  • P3: one unforgettable moment, slowed down, with feelings and a lesson.
  • P4: a strength and a weakness with examples + my lifelong dream.
  • Time connectives link the paragraphs. The ending links back to the start or the title.
  • Checked: tenses, full stops, commas, apostrophes, spelling of my ambitious words.
كيف تحصل على الدرجة النهائية في السيرة الذاتية

اكتب بضمير المتكلم (I) من البداية إلى النهاية، ولا تكتب قائمة معلومات فقط: كل معلومة تحتاج تفصيلة أو شعوراً. اتبع ترتيب الأستاذ: (1) عني وعن عائلتي (2) أهم الأشخاص والأشياء في حياتي (3) أكثر لحظة لا تُنسى، احكها ببطء مع مشاعرك والدرس الذي تعلمته (4) صفاتي (صفة قوة وصفة ضعف مع مثال) وحلمي في المستقبل. واربط الجملة الأخيرة بالعنوان أو بالبداية.

Practice task

  1. Practice: "A children's magazine is running a competition called 'My Life So Far'. Write a short autobiography for the competition. Consider: • your family and home • a hobby or interest that is important to you • a time you felt very proud • your dream for the future." Write a short plan.

    Show answer
    G.A.A.P.: autobiography (title, first person, sub-headings optional) · audience = magazine readers and judges → friendly but correct, little slang · 'Consider' → use at least 3 bullets, use all 4 · purpose = recount + reflect + entertain. Title: 'The Goalkeeper Who Never Gave Up'. P1 About me: born 2015 in Cairo, parents, older brother who taught me football, our balcony 'stadium'. P2 Hobby: goalkeeping · the old gloves my brother gave me · my fear of letting the team down. P3 Proud moment: the penalty I saved in the school final, slowed down: silence, the run-up, the dive, the cheer, what I learned (practice beats fear). P4 Me and my dream: strength (hard-working), weakness (impatient when we lose), dream: to coach a junior football team one day. Last line links back to 'never gave up'.

  2. Fix the weakness: "I was born in 2014. I have a brother. I like reading. I want to be a doctor."

    Show answer
    These are bare facts with no detail or feeling. A stronger version: "I was born in 2014 in Mansoura, a busy city on the Nile. My older brother, Ali, is my best friend and my worst critic. Reading has always been my escape: I finish a book every week. One day, I hope to become a doctor, because I want to help people the way a kind doctor once helped my grandfather."
