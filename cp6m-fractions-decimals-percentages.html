Fractions, Decimals and Percentages: Equivalence and Ordering

Change confidently between fractions, decimals and percentages, find percentages of shapes and amounts, and compare and order mixed numbers with =, > and <.

British & IGCSE › Checkpoint 6 › Checkpoint 6 Maths — Complete Course

In the curriculum
  • Know that a fraction, a decimal (with one or two decimal places) and a percentage can have the same value, e.g. 3/4 = 0.75 = 75% (6Nf.04)
  • Recognise percentages of shapes and of whole numbers: 1% and multiples of 5% up to 100%, e.g. 35% of a shape, 15% of 60 (6Nf.07)
  • Compare and order decimals (one or two decimal places), proper fractions with different denominators and percentages, using =, > and < (6Nf.08)

Fractions, decimals and percentages are three different ways of writing the same amount. Half a pizza is 1/2, 0.5 or 50% of it. If you can move quickly between the three forms, you can compare any two numbers, find percentages of amounts in your head and answer a lot of Checkpoint questions in seconds.

1. What does 'per cent' mean?

Per cent means 'out of 100'. If a square is cut into 100 equal small squares and 35 are shaded, then 35/100 of it is shaded. That is 35 hundredths, which as a decimal is 0.35, and as a percentage is 35%.

The whole shape is always 100%. So 100% = 1 = 100/100. The number line below shows the most important equivalents. Learn them by heart — Checkpoint expects you to know them without working.

Making the denominator 100

Ask: 'What do I multiply the denominator by to get 100?' Then multiply the numerator by the same number. Denominators that work easily: 2 (× 50), 4 (× 25), 5 (× 20), 10 (× 10), 20 (× 5), 25 (× 4), 50 (× 2). Example: 9/25 → × 4 → 36/100 = 0.36 = 36%.

3. Percentages of shapes

To find what percentage of a shape is shaded, the shape must be split into equal parts. Work out what percentage one part is (100% ÷ the number of parts), then multiply by the number of shaded parts.

Number of equal partsEach part is worthExample
1001%23 shaded → 23%
502%18 shaded → 36%
205%9 shaded → 45%
1010%7 shaded → 70%
520%3 shaded → 60%
425%3 shaded → 75%

You can also go the other way: 'Shade 35% of a shape with 20 squares.' Each square is 5%, and 35% ÷ 5% = 7, so shade 7 squares.

5. Comparing and ordering

To compare numbers written in different forms, change them all into the same form — usually decimals with two decimal places, or percentages. Then compare digit by digit from the left.

  1. Change every number to a decimal with two decimal places (write 0.6 as 0.60).
  2. Compare the tenths first, then the hundredths.
  3. Put them in order, then write the original numbers (not your decimals) in the answer.
  4. For two fractions, you can also use a common denominator: 3/4 and 7/10 → 15/20 and 14/20, so 3/4 > 7/10.
The signs =, > and <

The wide open side of the sign faces the bigger number: 0.75 > 0.7 (0.75 is greater than 0.7), 0.09 < 0.1 (0.09 is less than 0.1). Use = only when the values are exactly the same, e.g. 2/5 = 40%.

6. Worked examples

Worked example 1 — Write 0.35 (a) as a fraction in its simplest form (b) as a percentage. [2]

(a) 0.35 = 35 hundredths = 35/100. The HCF of 35 and 100 is 5: 35/100 = 7/20. (b) 0.35 × 100 = 35%. Common mistake: writing 35/10 or 3.5%.

Worked example 2 — A shape is made of 20 equal squares. 9 are shaded. What percentage is shaded? [1]

Each square = 100% ÷ 20 = 5%. 9 × 5% = 45%. (Check: 9/20 = 45/100.)

Worked example 3 — Find 35% of 80. [1]

10% of 80 = 8, so 30% = 24. 5% = half of 8 = 4. 35% = 24 + 4 = 28.

Worked example 4 — Find (a) 1% of 600 (b) 95% of 600. [2]

(a) 600 ÷ 100 = 6. (b) 95% = 100% − 5%. 10% of 600 = 60, so 5% = 30. 600 − 30 = 570. Common mistake: finding 95% as 9 × 10% + 5% and adding wrongly — subtracting from 100% is quicker and safer.

Worked example 5 — Write >, < or = in each box. [2] (a) 3/4 ☐ 0.7 (b) 2/5 ☐ 40% (c) 0.09 ☐ 0.1

  • (a) 3/4 = 0.75 and 0.7 = 0.70. 0.75 > 0.70, so 3/4 > 0.7.
  • (b) 2/5 = 4/10 = 40/100 = 40%, so 2/5 = 40%.
  • (c) 0.09 has 0 tenths; 0.1 = 0.10 has 1 tenth. So 0.09 < 0.1. Common mistake: thinking 0.09 is bigger because 9 > 1.
  • Mark scheme style: 2 marks for all three correct, 1 mark for two correct.

Worked example 6 — Which is bigger, 5/8 or 7/12? Show how you know. [1]

Find a common denominator: 24 is a multiple of both 8 and 12. 5/8 = 15/24 (× 3) and 7/12 = 14/24 (× 2). 15/24 > 14/24, so 5/8 is bigger (see the fraction bars above).

Worked example 7 — Write these numbers in order, starting with the smallest: 0.62 3/5 66% 0.58 16/25 [2]

Common mistakes
  • 'Longer decimals are bigger': 0.56 is NOT bigger than 0.6. Write 0.6 as 0.60 and compare tenths first.
  • 0.6 = 6% ✗. Multiply by 100: 0.6 = 60%. And 0.06 = 6%.
  • 5% = 0.5 ✗. Divide by 100: 5% = 0.05.
  • Comparing fractions by the denominator only: 1/8 looks 'bigger' because 8 > 4, but 1/8 < 1/4.
  • Counting shaded parts of a shape that is not split into equal parts, or forgetting how many parts the whole has.
  • In ordering questions, writing the converted decimals instead of the original numbers, or ordering largest first when the question says smallest first.
How Checkpoint asks this
  • Matching lines: join each fraction to the equal decimal or percentage.
  • Complete a table of equivalent fractions, decimals and percentages.
  • 'What percentage of the shape is shaded?' or 'Shade 45% of this shape' on a grid.
  • Find a percentage of an amount (often money or pupils), e.g. 15% of $60 — show 10% and 5% in your working.
  • Write >, < or = in a box, ring the largest/smallest, or order a mixed list. Change everything into decimals or percentages first and write your conversions next to the numbers.
  • 'Explain how you know' — show the common denominator or the conversions; a bare answer may not get the mark.

Practice

Remember
  • Per cent means out of 100: 35% = 35/100 = 0.35.
  • Know the key equivalents: 1/2 = 0.5 = 50%, 1/4 = 0.25 = 25%, 3/4 = 0.75 = 75%, 1/5 = 0.2 = 20%, 1/10 = 0.1 = 10%, 1/100 = 0.01 = 1%.
  • Percentages of amounts: build from 10% (÷ 10), 5% (half of 10%), 1% (÷ 100), 50% and 25%.
  • Percentage of a shape: 100% ÷ number of equal parts = value of one part.
  • To compare or order, change everything to the same form (two-decimal-place decimals or percentages), then compare tenths first.
