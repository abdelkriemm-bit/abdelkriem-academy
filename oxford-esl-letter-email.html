Question 2: Letters and Emails (How to Get 12/12)

The complete letter and email pack: formal and informal register, greetings and sign-offs, a paragraph plan, the tick strategy for compulsory points, and four full-mark models (Q2 letters and a Q3 email).

British & IGCSE › Oxford International GCSE English as a Second Language

Question 2 asks you to write a letter of 50-70 words that includes three compulsory points. It is worth 12 marks: 6 for Content and Communication and 6 for Language. This pack shows you how to get 12/12: when to be formal and when to be informal, how to open and close, how to cover every point quickly, and how to show the variety of language examiners reward. It also covers the email, which can appear as the form for Question 3 (longer, with more marks), so you know exactly what changes.

Letter or email: what really changes?
  • For marks, a letter and an email are written in the same way. The examiner does not reward layout, so the difference is only on the surface (an email may be a little more relaxed and may have a subject line).
  • What changes is the QUESTION NUMBER. In Q2 you write 50-70 words for 12 marks with 3 points. When an email is set as Q3, you write 70-90 words for 16 marks, usually with 4 bullets, and the top language band asks for a wide and effective variety.
  • Recent papers have set a letter in Q2 every time. If Q2 ever asks for an email instead, write it exactly like a Q2 letter: 50-70 words, 3 points, same 12 marks.

For an email set as Question 3, each grid has four levels (1-2, 3-4, 5-6, 7-8). To reach 7-8 for content you need an excellent, fully relevant answer with a wide range of detail and clear, coherent communication. To reach 7-8 for language you need accurate spelling and punctuation and a wide variety of effective vocabulary and structures. The blog and diary pack explains these bands in full.

How to get 12/12: checklist

  • Underline the reader. Parents, head teacher, officials = formal. Friend, cousin, family member = informal.
  • Number the three points in the question and tick each one as you write it.
  • Start the first point in your first or second sentence. No long "How are you?" chat.
  • Give a reason or example for the last point. It is usually a "why" point and it is where examiners look for detail.
  • Use at least 3 complex sentences (because, which, when, if, although, so that).
  • Use at least 2 tenses where they fit (e.g. present perfect + future, or past + present).
  • Vary your vocabulary. Do not repeat "good", "nice", "very" or "fun".
  • Keep one register from start to end: no slang in a formal letter, no stiff formal phrases to a friend.
  • Use a greeting and a sign-off that match the register (they earn no marks, but the wrong one weakens your sense of audience).
  • Stay within about 50-70 words. Around 60-68 gives room for detail without extra errors.
  • Check: capital letters, full stops, no comma splices, verb endings, spelling of the words from the question.

Layout rules that keep you safe
  • Greeting on ONE line: Hi + name + comma ("Hi Youssef,"). Do not put a comma after "Hi".
  • Sign-off + comma on one line, then your name on the line below. In an informal letter or email, write your first name only.
  • "Yours sincerely" and "Yours faithfully": capital Y, small s/f.
  • No address, no date. The task tells you not to write an address.
  • Remember: none of this earns marks by itself. Learn it once, use it quickly, and spend your time on the content.

Structure: paragraph by paragraph

Examiners have no single structure in mind, and you may combine points if that is more natural. But a clear order makes your letter easy to follow, which helps your content mark. Use this plan.

The tick strategy: covering every point

  1. Read the task and draw a box around the reader and the form (letter / email).
  2. Number the bullets ①②③ (④ in Q3).
  3. Next to each bullet, write 2-4 words of detail: a name, a time, a place, a reason.
  4. Find the "why" or opinion bullet. Plan a reason AND an example or result for it.
  5. Write, then put a small tick next to each bullet in the question as you finish it.
  6. Before you move on, check: are there ③ ticks (or ④ in Q3)? If one is missing, add one sentence now.
Watch the small bullets

Students rarely forget the big "why" bullet. They forget the small ones: the name of the event, where it is, when it happened. Examiners report that a missed short bullet (such as "where") is the most common reason a good answer drops out of the top band. One clear sentence is enough for a short bullet.

Get to the point quickly

Long opening (several sentences before any point)Quick opening for this exam (point 1 at once)
Hi Karim, How are you? How is your family? I hope you are all in good health. I am sorry I have not written for a long time because I was busy with school. (34 words, no points covered)Hi Karim, Guess what? I've just finished a four-week cooking course at our local youth centre! (point 1 covered in 16 words)
Dear Parents, I hope this letter finds you well. As you know, education is very important in this modern world. (no point covered)Dear Parents, We would like to invite you to our Science Fair on Thursday 4 May in the school hall. (when and where covered at once)
Cambridge 0510 or Oxford 9280? Know the difference

If you also study Cambridge IGCSE 0510, you learn an informal email opening with "How are you?", a question about your friend's well-being and an apology for not writing for so long with a reason. That layout is right for 0510, where the email is longer. In this Oxford exam the word range is much shorter (50-70 in Q2, 70-90 in Q3) and the Lead Examiner advises skipping long social openings, because they earn no marks. So here keep it to one short line at most ("How are you? Guess what?" or "Great news!") and reach the first bullet in your first or second sentence.

How to start

Formal openings

  • I am writing to invite you to...
  • I am writing on behalf of the student council to...
  • We are delighted to announce that...
  • I am writing to ask whether it would be possible to...
  • We would be grateful for your help with...

Informal openings

  • Guess what? I've just...
  • Great news! Our school is...
  • I've got something exciting to tell you.
  • I'm writing because I need your advice about...
  • You'll never believe what happened last weekend!

How to end

Formal closings

  • We very much hope that you will be able to attend.
  • Thank you in advance for your support.
  • I look forward to hearing from you.
  • Please do not hesitate to contact the school office if you have any questions.
  • Your help will make a real difference to our students.

Informal closings

  • Let me know if you can come!
  • Write back soon and tell me what you think.
  • Why don't you join us next time?
  • I can't wait to see you.
  • Give my regards to your family.

Useful phrases for common letter purposes

Inviting

  • We would like to invite you to...
  • It would be wonderful if you could come.
  • Do you fancy coming with me to...?
  • How about joining us on...?

Giving details (when / where / what)

  • The event will take place on... at...
  • It starts at 6 p.m. and finishes at about 9.
  • There will be...
  • It's being held in...

Giving reasons and benefits

  • This will allow students to...
  • ...which means that...
  • so that everyone can...
  • The main reason is that...
  • Not only will you..., but you will also...

Asking and requesting

  • I would be grateful if you could...
  • Would it be possible to...?
  • Could you possibly...?
  • Can you help me with...?

Advising

  • If I were you, I would...
  • You should definitely...
  • Why don't you...?
  • It might be a good idea to...

Telling news and feelings

  • I've just...
  • Since then, I have...
  • I was thrilled when...
  • It was exhausting but incredibly rewarding.

Model answers

Word counts below include the greeting, the sign-off and the name, because examiners read and credit every word you write. Each body paragraph is shown separately.

Model 1: Formal letter to parents (Q2)67 words · 12/12 (Content 6/6 + Language 6/6)
Task: Your school is collecting used books for a new school library. Write a letter to parents asking them to donate books. You must include: • when and where parents can bring the books • what kind of books the school needs • why the new library is important. Your letter should be between 50 and 70 words. Do not write an address.

Dear Parents,

Our school is opening a new library next term, and we would be grateful for your help.

Please bring any used books to the school hall between 9 and 13 March. We especially need English novels, science books and atlases in good condition.

The library will give every student a quiet place to read, which will improve their English.

Yours sincerely,

Ahmed Samir, Student Council

Why this gets the marks
  • Point 1 (when and where) ✓: "to the school hall between 9 and 13 March", in the second sentence of the letter.
  • Point 2 (what kind of books) ✓: "English novels, science books and atlases in good condition": specific, not just "books".
  • Point 3 (why it is important) ✓: a clear benefit with a result: "a quiet place to read, which will improve their English".
  • Content 6/6: all three points, everything relevant, clear purpose stated at once. No social chat.
  • Audience: polite formal register for parents ("we would be grateful", "Please bring", "Yours sincerely"). No contractions.
  • Language 6/6, variety: compound sentence ("..., and we would be grateful"), imperative ("Please bring"), relative clause ("which will improve"), future ("will give"), present continuous for a plan ("is opening"), precise vocabulary ("used books", "in good condition").
  • Language 6/6, accuracy: correct spelling and punctuation, commas in the list, capital letters for names and months.
Model 2: Formal letter to the head teacher (Q2)68 words · 12/12 (Content 6/6 + Language 6/6)
Task: Write a letter to your head teacher asking for a quiet study room to be opened after school. You must include: • why students need the room • when it should be open • how students will look after it. Your letter should be between 50 and 70 words. Do not write an address.

Dear Mr Fathy,

I am writing on behalf of Year 10 to ask whether a quiet study room could be opened after school. Many of us cannot concentrate at home because our flats are noisy.

The room would be open from 3 to 5 p.m., Sunday to Thursday.

In return, we promise to keep it tidy, and two prefects will lock it every evening.

Yours sincerely,

Yassin Adel

Why this gets the marks
  • Point 1 (why students need it) ✓: "Many of us cannot concentrate at home because our flats are noisy": a real reason, not just "we need it".
  • Point 2 (when) ✓: "from 3 to 5 p.m., Sunday to Thursday": precise.
  • Point 3 (how students will look after it) ✓: two concrete actions: keep it tidy, prefects lock it.
  • Content 6/6: purpose in the first sentence. All points covered with detail. Clear and convincing.
  • Audience: respectful formal tone for a head teacher ("I am writing on behalf of", "ask whether... could be opened", "In return").
  • Language 6/6: indirect question ("ask whether a quiet study room could be opened"), passive ("could be opened"), modal ("would be open"), cause ("because"), compound sentence and future ("will lock"). Varied, accurate and appropriate.
Model 3: Informal letter to a friend (Q2)65 words · 12/12 (Content 6/6 + Language 6/6)
Task: Write a letter to a friend about a cooking course you have just finished. You must include: • where the course took place • what you learned to cook • how the course has changed the way you eat. Your letter should be between 50 and 70 words. Do not write an address.

Dear Karim,

Guess what? I have just finished a four-week cooking course at our local youth centre, and I loved every minute!

We learned to make healthy meals such as lentil soup, grilled chicken with rice and colourful salads.

Since the course ended, I have stopped ordering fast food. Instead, I cook dinner twice a week, which gives me far more energy.

Best wishes,

Omar

Why this gets the marks
  • Point 1 (where) ✓: "at our local youth centre", in the first real sentence.
  • Point 2 (what you learned) ✓: three specific dishes, introduced with "such as".
  • Point 3 (how it changed the way you eat) ✓: before and after ("stopped ordering fast food" → "cook dinner twice a week") plus a result ("far more energy"). This is the detail examiners look for.
  • Content 6/6: friendly, relevant and clear. Only two words of social opening ("Guess what?").
  • Audience: natural informal tone (question, exclamation, "loved every minute"), but still accurate.
  • Language 6/6: present perfect ("have just finished", "have stopped"), past simple ("learned"), present simple for habits ("cook"), time clause ("Since the course ended"), relative clause ("which gives me"), linkers ("Instead"). Vocabulary is precise (grilled, colourful, energy).
Model 4: Informal email to a friend (Q3 form, 70-90 words, 16 marks)84 words · 16/16 (Content 8/8 + Language 8/8)
Task: Write an email to a friend about a day you spent volunteering at a beach clean-up. You must include: • when the clean-up took place • who organised it • what you did on the day • your opinion of the experience. Your email should be between 70 and 90 words.

Hi Youssef,

Last Friday, I spent the whole day cleaning a beach in Alexandria with forty other volunteers.

The event was organised by our school's environment club, which gave everyone gloves, bags and cold water.

First, we collected plastic bottles and old fishing nets from the sand. After lunch, we sorted the rubbish so that most of it could be recycled. By sunset, we had filled ninety bags!

Honestly, it was exhausting but incredibly rewarding. Why don't you join us next time?

Cheers,

Hassan

Why this gets the marks
  • Bullet 1 (when) ✓: "Last Friday", the very first words.
  • Bullet 2 (who organised it) ✓: "our school's environment club", with an extra detail about what they provided.
  • Bullet 3 (what you did) ✓: a clear sequence with time linkers (First, After lunch, By sunset) and specific actions.
  • Bullet 4 (opinion) ✓: a balanced opinion ("exhausting but incredibly rewarding") and an invitation that ends naturally.
  • Content 8/8: excellent, fully relevant, a wide range of detail (place, numbers, objects, results), clear and coherent from start to end.
  • Language 8/8: wide variety of structures: passive ("was organised", "could be recycled"), relative clause ("which gave everyone"), purpose clause ("so that"), past perfect ("had filled"), question to the reader. Effective vocabulary (rewarding, sorted, volunteers). Accurate spelling and punctuation, including the apostrophe in "school's".
  • Compare with Q2: the same skills, but 20 more words and a fourth bullet, so there is more room for detail and variety.

Why these models score full marks: the pattern

  • Point 1 is answered within the first two sentences.
  • Every point has at least one specific detail (a name, a number, a time, an example).
  • The "why" or opinion point has a reason AND a result.
  • There are at least three different complex structures (because, which, so that, since, whether).
  • There are at least two different tenses, each used correctly.
  • The tone fits the reader from the first word to the last.
  • There is no social chat and no memorised filler.

Practice

  1. Practice task 1 (Q2, formal): Your school needs volunteers for its sports day. Write a letter to parents asking for their help. You must include: • what jobs the volunteers will do • when and where parents should arrive • why their help is important. 50-70 words. Make a plan.

    Show answer
    Register: formal (parents). Plan: ① serve water, keep scores, help younger pupils find their races ② 8 a.m. on Thursday 20 April, at the main gate, for a short briefing ③ teachers cannot run twelve events alone. Parents' help keeps the day safe and on time. Opening: "We are looking for parent volunteers to help at our Sports Day on Thursday 20 April." Closing: "Thank you in advance for your support." Then "Yours sincerely," on one line and your full name on the next.

  2. Practice task 2 (Q2, informal): Write a letter to your cousin advising him how to settle into a new school. You must include: • how to make new friends • what to do if lessons are difficult • why it will get easier. 50-70 words. Make a plan.

    Show answer
    Register: informal. Plan: ① join a club (football, chess). Sit with different people at break ② ask the teacher after class, study with a classmate ③ everyone feels lost at first. After a month you will know the routines and people. Useful structures: "If I were you, I would...", "Why don't you...?", "Once you..., you will...". Sign-off: "Best wishes," then your first name on the next line.

  3. Practice task 3 (Q3 email): Write an email to a friend about a new phone app you have started using. You must include: • the name of the app • how you found out about it • what you use it for • why you would recommend it. 70-90 words. Make a plan.

    Show answer
    Plan: ① WordWise (an invented vocabulary app) ② my English teacher showed it in class last month ③ I learn ten new words every morning on the bus. Quizzes and a daily streak ④ free, takes only ten minutes, my reading test mark went up from 14 to 18 → recommend. Include a past tense, present habit, present perfect ("I have learned over 300 words"), a relative clause and a question to your friend.

  4. Which sign-off goes with "Dear Sir or Madam,"?

    Show answer
    Yours faithfully, followed by your full name.

  5. Rewrite in a formal register: "Hey parents! U should totally come to our play, it's gonna be awesome."

    Show answer
    Dear Parents, We would like to invite you to our school play. We are sure that you will enjoy it.

  6. A student covers all three points but writes: "Come to the event. It is good. It is fun. It is nice." What will hold the mark down?

    Show answer
    Content: the reasons have no detail, so it may not reach 5-6. Language: vocabulary and structures are limited and repetitive (Level 1-2). Fix it with specific reasons and complex sentences: "You should come because the money we raise will buy books for a village school."

  7. If Q2 asked for an email instead of a letter, what would change?

    Show answer
    Almost nothing: still 50-70 words, 3 compulsory points, 12 marks. You may use a more email-style greeting and sign-off, but layout earns no marks.
