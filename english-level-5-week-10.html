Grammar: Collocations with make / do / take / have · common idioms

Collocations are words that naturally go together. English uses make, do, take and have in fixed combinations that must be learned as chunks. Idioms are fixed expressions whose meaning is not literal.

المتعلم العربي يترجم «يعمل» إلى do أو make بشكل عشوائي: do a mistake ✗ (الصحيح make a mistake)، make research ✗ (الصحيح do research)، make a photo ✗ (الصحيح take a photo)، do a shower ✗ (الصحيح have / take a shower). القاعدة التقريبية: make = نُنتج أو نصنع شيئًا جديدًا (make a cake, make a plan, make a noise) · do = نؤدّي نشاطًا أو عملًا أو واجبًا (do homework, do business, do the shopping) · take = نأخذ أو نقوم بخطوة (take a break, take a risk) · have = نعيش تجربة أو نتناول شيئًا (have fun, have a meal). لكن الأفضل أن تحفظها كمجموعات جاهزة. أما التعبيرات الاصطلاحية (idioms) فلا تُترجم كلمة بكلمة، ولا تُكثر منها في الكتابة الرسمية.

Idioms are informal: use them in speaking, stories and informal emails, not in formal essays.

Learn the whole chunk: take something for granted (not take it as granted). Both make a decision and take a decision are used in British English; make is more common.

Keep a collocation notebook: write the verb + noun and an example sentence about your own life.

Exercises

Exercise 1 · Complete with the correct form of make, do, take or have.

  1. 1) Could you ___ me a favour and post this letter?
  2. 2) I ___ a terrible mistake in the exam yesterday.
  3. 3) Don't ___ your health for granted.
  4. 4) Let me ___ a look at your drawing.
  5. 5) The new library will ___ a big difference to the town.
  6. 6) More than two hundred artists ___ part in last year's festival.
  7. 7) We need to ___ more research before we decide.
  8. 8) The film ___ a huge impact on young viewers when it came out.
Show the answers
1) do 2) made 3) take 4) have / take 5) make 6) took 7) do 8) had / made

Exercise 2 · Match each idiom with its meaning: a) very rarely · b) receiving a lot of public attention · c) very easy · d) say exactly the right thing · e) make people feel relaxed at the start · f) something extra that makes a good thing even better.

  1. 1) a piece of cake
  2. 2) break the ice
  3. 3) once in a blue moon
  4. 4) hit the nail on the head
  5. 5) in the spotlight
  6. 6) the icing on the cake
Show the answers
1) c 2) e 3) a 4) d 5) b 6) f

Exercise 3 · Correct the mistake.

  1. 1) I did a big mistake.
  2. 2) She made her homework after dinner.
  3. 3) Can I do a photo of your painting?
  4. 4) The exam was a piece of cheese.
  5. 5) He took part to the competition.
  6. 6) It doesn't do sense.

Exercise 4 · Translate into English.

  1. 1) بذلت قصارى جهدي في المسابقة.
  2. 2) لا أفهم هذه اللوحة؛ إنها غير منطقية بالنسبة لي.
  3. 3) هل تريد أن تجرّب الرسم بالألوان المائية؟

Show the answers

Do each exercise in pencil first, then open the answers to check.

Write & Speak

Write & Speak — Write a review (200–250 words) of an exhibition, concert, play or film you have seen recently. Use at least six make / do / take / have collocations and two idioms (in the less formal parts). Then record your review as a 2-minute video.

Example: A night of colour in Downtown Cairo I go to art exhibitions once in a blue moon, so when a friend invited me to “Walls That Speak”, a new street-art exhibition in Downtown Cairo, I nearly said no. I'm very glad I had second thoughts. The exhibition takes place in an old cinema that has been empty for years. More than forty young Egyptian artists have taken part, each painting a wall, a door or even a ceiling. The theme is the city itself: its noise, its traffic, its people and its history. What made the strongest impression on me was a huge mural of a Cairo microbus full of passengers from different centuries: a pharaoh, a Mamluk soldier and a teenager with headphones, all squeezed together. It is funny, but it also makes you think about how the past and the present live side by side. The organisers have clearly made an effort to make art accessible. Entry is free, the descriptions are in Arabic and English, and on Fridays you can have a go at painting a small section of a “community wall”. I tried it, and it was definitely not a piece of cake! If I had to make one criticism, it would be the lighting, which is too dark in the back rooms, so it is hard to take good photos. Overall, the exhibition does a great job of showing that art does not belong only in museums. Don't miss it: it runs until the end of next month.
