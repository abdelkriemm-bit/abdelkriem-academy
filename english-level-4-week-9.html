Grammar: Contrast and purpose — despite, in spite of, whereas, so that, in order to

اقرا الشرح بالعربي الأول، وبعدين بص على الجدول والأمثلة. لو مش فاهم، اسمع درس الجرامر اللي في خطة الأسبوع.

Contrast: despite / in spite of + noun or -ing · although / even though + subject + verb · whereas / while compare two different facts.

Purpose: to / in order to / so as to + verb · so that + subject + can / could / will / would.

كلمة «رغم» في العربية يأتي بعدها اسم أو جملة، أما في الإنجليزية فلكل كلمة قاعدتها: بعد despite و in spite of يأتي اسم أو فعل بـ ing فقط (Despite the rain / Despite being tired)، ولا يأتي بعدهما فاعل وفعل مباشرة. فالخطأ الشائع Despite it was raining، والصحيح Although it was raining أو Despite the fact that it was raining. ولا نقول despite of أبدًا. وللتعبير عن الهدف لا تقل for to learn أو for learn، بل قل to learn أو in order to learn، وبعد so that نضع جملة كاملة: so that I can learn.

LinkerFollowed byExample
despite / in spite ofnoun or -ingDespite the traffic, we arrived on time. In spite of being ill, she went to school.
despite the fact thatsubject + verbDespite the fact that it was expensive, he bought it.
although / even thoughsubject + verbAlthough he studied hard, he failed.
whereas / whilesubject + verb (two different facts)My brother loves maths, whereas I love languages.
in order to / so as toverb (base form)I got up early in order to revise.
so thatsubject + can / could / will / wouldI left early so that I could catch the bus.

Wrong: Despite of the rain … ✔ Despite the rain … / In spite of the rain …

Wrong: I went to Cairo for study. ✔ I went to Cairo to study. (for + noun: I went to Cairo for an interview.)

Negative purpose: in order not to / so as not to + verb, or so that … won't / wouldn't.

Exercises · تمارين

حل كل تمرين في يومه بالقلم الرصاص، وبعدين دوس «Show the answers» وصحّح لنفسك. اكتب أخطاءك في الكشكول وراجعها في اليوم السادس.

Exercise 1 · Choose the correct linker.

  1. 1) (Despite / Although) the bad weather, the school trip went ahead.
  2. 2) (In spite of / Even though) she was tired, she kept revising.
  3. 3) Public schools are free, (whereas / despite) private schools are expensive.
  4. 4) He joined a course (so that / in order to) improve his English.
  5. 5) Speak slowly (so that / so as to) everyone can understand.
  6. 6) (In spite of / Although) having a scholarship, he worked part-time.
Show the answers
1) Despite 2) Even though 3) whereas 4) in order to 5) so that 6) In spite of

Exercise 2 · Join the sentences. Use the word in brackets.

  1. 1) It was raining. We played football. (despite)
  2. 2) She studied hard. She wanted to get a scholarship. (in order to)
  3. 3) I live in Giza. My brother lives in Alexandria. (whereas)
  4. 4) He saved money. He could pay the tuition fees. (so that)
  5. 5) Omar was ill. He sat the exam. (in spite of)
  6. 6) I set two alarms. I didn't want to oversleep. (so as not to)
Show the answers
1) Despite the rain, we played football. / Despite the fact that it was raining, we played football. 2) She studied hard in order to get a scholarship. 3) I live in Giza, whereas my brother lives in Alexandria. 4) He saved money so that he could pay the tuition fees. 5) In spite of being ill, Omar sat the exam. / In spite of the fact that he was ill, Omar sat the exam. 6) I set two alarms so as not to oversleep.

Exercise 3 · Correct the mistake.

  1. 1) Despite of the noise, I finished my homework.
  2. 2) Although the high fees, they sent their son abroad.
  3. 3) I went to the library for study.
  4. 4) She took notes so that remember the lesson.
  5. 5) In spite of he was nervous, he gave a great presentation.
  6. 6) I whispered so as not wake the baby.
Show the answers
1) Despite the noise, I finished my homework. 2) Despite (In spite of) the high fees, they sent their son abroad. 3) I went to the library to study. 4) She took notes so that she could remember the lesson. / …in order to remember the lesson. 5) In spite of being nervous, … / Although he was nervous, … 6) I whispered so as not to wake the baby.

Exercise 4 · Translate into English.

  1. 1) على الرغم من المطر، ذهبنا إلى الجامعة.
  2. 2) أذاكر كل يوم لكي أنجح بتفوق.
  3. 3) أخي يحب العلوم، بينما أحب أنا اللغات.
  4. 4) غادرت مبكرًا حتى لا أتأخر.
  5. 5) رغم أنها أخذت دروسًا خصوصية، رسبت.
Show the answers
1) Despite the rain, we went to university. / In spite of the rain, we went to (the) university. 2) I study every day in order to pass with flying colours. / I study every day so that I can pass with flying colours. 3) My brother loves science, whereas I love languages. / My brother loves science, while I love languages. 4) I left early so as not to be late. / I left early in order not to be late. / I left early so that I wouldn't be late. 5) Even though (Although) she had private lessons, she failed.

Do each exercise in pencil first, then open the answers to check.

Write & Speak · اكتب واتكلم

اكتب الموضوع في الكشكول بنفسك، وبص على المثال لو احتجت، وبعدين اقراه بصوت عالي أو سجّل نفسك.

Write & Speak — “Are private lessons necessary?”: write an opinion essay of 150–200 words. Compare the two sides with whereas and despite / in spite of, and explain purposes with in order to and so that. Then give your opinion aloud in two minutes.

Example: Private lessons have become a normal part of education in Egypt. Many families spend a large part of their income on them, despite the fact that public schools are free. In my opinion, private lessons can be useful, but they are not always necessary. Supporters say that students need extra help in order to understand difficult subjects and get high grades in Thanaweya Amma. Teachers in private lessons explain slowly so that every student can ask questions, whereas classes at school often have fifty students. Some students also feel more confident in small groups. However, in spite of these advantages, private lessons have serious problems. They are expensive, and they take up students' evenings, so many teenagers have no time for sport, reading or rest. Even though they spend hours with tutors, a lot of students only memorise answers instead of developing critical thinking. To sum up, I believe private lessons should be the exception, not the rule. Schools should improve so that families don't need to pay twice for their children's education.
