Dividing Whole Numbers (up to 1000 ÷ 2-digit)

Estimate and divide whole numbers up to 1000 by 1-digit and 2-digit numbers using short division, chunking and long division, and decide what to do with remainders.

British & IGCSE › Checkpoint 6 › Checkpoint 6 Maths — Complete Course

In the curriculum
  • Estimate the answer to a division before you calculate it, and use the estimate to check (6Ni.05).
  • Divide whole numbers up to 1000 by a 1-digit number (for example 935 ÷ 4) or a 2-digit number (for example 989 ÷ 23), and deal with remainders sensibly in word problems (6Ni.05).

Division means sharing equally or grouping. It is the inverse (opposite) of multiplication: if 142 × 6 = 852, then 852 ÷ 6 = 142. On this page you will learn to estimate, to use short division ("bus stop"), chunking and long division, and to decide what to do with a remainder in a real problem.

Remember: dividend ÷ divisor = quotient remainder, and the check is quotient × divisor + remainder = dividend.

1. Estimate first: use friendly numbers

For division do not just round to the nearest hundred. Change the dividend to a nearby number that divides easily because you know a multiplication fact. These are called compatible numbers.

CalculationFriendly numbersFact usedEstimate
852 ÷ 6840 ÷ 684 ÷ 6 = 14≈ 140
618 ÷ 8640 ÷ 864 ÷ 8 = 8≈ 80
935 ÷ 41 000 ÷ 4a quarter of 1 000≈ 250 (a bit less)
768 ÷ 24750 ÷ 254 × 25 = 100, so 750 ÷ 25 = 30≈ 30
989 ÷ 231 000 ÷ 251 000 ÷ 25 = 40≈ 40
460 ÷ 52450 ÷ 5045 ÷ 5 = 9≈ 9

You can also find which two tens the answer lies between: for 768 ÷ 24, 24 × 30 = 720 and 24 × 40 = 960, so the answer is between 30 and 40 (and close to 30).

2. Short division (÷ a 1-digit number)

  1. Write the dividend inside the "bus stop" and the divisor outside on the left.
  2. Start at the left (the biggest place value). How many 6s in 8? 1, remainder 2. Write 1 on top.
  3. Carry the remainder 2 to the next digit: it makes 25. How many 6s in 25? 4, remainder 1.
  4. Carry the 1: it makes 12. How many 6s in 12? 2, remainder 0.
  5. Answer 142. Check: 142 × 6 = 852 ✓ (and 142 is close to the estimate 140).

In 935 ÷ 4: 9 ÷ 4 = 2 r 1 → 13 ÷ 4 = 3 r 1 → 15 ÷ 4 = 3 r 3. Nothing is left to carry into, so the answer is 233 r 3. Check: 233 × 4 + 3 = 932 + 3 = 935 ✓

Two special cases. (1) If the first digit is smaller than the divisor, look at the first two digits: 315 ÷ 7 → 3 ÷ 7 = 0, so work out 31 ÷ 7 = 4 r 3, then 35 ÷ 7 = 5. Answer 45. (2) If a step gives 0, you must write the 0 in the answer: 624 ÷ 6 → 6 ÷ 6 = 1, 2 ÷ 6 = 0 r 2, 24 ÷ 6 = 4. Answer 104, not 14.

3. Dividing by a 2-digit number: chunking

Chunking means taking away big, easy chunks (multiples of the divisor) until nothing (or only a remainder) is left. Then add up how many lots you took. Start by writing a few easy multiples of the divisor: 1×, 2×, 5×, 10×, 20×.

Easy multiples of 24
1 × 24 = 242 × 24 = 485 × 24 = 120
10 × 24 = 24020 × 24 = 48030 × 24 = 720
  • 768 − 480 (20 lots) = 288
  • 288 − 240 (10 lots) = 48
  • 48 − 48 (2 lots) = 0
  • Total: 20 + 10 + 2 = 32, so 768 ÷ 24 = 32. Check: 32 × 24 = 768 ✓

Bigger chunks = fewer steps. Using 30 × 24 = 720 straight away: 768 − 720 = 48 = 2 × 24, so the answer is 30 + 2 = 32.

4. Long division (÷ a 2-digit number)

Long division is the written method for chunking. Each step has the same four parts: Divide, Multiply, Subtract, Bring down (remember "Does My Sister Bake?").

  1. 989 ÷ 23. 23 does not go into 9, so use 98. Divide: 23 × 4 = 92 and 23 × 5 = 115 (too big), so write 4 above the 8.
  2. Multiply: 4 × 23 = 92. Subtract: 98 − 92 = 6.
  3. Bring down the next digit 9 to make 69.
  4. Divide: 23 × 3 = 69, so write 3 above the 9. Multiply and subtract: 69 − 69 = 0.
  5. No digits left. Answer 43, remainder 0. Check: 43 × 23 = 989 ✓ and 43 is close to the estimate 40.

When the division is not exact, the last subtraction leaves a remainder. The remainder must always be smaller than the divisor — if it is not, your quotient digit is too small.

Short division by a 2-digit number works too if you list multiples first. For 864 ÷ 16: list 16, 32, 48, 64, 80, 96. 86 ÷ 16 = 5 r 6 (5 × 16 = 80), then 64 ÷ 16 = 4. Answer 54.

5. Remainders in real life

In a word problem you must decide what the remainder means. Read the question again before you write the answer.

SituationExampleCalculationAnswer
Round up — everyone/everything must fit460 students, 52 seats on each bus. How many buses?460 ÷ 52 = 8 r 449 buses (8 buses leave 44 students behind)
Round down — only complete groups countMona has 500 pounds. A book costs 35 pounds. How many books can she buy?500 ÷ 35 = 14 r 1014 books (10 pounds is not enough for another)
The remainder is the answer900 cups packed in boxes of 24. How many cups are left over?900 ÷ 24 = 37 r 1212 cups
Share the remainder as a fraction7 pizzas shared equally by 4 children7 ÷ 4 = 1 r 31 3/4 pizzas each (the remainder 3 out of 4)

Worked examples

Worked example 1 (1 mark) — Ring the best estimate for 618 ÷ 8. 7 60 80 700

640 is close to 618 and 64 ÷ 8 = 8, so 640 ÷ 8 = 80. Answer: 80. (Exact: 77 r 2.) Common mistake: 700 — that is the dividend rounded, not the quotient.

Worked example 2 (1 mark) — Calculate 624 ÷ 6.

6 ÷ 6 = 1. 2 ÷ 6 = 0 remainder 2 → write 0, carry 2. 24 ÷ 6 = 4. Answer: 104. Check: 104 × 6 = 624 ✓. Common mistake: writing 14. Estimate 600 ÷ 6 = 100 shows that 14 is far too small.

Worked example 3 (2 marks) — Calculate 816 ÷ 34. Show your working.

  • Estimate: 800 ÷ 40 = 20 (or 816 is about 850 = 25 × 34), so the answer is between 20 and 25.
  • Chunk: 20 × 34 = 680. 816 − 680 = 136.
  • Multiples: 2 × 34 = 68, 4 × 34 = 136. 136 − 136 = 0.
  • 20 + 4 = 24. Check: 24 × 34 = 816 ✓

Worked example 4 (1 mark) — 460 students go on a school trip. Each bus has 52 seats. How many buses are needed?

460 ÷ 52: 52 × 8 = 416, 52 × 9 = 468 (too big). 460 − 416 = 44, so 460 ÷ 52 = 8 r 44. The 44 students still need a bus, so round up. Answer: 9 buses. Common mistake: writing "8 r 44" or "8" — a remainder is never the final answer to a "how many buses/boxes are needed" question.

Worked example 5 (1 mark) — When a number is divided by 15 the answer is 42 remainder 7. What is the number?

Use the inverse: number = quotient × divisor + remainder = 42 × 15 + 7 = 630 + 7 = 637. Check: 637 ÷ 15 → 15 × 42 = 630, remainder 7 ✓

Worked example 6 (2 marks) — 932 ÷ 7. Give your answer with a remainder, then explain how to check it.

9 ÷ 7 = 1 r 2 → 23 ÷ 7 = 3 r 2 → 22 ÷ 7 = 3 r 1. Answer: 133 r 1. Check: 133 × 7 + 1 = 931 + 1 = 932 ✓

Common mistakes
  • Missing a 0 in the quotient (624 ÷ 6 = 104, not 14; 972 ÷ 9 = 108, not 18).
  • Starting from the right as in multiplication — division starts from the left.
  • A remainder bigger than (or equal to) the divisor — your quotient digit was too small.
  • Forgetting to carry the remainder to the next digit in short division.
  • Writing "8 r 44 buses" — in context, round up or down and give a whole-number answer with units.
  • Mixing up dividend and divisor: 52 ÷ 460 is not the same as 460 ÷ 52.
  • Not checking with the inverse: quotient × divisor + remainder should give the dividend.
How Checkpoint asks this
  • "Calculate 816 ÷ 34" with a working space — show chunks or long division; working can earn a method mark.
  • "Ring the best estimate" — choose friendly numbers from a known fact.
  • Word problems with remainders: "How many boxes are needed?" (round up), "How many can she buy?" (round down), "How many are left over?" (the remainder).
  • Missing digits in a written division — use the inverse and work digit by digit.
  • "A number is divided by … the answer is … remainder …. Find the number." — multiply, then add the remainder.
  • "Tick the division that has a remainder" — check quickly with tests of divisibility (see the Factors page).
  • On Paper 2 (calculator) a remainder shows as a decimal: 460 ÷ 52 = 8.846… — you still have to decide whether to round up or down.

Practice

Remember
  • Estimate with friendly numbers from a known fact (618 ÷ 8 ≈ 640 ÷ 8 = 80).
  • Short division starts from the left; carry each remainder to the next digit; write any 0 in the answer.
  • For a 2-digit divisor, list easy multiples (×1, ×2, ×5, ×10, ×20) and chunk, or use long division: Divide, Multiply, Subtract, Bring down.
  • The remainder is always smaller than the divisor.
  • In word problems decide: round up, round down, or the remainder is the answer.
  • Check: quotient × divisor + remainder = dividend.
