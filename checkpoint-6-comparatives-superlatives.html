Comparatives and Superlatives

How to form and spell comparatives and superlatives, as...as, irregular forms, fewer or less, and why writers use them in persuasive texts, adverts and reviews.

British & IGCSE › Checkpoint 6 › Checkpoint 6 English (Cambridge Primary 0058)

1. Comparing things

A comparative compares two things (taller than). A superlative compares three or more and shows the top or bottom (the tallest).

  • Comparative: The Nile is longer than the Thames.
  • Superlative: The Nile is the longest river in Africa.

2. How to make them

AdjectiveRuleComparativeSuperlative
short (one syllable): tall, fastadd -er / -esttaller, fasterthe tallest, the fastest
ends in -e: large, safeadd -r / -stlarger, saferthe largest, the safest
one vowel + one consonant: big, hot, thindouble the last letterbigger, hotter, thinnerthe biggest, the hottest, the thinnest
ends in -y: happy, busy, easychange y to ihappier, busier, easierthe happiest, the busiest, the easiest
long (most two syllables, all three or more): careful, beautiful, dangerousmore / mostmore careful, more dangerousthe most careful, the most dangerous
irregular: good, bad, farlearn thembetter, worse, further/fartherthe best, the worst, the furthest/farthest
little / much / manylearn themless, morethe least, the most
Remember the little words

Comparatives usually need than: bigger than a bus. Superlatives usually need the: the biggest animal.

3. Other ways to compare

  • With as ... as (the same): My bag is as heavy as yours.
  • With not as ... as (less): The film was not as exciting as the book.
  • Going down: This puzzle is less difficult than the last one. / the least expensive ticket
  • Adverbs too: She ran faster than me. / He spoke more quietly than before.
  • Getting stronger: The storm grew stronger and stronger. / It became more and more frightening.

4. Fewer or less? Many or much?

Countable (you can count them)Uncountable (you cannot count them)
fewer cars, fewer mistakesless water, less time
many books, many peoplemuch noise, much money
the fewest pointsthe least sugar

5. Common mistakes

Never double them
  • more better ✗ → better ✓
  • the most fastest ✗ → the fastest ✓
  • more easier ✗ → easier ✓
  • badder ✗ → worse ✓
  • gooder / the goodest ✗ → better / the best ✓
  • bigger then ✗ → bigger than ✓
  • biger / hotest / happyer ✗ → bigger / hottest / happier ✓ (spelling rules)

6. How Checkpoint asks about it

Reading questions in this style
  • Find and copy a comparative adjective from the text. (for example bigger, more dangerous)
  • Find a superlative that the writer uses to persuade the reader.
  • Why does the writer use the superlative “the most exciting”? → to make the place or product sound better than all the others and persuade the reader.

Why do writers use comparatives and superlatives? To compare things clearly, to show which is best or worst, to make something sound better than anything else in persuasive texts and adverts (the most exciting ride in Egypt), and to give a clear judgement in reviews.

In your writing:

Text typeExample
Leaflet or advertVisit the biggest water park in Hurghada, with longer slides than ever before!
ReviewThe second half of the book is more gripping than the first.
Balanced argumentWalking to school is cheaper and healthier than going by car, but it is slower.
Information textThe cheetah is the fastest land animal in the world.
StoryIt was the darkest night of the year.
نصيحة سريعة
  • الكلمة القصيرة تاخد er أو est، والطويلة تاخد more أو most. ما تجمعش الاتنين: more better غلط.
  • المقارنة بين حاجتين = er + than. الأفضل من الكل = the + est.
  • fewer مع الحاجات اللي بتتعد (fewer cars)، و less مع اللي ما بتتعدش (less water).

7. Practice

  1. Correct: This is the most best day of my life.

    Show answer
    This is the best day of my life.

  2. Write the comparative of busy and the superlative of thin.

    Show answer
    busier and the thinnest.

  3. Correct: There is less people here than yesterday.

    Show answer
    There are fewer people here than yesterday. (people are countable and plural)

  4. Complete: A plane is ______ (fast) than a train.

    Show answer
    faster

  5. Complete: That was ______ (bad) film I have ever seen.

    Show answer
    the worst

  6. Complete: Science is ______ (interesting) than history for me.

    Show answer
    more interesting

  7. Correct: My brother is more taller then me.

    Show answer
    My brother is taller than me.

  8. Rewrite with not as ... as: Alex is faster than Tarek.

    Show answer
    Tarek is not as fast as Alex.

  9. Why might a leaflet say the friendliest hotel on the coast?

    Show answer
    The superlative makes the hotel sound better than every other hotel, which persuades the reader to choose it.

  10. Choose: There is (fewer / less) traffic on Fridays.

    Show answer
    less: traffic is uncountable.
