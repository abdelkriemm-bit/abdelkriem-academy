Pronouns

Match every pronoun to its antecedent, choose the right form (who/whom, I/me), avoid unclear pronouns and never confuse its/it's, their/they're/there or your/you're.

American Diploma › Digital SAT Reading and Writing — Complete Course

A pronoun (it, they, its, their, who, him …) stands in for a noun. On the Digital SAT, pronoun questions check three things: that the pronoun matches the noun it replaces, that it is in the right form (subject, object or possessive), and that the reader can tell which noun it means. The rules are few and fixed, so this is one of the easiest places on the test to gain points quickly.

What this question type tests

Pronoun questions belong to Standard English Conventions, which is about 26% of the scored Reading and Writing questions (about 11–15 questions). The official skill list for Form, Structure, and Sense includes pronoun–antecedent agreement and the confusing pairs its/it's, their/they're/there and your/you're. These questions usually come in the later part of each module, after Information and Ideas.

The stem is always: "Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?" You know it is a pronoun question when the four choices are pronouns or pronoun look-alikes, for example:

  • its / it's / their / they're
  • who / whom / which / whose
  • it / they / this / the device
  • I / me / myself / us

The key word is antecedent: the noun a pronoun refers back to. In "The museum reopened its doors", the antecedent of *its* is *museum*. Almost every pronoun question is solved by finding the antecedent first.

أول خطوة دايمًا

قبل ما تختار الضمير، دوّر على الاسم اللي بيرجع له (الـ antecedent). لو الاسم مفرد (شركة، جامعة، فريق، حيوان واحد) يبقى it / its. لو جمع يبقى they / their. الشركة مش they حتى لو فيها آلاف الموظفين.

Rule 1: Pronoun–antecedent agreement (number)

A singular antecedent takes a singular pronoun; a plural antecedent takes a plural pronoun. The test makes this hard in two ways: it puts the antecedent far from the pronoun, and it uses nouns that feel plural but are grammatically singular.

AntecedentPronoun✓ Correct✗ Wrong
One company, university, museum, government, team, committee, organization, city, countryit / itsThe company moved its factory to Sadat City.The company moved their factory.
A species or one animal described in general (the fennec fox, the honeybee)it / itsThe fennec fox uses its large ears to lose heat.The fennec fox uses their large ears.
Plural nouns (scientists, turtles, the pyramids)they / them / theirThe turtles return to their home beach.The turtles return to its home beach.
each, every, either, neither, one + of + pluralit / its (for things)Each of the bridges has its own toll.Each of the bridges has their own toll.
A and Bthey / theirCairo and Giza share their water supply.Cairo and Giza share its water supply.
British school students: groups

British English often says "The team have won their match." The SAT follows American Standard English: a team, company, university, government or band is one thing, so it takes it/its — "The team has won its match." Look for clues in the passage: if another sentence already says "its", keep "its".

Rule 2: Clear antecedents — no confusing pronouns

A pronoun is wrong if the reader cannot be sure which noun it means. This happens when two or more nouns of the same number come before the pronoun. On the test, the correct choice is often a specific noun instead of a pronoun.

✗ UnclearWhy✓ Clear
When the scientist showed the robot to the engineer, she was impressed.She = scientist or engineer?When the scientist showed the robot to the engineer, the engineer was impressed.
The new lamps replaced the old ones, and they use less electricity.They = new lamps or old ones?… and the new lamps use less electricity.
The report compared the two dams. This surprised the government.This = the report? the comparison? a result?This comparison surprised the government.

How to decide: list every noun before the pronoun that has the same number. If there is only one, the pronoun is fine. If there are two or more, choose the choice that names the noun. Do not choose the noun version when the pronoun is already clear — the SAT does not reward extra words for no reason.

Rule 3: Subject, object and possessive forms (case)

Subject form (does the action)Object form (receives the action / after a preposition)Possessive (no apostrophe!)
Imemy / mine
hehimhis
sheherher / hers
weusour / ours
theythemtheir / theirs
whowhomwhose
ititits
  • Two people trick: cover the other person. "Salma and (I / me) won" → "I won" → Salma and I won. "The coach thanked Salma and (I / me)" → "thanked me" → Salma and me.
  • After a preposition (between, for, with, to, from, among, like) use the object form: between you and me, for him and her.
  • Comparisons with than / as: finish the sentence in your head: "She runs faster than (he / him)" → "than he runs" → than he (or "than he does").
  • Myself / himself (reflexive) only when the subject does the action to itself or for emphasis: "I taught myself." ✗ "Karim and myself presented." Words like *hisself* and *theirselves* do not exist.

Rule 4: Who, whom, which, that, whose

WordUse✓ Example
whoa person, as the subject of its clause (he/she/they)The poet who wrote the anthem … (she wrote it)
whoma person, as the object of its clause (him/her/them), or after a prepositionThe poet whom the committee honored … (honored her) / the poet to whom it was dedicated
whichthings and animals, never peopleThe bridge, which opened in 2019, …
thatpeople or things, in essential clauses (no commas)The tool that they used …
whosepossession, for people or thingsa city whose streets flood easily

The he/him test: take the words after the blank and make a small sentence with he or him. If he fits, use who; if him fits, use whom. Note the m: him = whom.

who ولا whom؟

جرّب تحط he أو him مكان الفراغ في الجملة الصغيرة اللي بعده. لو ينفع he (هو اللي عمل الفعل) يبقى who. لو ينفع him (الفعل وقع عليه) يبقى whom — الاتنين آخرهم حرف m. ومتستخدمش which للأشخاص أبدًا.

Rule 5: Its/it's, their/there/they're, your/you're, whose/who's

Possessive pronouns never have an apostrophe. An apostrophe in a pronoun always means a contraction (two words joined). Test every apostrophe by opening it up.

WordMeansTest✓ Example
itsbelonging to it—The city cleaned its canals.
it'sit is / it hasCan you say "it is"?It's been a dry year.
theirbelonging to them—The students lost their keys.
therea place / "there is"Can you say "here"?Leave the boxes there. There are two exits.
they'rethey areCan you say "they are"?They're arriving tomorrow.
your / you'rebelonging to you / you areCan you say "you are"?You're late for your exam.
whose / who'sbelonging to whom / who isCan you say "who is"?Who's the author whose book won?

Rule 6: Stay consistent — one / you / we

Once a passage chooses a pronoun for people in general, keep it. If the sentence begins with one, continue with one (or *one's*), not *you*. If it begins with you, continue with you. Do not switch between *we*, *you* and *one* inside the same sentence.

  • ✓ If one wants to see the sunrise, one should start early.
  • ✗ If one wants to see the sunrise, you should start early.
  • ✓ If you want to see the sunrise, you should start early.

The 5-step method for test day

  1. Read the choices. Pronouns or look-alikes? It is a pronoun question.
  2. Find the antecedent. Go back in the sentence (or the sentence before) and ask "Who or what does this pronoun mean?"
  3. Check number: singular antecedent → it/its/he/she; plural → they/their. Remember companies, teams and species are singular.
  4. Check form: subject or object (he/him test, cover the other person)? possessive or contraction (open the apostrophe)?
  5. Check clarity: if two nouns could be the antecedent, pick the choice that names the noun.

Worked examples

Worked example 1: a company is "it"

Passage: "The textile company began as a small workshop in El-Mahalla El-Kubra in the 1950s. Today, ______ products are sold in more than thirty countries, although most of its cotton is still grown in the Nile Delta."

Question: Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

  • A) their
  • B) its
  • C) it's
  • D) they're

Answer: B) its. The antecedent is "The textile company" — one company, so it is singular. A possessive is needed ("the company's products"), so "its" is correct. The passage confirms it later: "its cotton".

  • A) their — Plural. A company is one thing on the SAT, even though many people work for it.
  • C) it's — "It's" = "it is": "it is products are sold" makes no sense.
  • D) they're — "They're" = "they are", and it is plural.

Worked example 2: they're vs their vs there

Passage: "Honeybees share the location of food through a movement called the waggle dance. When forager bees return to the hive, ______ able to show both the direction and the distance of a flower patch through the angle and length of their dance."

Question: Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

  • A) its
  • B) their
  • C) they're
  • D) there

Answer: C) they're. The clause needs a subject and a verb: "the forager bees are able to show". "They're" = "they are", and "they" agrees with the plural "forager bees".

  • A) its — "Its" is possessive and singular; there would be no verb ("its able").
  • B) their — "Their" is possessive; "their able" has no verb.
  • D) there — "There able" has no subject or verb.

Worked example 3: unclear pronoun

Passage: "In the 1820s, Jean-François Champollion compared the Greek text on the Rosetta Stone with the hieroglyphic text above it. Since scholars could already read the Greek, ______ helped him identify the hieroglyphic signs for royal names such as Ptolemy."

Question: Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

  • A) it
  • B) they
  • C) this
  • D) the Greek inscription

Answer: D) the Greek inscription. Three singular nouns come before the blank: the Rosetta Stone, the hieroglyphic text and the Greek. A pronoun would not make clear which one helped. Only the noun "the Greek inscription" removes the doubt.

  • A) it — Unclear: "it" could mean the stone, the Greek text or the hieroglyphic text.
  • B) they — Plural, and the only plural noun, "scholars", is not what helped him: the readable Greek text did.
  • C) this — Vague: "this" does not name what it refers to.

Worked example 4: who vs whom

Passage: "The engineer ______ the city hired to design the new pedestrian bridge had already worked on several projects along the Nile Corniche. Her design, which uses lightweight steel cables, was chosen from more than forty proposals."

Question: Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

  • A) who
  • B) whom
  • C) which
  • D) whose

Answer: B) whom. Make the small sentence: "the city hired ___" → "the city hired her" (object form). Object = "whom". The engineer receives the action of "hired"; "the city" is the subject.

  • A) who — "Who" is the subject form; but the subject of "hired" is "the city", not the engineer.
  • C) which — "Which" is for things, never for people.
  • D) whose — "Whose" shows possession: "whose the city hired" makes no sense.
Classic traps
  • "Their" for a company or group: company, university, museum, team, committee, government → its.
  • Apostrophe trap: its, their, your, whose have no apostrophe. it's / they're / you're / who's are always two words.
  • Far antecedent: the noun is at the start of a long sentence; a closer noun with a different number is the bait.
  • "Me" vs "I" with two people: cover the other person and test each pronoun alone.
  • "Which" for people: always wrong; use who/whom/that.
  • Unclear "they" / "it" / "this": if two nouns could be meant, the choice that names the noun wins.
  • Switching one ↔ you: keep the same pronoun for people in general.
Fast strategy for test day
  • Point to the antecedent before reading the choices; say it with the pronoun ("the company … its").
  • For apostrophes, open the contraction: "it is", "they are", "you are". If it does not make sense, the possessive is correct.
  • For who/whom, use the he/him test on the words after the blank.
  • Choose a noun instead of a pronoun only when there really are two possible antecedents.
  • Target time: 30–40 seconds per question.
الأبوستروف في الضمائر

لو لقيت ' في ضمير يبقى معناه اختصار لكلمتين: it's = it is، they're = they are، you're = you are. الملكية في الضمائر من غير أبوستروف خالص: its, their, your, whose. افتح الاختصار في دماغك: لو الجملة باظت، يبقى الصح هو الملكية.

Remember
  • Find the antecedent first. Singular (including company, university, team, species) → it/its; plural → they/their.
  • If two nouns could be the antecedent, choose the answer that names the noun.
  • Subject form (I, he, who) does the action; object form (me, him, whom) receives it or follows a preposition. Cover the other person to test.
  • Possessive pronouns have no apostrophe: its, their, your, whose. it's / they're / you're / who's = two words.
  • Keep the same pronoun for people in general: one … one, you … you.
