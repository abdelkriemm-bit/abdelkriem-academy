Grammar in Use: Tenses, Passive, Reported Speech and More

Tenses (including present perfect and past progressive), active and passive verb forms, direct and reported speech, subject-verb agreement, comparatives and superlatives, if-sentences, standard English, formal and informal language and proofreading.

British & IGCSE › Checkpoint 6 English (Cambridge Primary 0058)

1. Grammar in Checkpoint 6: what you need

This page covers the grammar you use every time you write and the verb forms the reading questions can ask you to recognise. In both Paper 1 (Non-fiction) and Paper 2 (Fiction), Section A Reading is worth 25 marks and Section B Writing is worth 25 marks.

WhereWhat can happen
ReadingA tick-one question can ask you to choose the verb form of a highlighted verb, for example simple past passive or present perfect passive, or to find a passive verb in the text.
ReadingYou may be asked to find or explain a comparative adjective (*bigger, more dangerous*) in the text.
WritingGrammar and punctuation (7 marks): correct tenses, correct agreement, standard English and a range of verb forms. Vocabulary and language (3 marks): the right level of formality for your audience.
Exam words
  • Tense: the form of a verb that shows when something happens (past, present, future).
  • Base form: the verb with nothing added: *go, eat, play*.
  • Past participle (3rd form): *gone, eaten, played*. Used after *have/has/had* and in the passive.
  • Auxiliary (helping) verb: *be, have, do, will, can...* used with the main verb.
  • Progressive (also called continuous): a form with *be + -ing* (*is running, was running*).
  • Perfect: a form with *have/has/had + past participle* (*has finished, had finished*).

3. Past simple and past progressive together

In stories (Paper 2) you will use these two tenses all the time. The past progressive sets the scene or shows a longer action; the past simple shows the short action that happens in the middle of it.

  • *I was reading in bed when the lights went out.* (long action + short interrupting action)
  • *While we were eating dinner, the phone rang.*
  • *The sun was shining and the birds were singing.* (setting the scene)
While or when?

Use while before the long action (past progressive) and when before the short action (past simple): *While I was walking, I saw a fox.* / *I was walking when I saw a fox.*

4. Present perfect or past simple?

Present perfectPast simple
No finished time: *I have visited Luxor.* (at some time in my life)Finished time is given: *I visited Luxor last summer.*
Result now: *I have lost my key.* (I still can't find it)Just the past event: *I lost my key yesterday, but I found it.*
Unfinished time: *We have lived here since 2019 / for five years.*Finished time: *We lived in Giza for five years.* (we don't now)
Never mix them

Do not use the present perfect with a finished time word: *I have seen him yesterday* ✗ → *I saw him yesterday* ✓

6. Keep your tense consistent

Choose one main tense for your writing and stay in it. Most stories, recounts, diaries and news reports are written in the past. Information texts, reviews and persuasive texts are mostly in the present. Changing tense by mistake is one of the most common errors.

Wrong (tense changes)Right
*We arrived at the beach and Dad sets up the umbrella.**We arrived at the beach and Dad set up the umbrella.*
*Suddenly the door opens and a man walked in.**Suddenly the door opened and a man walked in.*

7. Active and passive

In an active sentence, the subject does the action. In a passive sentence, the subject receives the action, and the doer moves to the end after by, or disappears.

  • Active: *The children painted the wall.* (the children do the action)
  • Passive: *The wall was painted by the children.* (the wall receives the action)
  • Passive with no doer: *The wall was painted last week.*

How to recognise a passive verb: look for a form of be + a past participle: *is made, was found, were taken, has been opened, have been sold, will be built.*

How to change active into passive (steps):

  1. Take the object of the active sentence and make it the subject.
  2. Add the correct form of be, in the same tense as the active verb.
  3. Add the past participle of the main verb.
  4. Add by + the doer only if it is important.
Verb formActivePassive (be + past participle)
Present simple passiveWorkers make these toys.These toys are made (by workers).
Present progressive passiveThey are building a school.A school is being built.
Simple past passiveSomeone stole my bike.My bike was stolen.
Past progressive passiveThey were repairing the road.The road was being repaired.
Present perfect passiveThe council has opened a new park.A new park has been opened (by the council).
Past perfect passiveSomeone had locked the gate.The gate had been locked.
Future passiveThey will announce the winners.The winners will be announced.
With a modalYou must complete the form.The form must be completed.
The two forms most often asked about
  • Simple past passive = was / were + past participle: *was found, were taken, was built, were invited*.
  • Present perfect passive = has / have + been + past participle: *has been found, have been taken, has been built*.
  • To tell them apart, look for been. If you see *has been / have been* + past participle, it is present perfect passive.

Why do writers use the passive? Learn these answers:

  • to focus on the thing or the action, not on who did it (*The pyramids were built over 4,000 years ago.*)
  • because the doer is unknown (*My phone was stolen.*)
  • because the doer is obvious or not important (*The thief was arrested.* We know it was the police.)
  • to make the writing sound formal and factual, as in reports, news reports and science texts
  • in stories: to hide who did something, which creates mystery (*The window had been smashed.*)
Worked example (tick-one style)

*Text: ...the old lighthouse has been repaired and will open again in June.* Which verb form is the highlighted verb? A simple past passive B present perfect passive C present progressive D past perfect. Answer: B, because it is *has been* + the past participle *repaired*.

8. Direct and reported speech

Direct speech gives the speaker's exact words inside inverted commas. Reported speech (also called indirect speech) tells us what someone said without their exact words and without inverted commas. Reported speech is very useful in news reports, recounts and stories.

  • Direct: *Hana said, “I am tired.”*
  • Reported: *Hana said (that) she was tired.*

Four steps to change direct speech into reported speech:

  1. Remove the inverted commas. You can add *that* (it is optional).
  2. Change the pronouns to fit who is speaking: *I → he/she, we → they, my → his/her, our → their*.
  3. Move the tense one step back if the reporting verb is in the past (*said, told, asked*).
  4. Change time and place words if needed.
Direct speechReported speech
present simple: “I play chess.”past simple: He said he played chess.
present progressive: “I am reading.”past progressive: She said she was reading.
present perfect: “I have finished.”past perfect: He said he had finished.
past simple: “I saw a fox.”past perfect: She said she had seen a fox.
will: “I will help.”would: He said he would help.
can: “I can swim.”could: She said she could swim.
must: “I must go.”had to: He said he had to go.

Questions and commands:

  • Yes/no question: *“Are you ready?” he asked.* → *He asked if (whether) I was ready.*
  • Wh- question: *“Where do you live?” she asked.* → *She asked where I lived.* (no question mark, normal word order)
  • Command: *“Sit down,” the teacher said.* → *The teacher told us to sit down.*
  • Negative command: *“Don't run!” Mum said.* → *Mum told us not to run.*
No backshift needed

If the reporting verb is in the present (*says, tells*), the tense does not change: *Ali says, “I am hungry.”* → *Ali says he is hungry.* The tense also usually stays the same for facts that are always true: *She said the Nile flows north.*

Use different reporting verbs

Instead of *said* every time, choose a verb that shows how something was said: *explained, replied, admitted, promised, warned, suggested, insisted, complained, whispered, announced*. This also helps your Vocabulary and language mark.

9. Subject-verb agreement

The verb must agree with its subject: a singular subject takes a singular verb, and a plural subject takes a plural verb.

RuleCorrect example
Singular subject → singular verb (+s in the present)The dog barks. / She was late.
Plural subject → plural verbThe dogs bark. / They were late.
Two subjects joined by *and* → pluralOmar and Ali are cousins.
*either ... or / neither ... nor* → the verb agrees with the nearer subjectNeither the teacher nor the students were ready. / Either the students or the teacher is coming.
*everyone, everybody, someone, nobody, each, every* → singularEveryone is here. / Each child has a book.
The real subject, not the nearest nounThe box of chocolates is on the table. (the box is)
*one of* + plural noun → singular verbOne of my friends lives in Dubai.
*There is* + singular / *There are* + pluralThere is a cat. / There are many shops.
Collective nouns (team, family, class) usually singularOur team is winning.
Amounts of time, money and distance as one unit → singularTen kilometres is a long way to walk.
Mistakes the examiner sees every year
  • *we was* ✗ → *we were* ✓
  • *he don't* ✗ → *he doesn't* ✓
  • *there was many people* ✗ → *there were many people* ✓
  • *everyone have* ✗ → *everyone has* ✓
  • *the information were* ✗ → *the information was* ✓

10. Comparatives and superlatives

A comparative compares two things (*taller than*). A superlative compares three or more and shows the top or bottom (*the tallest*).

AdjectiveRuleComparativeSuperlative
short (one syllable): tall, fastadd -er / -esttaller, fasterthe tallest, the fastest
ends in -e: large, safeadd -r / -stlarger, saferthe largest, the safest
one vowel + one consonant: big, hot, thindouble the last letterbigger, hotter, thinnerthe biggest, the hottest, the thinnest
ends in -y: happy, busy, easychange y to ihappier, busier, easierthe happiest, the busiest, the easiest
long (most two syllables, all three or more): careful, beautiful, dangerousmore / mostmore careful, more dangerousthe most careful, the most dangerous
irregular: good, bad, farlearn thembetter, worse, further/fartherthe best, the worst, the furthest/farthest
little / much / manylearn themless, morethe least, the most
  • Comparative: *The Nile is longer than the Thames.*
  • Superlative: *The Nile is the longest river in Africa.*
  • With *as ... as*: *My bag is as heavy as yours.* / *not as heavy as*
  • Adverbs too: *She ran faster than me. / He spoke more quietly than before.*
Never double them
  • *more better* ✗ → *better* ✓
  • *the most fastest* ✗ → *the fastest* ✓
  • *more easier* ✗ → *easier* ✓
  • *badder* ✗ → *worse* ✓

Fewer or less? Many or much? Use fewer and many with things you can count (*fewer cars, many books*). Use less and much with things you cannot count (*less water, much noise*).

Why do writers use comparatives and superlatives? To compare things clearly, to show which is best or worst, to make something sound better than anything else in persuasive texts and adverts (*the most exciting ride in Egypt*), and to give a clear judgement in reviews.

11. If-sentences (conditionals)

If-sentences talk about a condition and its result. They are not usually asked about directly in the reading questions, but you must use them correctly in your writing.

TypeFormUseExample
ZeroIf + present simple, present simplefacts that are always trueIf you heat ice, it melts.
FirstIf + present simple, will + base formreal, possible futureIf it rains tomorrow, we will stay at home.
SecondIf + past simple, would + base formimaginary or unlikely situationsIf I had wings, I would fly to the moon.
ThirdIf + had + past participle, would have + past participleimagining a different past (regrets)If we had left earlier, we would have caught the train.
Common mistakes
  • *If it will rain, we will stay home* ✗ → *If it rains, we will stay home* ✓ (no *will* after *if*)
  • *If I would be rich...* ✗ → *If I were rich...* ✓
  • When the *if*-clause comes first, put a comma after it. When it comes second, no comma: *We will stay home if it rains.*

Where they help your writing: persuasive texts (*If we do nothing, our beaches will disappear.*), instructions and advice (*If the water boils, turn down the heat.*), balanced arguments (*If the centre were built here, traffic would increase.*) and stories (a character's thoughts: *If only I had listened...*).

12. Standard English

Standard English is the correct, accepted form of English used in writing, school and the exam. Some forms are fine when chatting with friends, but they are non-standard and lose marks in writing (except inside a character's speech in a story, where they can show how the character talks).

Non-standardStandard English
We was playing.We were playing.
I done my homework.I did my homework. / I have done my homework.
Me and Adam went to the park.Adam and I went to the park.
Them books are mine.Those books are mine.
I could of won.I could have won.
I didn't do nothing.I didn't do anything. / I did nothing.
He ain't here.He isn't here.
She sings real good.She sings really well.
Everyone should bring their book if they wants.Everyone should bring their book if they want to.
Me and I

Take the other person away and test: *Adam and I went* → *I went* ✓ (*me went* ✗). *The teacher thanked Adam and me* → *thanked me* ✓.

14. Proofreading: the last few minutes

Proofreading means reading your writing again to find and fix mistakes in grammar, spelling and punctuation. Leave the last few minutes of your 30 minutes of writing time for it. Check in this order:

  1. Sentences: every sentence starts with a capital letter and ends with . ? or ! (no comma splices or run-ons).
  2. Tense: the same main tense all the way through.
  3. Agreement: *we were, he doesn't, there are, everyone has*.
  4. Standard English: no *we was, could of, me and him went*.
  5. Punctuation: commas in lists and after opening clauses, speech punctuated correctly, apostrophes in the right place (*its/it's, their/they're/there*).
  6. Spelling: check tricky words, especially irregular past forms and *-er/-est* spellings.

Worked example. This original paragraph has at least ten mistakes. Try to find them before you look at the correction.

*last saturday me and my cousin goes to the zoo, we was very excited. The lions was sleeping but the monkeys were the most funniest animals there. Their was a sign that said “dont feed the animals.”*

Corrected: *Last Saturday, my cousin and I went to the zoo. We were very excited. The lions were sleeping, but the monkeys were the funniest animals there. There was a sign that said, “Don't feed the animals.”*

  • *last* → *Last* (capital at the start of a sentence)
  • *saturday* → *Saturday* (days need capitals)
  • *me and my cousin* → *my cousin and I* (standard English)
  • *goes* → *went* (past tense)
  • *zoo, we* → *zoo. We* (comma splice fixed)
  • *we was* → *we were* (agreement)
  • *The lions was* → *The lions were* (agreement)
  • *the most funniest* → *the funniest* (double superlative)
  • *Their was* → *There was* (homophone)
  • *dont feed* → *Don't feed* (apostrophe for omission, and a capital letter at the start of the speech)
  • *said “* → *said, “* (a comma before the speech)

15. Practice A: tenses

  1. Complete: *While I ______ (walk) home, I ______ (find) a wallet.*

    Show answer
    *While I was walking home, I found a wallet.* (past progressive + past simple)

  2. Complete: *I ______ (never / see) snow.*

    Show answer
    *I have never seen snow.* (present perfect: life experience)

  3. Correct the mistake: *We have gone to Alexandria last summer.*

    Show answer
    *We went to Alexandria last summer.* (finished time → past simple)

  4. Complete: *By the time we arrived, the film ______ (start).*

    Show answer
    *the film had started.* (past perfect: it happened before we arrived)

  5. Which tense? *They are planting trees in the park this week.*

    Show answer
    Present progressive (am/is/are + -ing).

16. Practice B: active and passive

  1. Active or passive? *The letter was delivered this morning.*

    Show answer
    Passive: *was* + past participle *delivered*.

  2. Which verb form is it? *Three new classrooms have been built.*

    Show answer
    Present perfect passive (*have been* + past participle).

  3. Which verb form is it? *The winners were chosen by the judges.*

    Show answer
    Simple past passive (*were* + past participle).

  4. Change to passive: *A strong wind knocked down the tree.*

    Show answer
    *The tree was knocked down by a strong wind.*

  5. Change to passive (no doer needed): *Someone has broken the window.*

    Show answer
    *The window has been broken.*

  6. Why does a news report say *A man has been rescued from the river* and not name the rescuers?

    Show answer
    The passive focuses on the man and the rescue, which is the important news; it also sounds formal and factual.

17. Practice C: reported speech

  1. Report it: *“I am going to the market,” said Mum.*

    Show answer
    *Mum said (that) she was going to the market.*

  2. Report it: *“We have finished the project,” the boys said.*

    Show answer
    *The boys said (that) they had finished the project.*

  3. Report it: *“Will you help me tomorrow?” Ali asked his brother.*

    Show answer
    *Ali asked his brother if (whether) he would help him the next day.*

  4. Report it: *“Don't touch the oven!” Grandma told us.*

    Show answer
    *Grandma told us not to touch the oven.*

  5. Change to direct speech: *The coach said that the match had been cancelled.*

    Show answer
    *The coach said, “The match has been cancelled.”*

18. Practice D: agreement, comparatives, standard English and formality

  1. Choose: *Neither my brother nor my sisters (like / likes) fish.*

    Show answer
    like: the verb agrees with the nearer subject, *sisters* (plural).

  2. Choose: *Each of the players (has / have) a shirt.*

    Show answer
    has: *each* is singular.

  3. Correct: *This is the most best day of my life.*

    Show answer
    *This is the best day of my life.*

  4. Write the comparative of *busy* and the superlative of *thin*.

    Show answer
    *busier* and *the thinnest*.

  5. Correct: *There is less people here than yesterday.*

    Show answer
    *There are fewer people here than yesterday.* (people are countable and plural)

  6. Correct: *If it will snow, we will build a snowman.*

    Show answer
    *If it snows, we will build a snowman.*

  7. Make formal: *The new sports hall is awesome and loads of kids use it.*

    Show answer
    *The new sports hall is excellent, and many pupils use it every day.*

  8. Correct all the non-standard English: *Me and Hassan done the washing up but we was tired.*

    Show answer
    *Hassan and I did the washing-up, but we were tired.*
