Grammar: Phrasal verbs — separable and inseparable

اقرا الشرح بالعربي الأول، وبعدين بص على الجدول والأمثلة. لو مش فاهم، اسمع درس الجرامر اللي في خطة الأسبوع.

Separable phrasal verbs: a noun object can go in the middle or at the end: turn down the music / turn the music down. A pronoun (it, them, him, her) MUST go in the middle: turn it down.

Inseparable phrasal verbs never split: I came across an old photo. I came across it.

معنى الفعل المركب (phrasal verb) غالبًا لا يُفهم من كلماته منفصلة، فاحفظه كوحدة واحدة مع مثال. أشهر خطأ هو وضع الضمير في آخر الجملة مثل Turn down it أو I looked up it، والصحيح Turn it down و I looked it up. أما الأفعال غير القابلة للفصل فلا نضع شيئًا في وسطها: I'm looking forward to the trip. وانتبه: بعد look forward to يأتي فعل بـ ing لأن to هنا حرف جر: I'm looking forward to seeing you (وليس to see).

TypeRuleExample
Separable + nounobject in the middle or at the endPut off the meeting. / Put the meeting off.
Separable + pronounpronoun always in the middlePut it off. (not Put off it)
Inseparableobject always after the particleI came across an old photo. I came across it.
Three-part verbsnever separateI'm looking forward to the film. We ran out of popcorn.

Separable: put off, turn down / up, look up, pick up, take up, give away, try on, fill in, throw away.

Inseparable: come across, look after, take after, get into, run out of, keep up with, look forward to, get over.

Wrong: I'm looking forward to see you. ✔ I'm looking forward to seeing you.

Exercises · تمارين

حل كل تمرين في يومه بالقلم الرصاص، وبعدين دوس «Show the answers» وصحّح لنفسك. اكتب أخطاءك في الكشكول وراجعها في اليوم السادس.

Exercise 1 · Put the words in the correct order.

  1. 1) up / it / I / looked
  2. 2) the music / turn / please / down
  3. 3) them / I / off / put / can't
  4. 4) across / I / an old film / came
  5. 5) forward / looking / I'm / to / the concert
  6. 6) after / her mother / takes / Mona
Show the answers
1) I looked it up. 2) Please turn the music down. / Please turn down the music. 3) I can't put them off. 4) I came across an old film. 5) I'm looking forward to the concert. 6) Mona takes after her mother.

Exercise 2 · Replace the words in brackets with a phrasal verb.

  1. 1) She (refused) the offer to act in the film.
  2. 2) We're (excited about) the summer holiday.
  3. 3) They (postponed) the festival until next year.
  4. 4) I (found by chance) a great bookshop in Alexandria.
  5. 5) I want to (start) photography as a hobby.
  6. 6) We have (used all our) tickets.
Show the answers
1) turned down 2) looking forward to 3) put off 4) came across 5) take up 6) run out of

Exercise 3 · Correct the mistake.

  1. 1) Can you turn down it?
  2. 2) I'm looking forward to watch the match.
  3. 3) Please pick up them from the cinema.
  4. 4) She takes her grandmother after.
  5. 5) We ran out popcorn.
  6. 6) I came an interesting article across.
Show the answers
1) Can you turn it down? 2) I'm looking forward to watching the match. 3) Please pick them up from the cinema. 4) She takes after her grandmother. 5) We ran out of popcorn. 6) I came across an interesting article.

Exercise 4 · Translate into English.

  1. 1) أجّلوا الحفل إلى الأسبوع القادم.
  2. 2) لم أعرف الكلمة فبحثت عنها.
  3. 3) أتطلع إلى رؤيتك في المهرجان.
  4. 4) يشبه أحمد جدّه كثيرًا.
  5. 5) كان الفيلم مشوقًا جدًا، لكن النهاية كانت متوقعة.
Show the answers
1) They put the concert off (put off the concert) until next week. / They put off the concert till next week. 2) I didn't know the word, so I looked it up. 3) I'm looking forward to seeing you at the festival. 4) Ahmed really takes after his grandfather. 5) The film was gripping, but the ending was predictable. / The film was really exciting, but the ending was predictable.

Do each exercise in pencil first, then open the answers to check.

Write & Speak · اكتب واتكلم

اكتب الموضوع في الكشكول بنفسك، وبص على المثال لو احتجت، وبعدين اقراه بصوت عالي أو سجّل نفسك.

Write & Speak — Write a review (150–200 words) of a book, film, series or concert you enjoyed. Describe it, give your opinion and recommend it. Use at least five phrasal verbs. Then record a one-minute spoken review, as if for your own channel.

Example: I recently came across an old copy of Naguib Mahfouz's Palace Walk (Bayn al-Qasrayn) in my father's bookcase, and I couldn't put it down. It is the first part of the famous Cairo Trilogy, and it tells the story of a family living in old Cairo in the years leading up to the 1919 revolution. The father, Al-Sayyid Ahmad Abd al-Jawad, is strict and serious at home but fun-loving with his friends outside. His wife, Amina, spends her life looking after the family and almost never leaves the house. Their children grow up in a changing country, and their dreams and problems are very moving. The plot is slow at the beginning, so some readers might give up after a few chapters. My advice is: don't! Once you get into the story, it becomes gripping. I had to look up a few historical events, but that made it even more interesting. In my opinion, the novel is not overrated at all. Mahfouz won the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1988, and after reading this book, I understand why. I'm already looking forward to reading the second part, Palace of Desire. A must-read!
