Modifiers: Misplaced, Dangling and Used Well

What modifiers are, how to fix misplaced and dangling modifiers, where to put words like only and nearly, adjectives versus adverbs, and how to use modifiers well in your writing, with practice and answers.

British & IGCSE › Checkpoint 8 › Checkpoint 8 English (Cambridge Lower Secondary 0861)

1. What a modifier is

A modifier is a word, phrase or clause that describes or gives more information about another word. The tired runner slowly crossed the line with a huge smile.

  • Adjectives modify nouns: a narrow street.
  • Adverbs modify verbs, adjectives or other adverbs: She spoke softly. It was extremely cold.
  • Phrases can modify: The girl in the blue coat waved. Shaking with fear, he opened the door.
  • Clauses can modify: The book that I borrowed is overdue.

The golden rule: put a modifier next to the thing it describes, or the meaning changes (often comically).

Key words
  • Misplaced modifier: a modifier in the wrong place, so it seems to describe the wrong word.
  • Dangling modifier: an opening phrase with no correct doer in the sentence, so it attaches to the wrong noun.
  • Limiting modifier: small words like only, just, nearly, almost, even that change meaning depending on where they go.

2. Misplaced and dangling modifiers

Wrong (misplaced or dangling)ProblemCorrect
Walking to school, the rain soaked my uniform.The rain was not walking to school.Walking to school, I got soaked by the rain.
Covered in sugar, Omar ate the doughnut.Omar was not covered in sugar (we hope).Omar ate the doughnut, which was covered in sugar.
I nearly ate the whole pizza. (if you mean you ate almost all of it)Nearly is in the wrong place: it says you did not eat at all.I ate nearly the whole pizza.
After finishing the race, the medal was given to Hana.The medal did not finish the race.After finishing the race, Hana was given the medal.
She found a gold ring walking along the beach.The ring was not walking.Walking along the beach, she found a gold ring.
We saw a camel on the way to the hotel eating grass.The hotel was not eating grass.On the way to the hotel, we saw a camel eating grass.
Ask: who is doing this?

When a sentence begins with an -ing or -ed phrase, the very next noun must be the person or thing doing that action.

Two ways to fix a dangling modifier:

  1. Put the doer straight after the comma: Opening the box, Salma gasped.
  2. Turn the phrase into a full clause with its own subject: As Salma opened the box, a bird flew out.

3. Limiting modifiers: only, just, nearly, even

These small words change the meaning depending on where you put them. Place them directly before the word they limit.

SentenceMeaning
Only Karim ate the cake.Nobody else ate it.
Karim only ate the cake.Eating was the only thing he did with it. (In everyday speech this is often used to mean ate only the cake, so careful writers move only.)
Karim ate only the cake.He ate the cake and nothing else.
Even the teacher laughed.Surprisingly, the teacher laughed too.
The teacher even laughed.Laughing was a surprising extra thing the teacher did.

4. Adjective or adverb?

WrongRightWhy
She sings beautiful.She sings beautifully.sings is a verb, so it needs an adverb.
He did good in the test.He did well in the test.good is an adjective. The adverb is well.
The food smells deliciously.The food smells delicious.After linking verbs (smell, look, seem, feel, taste, sound), use an adjective.
It was real hot.It was really hot.really (adverb) modifies the adjective hot.
Drive slow.Drive slowly.Use the adverb in written Standard English.

Order of adjectives. When you use several adjectives before a noun, English usually follows this order: opinion – size – age – shape – colour – origin – material – purpose + noun. a beautiful old blue Egyptian vase, a small round wooden table. Two or three adjectives are usually enough: more can feel crowded.

5. Modifiers as a tool in your writing

Used well, modifiers make your sentences more varied and precise, which helps your marks for sentence structure and vocabulary.

  • Open with an -ing or -ed phrase for variety: Trembling, she reached for the handle. Exhausted by the climb, we sat on the rocks.
  • Add a relative clause for extra detail: The lighthouse, which had been empty for years, suddenly glowed.
  • Choose precise adverbs that show how an action is done: whispered urgently, crept cautiously.
  • Do not overload: one strong adjective (a deserted street) is better than three weak ones (a very empty, quiet, lonely street).

6. How Checkpoint asks about modifiers

Modifiers are rarely asked about by name in reading. In reading you are more likely to be asked about the effect of the writer's adjectives and adverbs (what they show about a character or setting). In your writing, misplaced and dangling modifiers make sentences unclear, and clarity and control of sentences are rewarded.

Exam-style example. Silently, the cat crept along the narrow, crumbling wall. Question: What do the words silently and crumbling suggest? Answer: Silently shows the cat is careful and stealthy, and crumbling shows the wall is old and unsafe, which creates tension because the cat could fall.

7. Practice

  1. Correct: Running for the bus, my phone fell out of my pocket.

    Show answer
    While I was running for the bus, my phone fell out of my pocket.

  2. Correct: Barking loudly, the postman ran away from the dog.

    Show answer
    The postman ran away from the dog, which was barking loudly.

  3. Correct: Having finished the homework, the television was switched on.

    Show answer
    Having finished the homework, I switched on the television. (the doer goes after the comma)

  4. Correct: I saw a crocodile on the boat trip with huge teeth.

    Show answer
    On the boat trip, I saw a crocodile with huge teeth.

  5. Explain the difference: (a) Only Sara passed the test. (b) Sara passed only the test.

    Show answer
    (a) Sara was the one person who passed. (b) Sara passed the test but nothing else (for example, not the other parts of the course).

  6. Choose: The soup tastes (wonderful / wonderfully).

    Show answer
    wonderful: tastes is a linking verb, so it takes an adjective.

  7. Choose: Mazen answered the questions (careful / carefully).

    Show answer
    carefully: it describes how he answered (a verb), so use an adverb.

  8. Put the adjectives in the right order: a (leather / old / brown) bag

    Show answer
    an old brown leather bag (age – colour – material).

  9. Rewrite with an -ing opening phrase, keeping the meaning: The boy whistled. He walked into the dark forest.

    Show answer
    Whistling, the boy walked into the dark forest.
Remember

Modifier next to the word it describes. After an opening -ing or -ed phrase, the doer comes straight after the comma. Place only / just / nearly before the exact word they limit.
