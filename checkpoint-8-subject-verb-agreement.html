Checkpoint 8 · Grammar in Use

Subject-Verb Agreement

The rules for making verbs agree with their subjects, including tricky subjects such as everyone, neither... nor, one of and there is or there are, with common mistakes and practice with answers.

British & IGCSE › Checkpoint 8 › Checkpoint 8 English (Cambridge Lower Secondary 0861)

1. What subject-verb agreement means

The verb must agree with its subject in number: a singular subject takes a singular verb, a plural subject takes a plural verb. The bus leaves at eight. The buses leave at eight.

In the present simple, a singular verb usually ends in -s (she runs, it works), which is the opposite of nouns, where -s usually means plural. Agreement errors are among the most common mistakes in student writing, so checking them is a quick way to protect your grammar mark.

VerbSingular subject (he / she / it / one thing)Plural subject (we / you / they / two or more)
be (present)isare
be (past)waswere
havehashave
dodoes / doesn'tdo / don't
other verbs (present)works, goes, watcheswork, go, watch

Remember: I takes am and was, and you always takes the plural form (you are, you were), even for one person.

2. The rules

RuleExample
Singular subject → singular verb, plural → pluralThe bus leaves at eight. The buses leave at eight.
Ignore phrases between the subject and verbThe box of old photographs was in the attic. (subject = box)
Two subjects joined by and → pluralSalma and her brother are here.
either... or / neither... nor → verb agrees with the nearer subjectNeither the teacher nor the students were ready. / Neither the students nor the teacher was ready.
each, every, everyone, nobody, someone → singularEveryone is welcome. Each of the rooms has a balcony.
as well as / along with do not make the subject pluralThe captain, as well as the players, was tired.
Amounts of money, time and distance as one unit → singularTen kilometres is too far to walk.
Collective nouns: singular as one unitThe team is in the final.
There is / There are: agree with the noun afterThere are many reasons. There is one reason.

3. More tricky subjects

SubjectVerbExample
one of the + plural nounsingular (the subject is one)One of my friends lives in Alexandria.
a number of (= many)pluralA number of students have complained.
the number ofsingularThe number of visitors has doubled.
Nouns ending in -s that are one subject: news, maths, physics, economicssingularThe news is good. Maths is my favourite subject.
Pair nouns: trousers, scissors, glasses, jeanspluralMy glasses are on the table. (but A pair of glasses is...)
-ing phrase as the subjectsingularReading novels helps your vocabulary.
Titles of books, films, placessingularGreat Expectations is a long novel.
Here / There at the start: the subject comes after the verbagree with the noun afterHere are your tickets. There was a loud noise.
who / which / that clausesagree with the noun the relative pronoun refers toShe is the student who wins every race. These are the books that belong to me.
Find the real subject

Cover the words between the subject and the verb (phrases with of, with, in, as well as, including). Then read the subject and verb together: The list (of names) is on the door.

4. Common mistakes

WrongRightWhy
We was late for school.We were late for school.we takes were.
She don't eat meat.She doesn't eat meat.she takes doesn't.
The price of the tickets are too high.The price of the tickets is too high.The subject is price (singular).
Everyone in the class have a laptop.Everyone in the class has a laptop.everyone is singular.
There is many problems.There are many problems.problems is plural.
One of the boys were hurt.One of the boys was hurt.The subject is one.
My friend and her sister is coming.My friend and her sister are coming.Two subjects joined by and = plural.
The news are shocking.The news is shocking.news is singular.

5. How Checkpoint asks about agreement

Agreement is rarely asked about by name in reading. It matters mostly in your writing: every agreement mistake is an accuracy error, and several of them pull your grammar mark down. It may also appear in proofreading-style tasks, where you correct a sentence or choose the right form.

In your writing, take extra care with long subjects (The number of cars on our streets is rising), with everyone / each / every, with There is / There are, and with was / were. Check these during your final proofread.

6. Practice

  1. Choose: The basket of oranges (was / were) on the table.

    Show answer
    was: the subject is basket (singular).

  2. Choose: Neither the players nor the coach (was / were) happy.

    Show answer
    was: the verb agrees with the nearer subject, the coach.

  3. Choose: Each of the students (has / have) a locker.

    Show answer
    has: each is singular.

  4. Choose: There (is / are) three reasons why I disagree.

    Show answer
    are: the noun after it, reasons, is plural.

  5. Choose: Physics (is / are) my hardest subject.

    Show answer
    is: physics is one subject, so it is singular.

  6. Choose: A number of parents (has / have) written to the school.

    Show answer
    have: a number of means many, so it is plural.

  7. Correct: One of my cousins live in Canada.

    Show answer
    One of my cousins lives in Canada. (the subject is one)

  8. Correct: The children, as well as their teacher, was excited.

    Show answer
    The children, as well as their teacher, were excited. (the subject is the children, plural)

  9. Find and correct the two agreement errors: Everybody in my family love football, and my brothers watches every match.

    Show answer
    Everybody in my family loves football, and my brothers watch every match.
Remember

Find the real subject, then match the verb. and = plural. each / every / everyone = singular. There is / are agrees with the noun after it. one of the... = singular.
