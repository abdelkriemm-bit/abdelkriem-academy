Tenses: Choosing and Using the Right Verb Form

The main English tenses, past simple and past progressive in stories, present perfect or past simple, irregular verbs and how to keep your tense the same all the way through.

British & IGCSE › Checkpoint 6 › Checkpoint 6 English (Cambridge Primary 0058)

1. What is a tense?

A tense is the form of a verb that shows when something happens: in the past, the present or the future. Choosing the right tense, and keeping it the same, is one of the easiest ways to make your writing clear and correct.

Words you need to know
  • Base form: the verb with nothing added: *go, eat, play*.
  • Past participle (3rd form): *gone, eaten, played*. Used after *have/has/had* and in the passive.
  • Auxiliary (helping) verb: *be, have, do, will, can...* used with the main verb.
  • Progressive (also called continuous): a form with *be + -ing* (*is running, was running*).
  • Perfect: a form with *have/has/had + past participle* (*has finished, had finished*).

Questions and negatives
  • Present simple: *Do you like maths? / He doesn't like maths.* (after *do/does*, use the base form)
  • Past simple: *Did you see the match? / I didn't see it.* (after *did*, use the base form, not the past form)
  • Present perfect: *Have you finished? / I haven't finished yet.*

3. Past simple and past progressive together

In stories (Paper 2) you will use these two tenses all the time. The past progressive sets the scene or shows a longer action; the past simple shows the short action that happens in the middle of it.

  • *I was reading in bed when the lights went out.* (long action + short interrupting action)
  • *While we were eating dinner, the phone rang.*
  • *The sun was shining and the birds were singing.* (setting the scene)
While or when?

Use while before the long action (past progressive) and when before the short action (past simple): *While I was walking, I saw a fox.* / *I was walking when I saw a fox.*

4. Present perfect or past simple?

Present perfectPast simple
No finished time: *I have visited Luxor.* (at some time in my life)Finished time is given: *I visited Luxor last summer.*
Result now: *I have lost my key.* (I still can't find it)Just the past event: *I lost my key yesterday, but I found it.*
Unfinished time: *We have lived here since 2019 / for five years.*Finished time: *We lived in Giza for five years.* (we don't now)
Since or for?

Use since with a starting point (*since Monday, since 2020, since I was six*). Use for with a length of time (*for two days, for a long time, for ten minutes*).

Never mix them

Do not use the present perfect with a finished time word: *I have seen him yesterday* ✗ → *I saw him yesterday* ✓

5. The past perfect: the earlier past

When you are already writing in the past and you need to go further back, use had + past participle. It helps the reader understand the order of events, especially in stories with a flashback.

  • *When we reached the station, the train had already left.* (the train left first)
  • *Sami was nervous because he had never flown before.*
  • *By the time the firefighters arrived, the neighbours had put out the fire.*

Brought or bought?

brought comes from *bring* (carry something with you). bought comes from *buy* (pay for something). *I bought a cake and brought it to school.*

8. Keep your tense consistent

Choose one main tense for your writing and stay in it. Changing tense by mistake is one of the most common errors in Year 6 writing.

Text typeMain tense
Stories, recounts, diaries, biographies, news reportsPast (past simple + past progressive; past perfect for earlier events)
Information texts (non-chronological reports), reviews, persuasive textsPresent (present simple for facts and opinions)
InstructionsImperative (bossy) verbs: *Mix, Cut, Turn*
Plans, predictions, promises in letters and leafletsFuture (*will / going to*) where you talk about what is coming
Wrong (tense changes)Right
*We arrived at the beach and Dad sets up the umbrella.**We arrived at the beach and Dad set up the umbrella.*
*Suddenly the door opens and a man walked in.**Suddenly the door opened and a man walked in.*
Changing tense on purpose is fine

Speech inside a story can be in any tense: *“I am so hungry,” Laila said.* The speech is present, the story is past. A news report can also end with the future: *The bridge will reopen next month.*

9. Common mistakes

Watch out for these
  • *He didn't went* ✗ → *He didn't go* ✓ (base form after *did*)
  • *Did you saw it?* ✗ → *Did you see it?* ✓
  • *I was go to school* ✗ → *I went / I was going* ✓
  • *She don't like it* ✗ → *She doesn't like it* ✓
  • *I have seen him yesterday* ✗ → *I saw him yesterday* ✓
  • *I have lived here since five years* ✗ → *I have lived here for five years* ✓
  • *He has went* ✗ → *He has gone* ✓ (past participle after *has*)

10. How Checkpoint asks about it

In the reading section, a question can point to a verb in the text and ask you to name its verb form, or ask you to find a verb in a certain form. You may also be asked why the writer chose a tense.

Reading questions in this style
  • *Tick one box to show the verb form of the highlighted verb.* (for example: past progressive / present perfect / past simple)
  • *Find and copy a verb in the past progressive from paragraph 2.*
  • *Why is the information text written in the present tense?* → Because it gives facts that are true now.
  • *Why does the writer use the past progressive at the start of the story?* → To set the scene and show what was already happening before the main event.
Worked example (tick-one style)

*Text: ...Nour was climbing the last few steps when she heard a strange noise.* Which verb form is the highlighted verb? A past simple B past progressive C present perfect D present progressive. Answer: B, because it is *was* + *-ing*.

In the writing section, tenses are marked in Grammar and punctuation (7 marks). The examiner wants a consistent main tense and a range of verb forms used correctly. A story that mixes the past simple, past progressive and past perfect correctly shows you are working at a high level.

  • Set the scene with the past progressive: *Rain was falling and the wind was howling.*
  • Show an earlier event with the past perfect: *Nobody had opened that door for years.*
  • In a letter or report, use the present perfect for recent news: *The school has started a new reading club.*

11. Practice

  1. Complete: *While I ______ (walk) home, I ______ (find) a wallet.*

    Show answer
    *While I was walking home, I found a wallet.* (past progressive + past simple)

  2. Complete: *I ______ (never / see) snow.*

    Show answer
    *I have never seen snow.* (present perfect: life experience)

  3. Correct the mistake: *We have gone to Alexandria last summer.*

    Show answer
    *We went to Alexandria last summer.* (finished time → past simple)

  4. Complete: *By the time we arrived, the film ______ (start).*

    Show answer
    *the film had started.* (past perfect: it happened before we arrived)

  5. Which tense? *They are planting trees in the park this week.*

    Show answer
    Present progressive (am/is/are + -ing).

  6. Correct the mistake: *Did you brought your football?*

    Show answer
    *Did you bring your football?* (base form after *did*)

  7. Choose *since* or *for*: *My grandparents have lived in Aswan ______ 1985.*

    Show answer
    since (1985 is a starting point).

  8. Write the past simple and past participle of *choose* and *throw*.

    Show answer
    *choose → chose → chosen*; *throw → threw → thrown*.

  9. Find the tense mistake and correct it: *The crowd cheered as the runners cross the finish line.*

    Show answer
    *cross* → *crossed*. The sentence is in the past, so both verbs must be past.

  10. Why might a story begin: *The wind was howling and the waves were crashing against the rocks*?

    Show answer
    The past progressive sets the scene: it shows what was already happening, which builds atmosphere before the main event.
