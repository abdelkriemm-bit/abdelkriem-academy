Grammar: Hedging and softening · tend to · seem to · it could be argued that · to some extent

اقرا الشرح بالعربي الأول، وبعدين بص على الجدول والأمثلة. لو مش فاهم، اسمع درس الجرامر اللي في خطة الأسبوع.

Hedging makes statements less direct and more careful. It is essential in presentations, academic writing and polite disagreement. Tools: verbs (tend to, seem to, appear to, suggest) · modals (may, might, could) · adverbs (arguably, possibly, generally) · phrases (it could be argued that, to some extent, in most cases).

يميل المتعلم العربي إلى التعميم القاطع: All young people are lazy · Social media destroys families. في الكتابة الأكاديمية والعروض يبدو هذا متسرّعًا وغير دقيق، فخفّف حكمك: Young people tend to… · Social media may damage some relationships. وانتبه للتركيب: seem / appear / tend + to + المصدر (He seems to be tired، وليس He seems that he tired ✗)، وللماضي نستخدم seem to have + التصريف الثالث: She seems to have forgotten.

Direct (too strong)Hedged (softer)
Young people don't read books.Young people tend not to read as many books as they used to.
This method is the best.This method is arguably the most effective.
The plan will fail.The plan may not be as successful as we hope.
You are wrong.I'm not sure I completely agree with that.
The results prove that exercise cures stress.The results seem to suggest that exercise can reduce stress.
Everyone feels nervous.Most people feel nervous to some extent.

seem / appear + to be + adjective (He seems to be calm) or + to have + V3 (They appear to have left) · It seems that + clause.

Softening in speech: I'm afraid… · I was wondering if… · Perhaps we could… · Wouldn't it be better to…?

Don't hedge everything: make your main point clearly, then hedge the details and the evidence.

Exercises · تمارين

حل كل تمرين في يومه بالقلم الرصاص، وبعدين دوس «Show the answers» وصحّح لنفسك. اكتب أخطاءك في الكشكول وراجعها في اليوم السادس.

Exercise 1 · Make the statements less direct, using the words in brackets.

  1. 1) Students forget most of a lecture within a week. (tend)
  2. 2) The speaker is nervous. (seem)
  3. 3) Social media causes loneliness. (may)
  4. 4) The new system has failed. (appear)
  5. 5) Online learning is better than classroom learning. (could be argued)
  6. 6) Practice is the key to good public speaking. (arguably)
Show the answers
1) Students tend to forget most of a lecture within a week. 2) The speaker seems (to be) nervous. 3) Social media may / might cause loneliness. 4) The new system appears to have failed. 5) It could be argued that online learning is better than classroom learning. 6) Practice is arguably the key to good public speaking.

Exercise 2 · Choose the correct option.

  1. 1) He seems (knowing / to know) a lot about the topic.
  2. 2) The study (suggests / suggests to) that people are reading less.
  3. 3) They appear (to leave / to have left) early yesterday.
  4. 4) People (tend / are tending) to remember stories better than facts.
  5. 5) I agree with you (in some extent / to some extent).
  6. 6) It (could argue / could be argued) that exams are unfair.
Show the answers
1) to know 2) suggests 3) to have left 4) tend 5) to some extent 6) could be argued

Exercise 3 · Correct the mistake.

  1. 1) She seems that she is tired.
  2. 2) People tend forgetting names.
  3. 3) It could argued that the speech was too long.
  4. 4) He appears to forget his notes yesterday.
  5. 5) To some extend, I agree with you.
Show the answers
1) She seems to be tired. / It seems that she is tired. 2) People tend to forget names. 3) It could be argued that the speech was too long. 4) He appears to have forgotten his notes yesterday. 5) To some extent, I agree with you.

Exercise 4 · Translate into English.

  1. 1) يبدو أن الجمهور كان مهتمًا جدًا.
  2. 2) يميل الطلاب إلى نسيان المعلومات بسرعة.
  3. 3) يمكن القول إن مهارات العرض أهم من الشهادات.
  4. 4) أوافقك الرأي إلى حدٍّ ما.
Show the answers
1) The audience seemed to be very interested. / The audience seems to have been very interested. / It seems (that) the audience was very interested. 2) Students tend to forget information quickly. 3) It could be argued that presentation skills are more important than qualifications. 4) I agree with you to some extent. / To some extent, I agree with you.

Do each exercise in pencil first, then open the answers to check.

Write & Speak · اكتب واتكلم

اكتب الموضوع في الكشكول بنفسك، وبص على المثال لو احتجت، وبعدين اقراه بصوت عالي أو سجّل نفسك.

Write & Speak — Write the script for a 2-minute presentation (200–250 words): “Why every student should learn public speaking”. Include an opening that grabs attention, three points with evidence and a strong ending. Use at least five hedging expressions. Then deliver it standing up, with eye contact, and record yourself.

Example: Good morning, everyone. Let me start with a question: how many of you felt your heart beating faster the last time you had to stand up and speak in class? If so, you are not alone. Surveys regularly suggest that public speaking is one of the most common fears in the world. Today I'd like to argue that every student should learn public speaking, and I'm going to give you three reasons. First, it seems to improve confidence in general. Students who practise speaking in front of others tend to participate more in class and appear to be less anxious in job interviews. Of course, it doesn't remove nerves completely, but it teaches us to control them. Second, it could be argued that communication is now as important as technical knowledge. Employers in Egypt and abroad often say that graduates have good qualifications but struggle to present their ideas clearly. A brilliant idea is arguably worth very little if you can't explain it. Third, public speaking helps us to think. When you prepare a talk, you have to organise your ideas, choose your evidence and predict the audience's questions. To some extent, it trains the same skills as essay writing, but in real time. So, what can schools do? Perhaps the simplest solution is to give every student the chance to present at least once a month, with friendly feedback. Thank you for listening. I'd be happy to take your questions now, and I promise my mind won't go blank!
