Oxford ESL 9280 · Writing

The Report (Question 3)

How to write a full-mark 70-90 word report for Question 3 of the OxfordAQA 9280 Writing paper: purpose, reader, register, optional headings, passive voice, recommending language and three model reports.

British & IGCSE › Oxford International GCSE English as a Second Language

1. What the task is and where it sits in the paper

The Writing paper (9280/W) lasts 1 hour 15 minutes, is worth 60 marks and has four compulsory questions. Question 3 is an extended piece of 70-90 words worth 16 marks (Content and Communication 8 + Language 8). The form of Question 3 changes: it can be a blog or diary entry, an email, an article or a report. Recent papers have mostly asked for a blog, but a report has been set as Question 3 recently, so you must be ready for it. The specification has always listed the report as a possible Question 3 form.

A report is a short, factual piece of writing that tells a specific reader (usually a teacher, the head teacher, a club leader or the school council) what happened or what was found, and often what should be done next. It is calm, clear and organised. It is not a story and it is not a chatty blog.

Report (Question 3)
Words70-90
Marks16 = Content and Communication 8 + Language 8
Bullet pointsUsually 4, all compulsory. Missing one means you cannot reach the top Content band.
ReaderNamed in the task: e.g. your teacher, the head teacher, the sports coach, the school council
RegisterFormal or semi-formal: polite, clear, no slang
HeadingsOptional. They earn no marks for layout, but they can help you organise
Suggested timeAbout 20 minutes: plan 4, write 13, check 3
Exam words you must understand
  • Report: a factual account for a specific reader, organised into clear sections.
  • Findings: what you saw, discovered or found out (e.g. from a survey or a visit).
  • Recommendation: your suggestion for what should happen next. Often the last bullet asks for it.
  • Register: how formal your language is. A report is formal or semi-formal.
  • Impersonal: focusing on facts, not on your feelings ('Students were given…' rather than 'I was so excited when…').

The golden rule

The examiner does not give marks for the 'look' of a report. Marks come from answering every bullet clearly, giving useful detail, writing for the named reader and using accurate, varied English. A report without headings that does all of this can still get 16/16.

3. How the 16 marks work (bands in plain English)

The examiner reads your report and places it in a band on two separate grids (working upwards from the lowest band and choosing the best fit). The descriptions below are in our own words.

Content and Communication (8 marks):

BandMarksWhat it looks like in a report
47-8All bullets answered, fully relevant, plenty of useful detail (facts, reasons, a clear recommendation). Clear and easy to follow.
35-6Almost always relevant with a range of detail. Generally clear, with an occasional unclear moment.
23-4Generally relevant, some detail, but sometimes the message breaks down, or a bullet is only partly answered.
11-2Limited: some relevant information, but often unclear.

Language (8 marks):

BandMarksWhat it looks like in a report
47-8Accurate spelling and punctuation. A wide variety of effective vocabulary and structures: passive forms, relative clauses, recommending language, precise words like 'informative' or 'organised'.
35-6Generally accurate, with a variety of suitable vocabulary and structures.
23-4More accurate than inaccurate. Some variety.
11-2Frequent errors. Limited or repetitive language.
The bullet rule

Examiners tick off the compulsory bullets one by one. If one bullet is missing, the answer is 'partial coverage' and cannot enter the top Content band, however good the English. Number the bullets on the question paper and tick each one when you have answered it.

4. How to get full marks: checklist

  1. Underline who the report is for and what it is about.
  2. Number the bullets 1-4. Plan one or two sentences for each.
  3. Open with the purpose of the report in one sentence.
  4. Answer the early 'fact' bullets (what, where, when, how) briefly and precisely.
  5. Put your best detail into the opinion/reason and recommendation bullets.
  6. Use a formal or semi-formal register: no slang, no 'guys', no 'LOL', few exclamation marks.
  7. Use impersonal language and at least one or two passive forms ('Students were divided into groups').
  8. Use quantity words instead of invented statistics: most, the majority, several, a few, almost everyone.
  9. Finish with a clear recommendation using 'I would recommend…', 'It is suggested that…' or 'should'.
  10. If you use headings, keep them to 1-3 words each and do not let them eat your word count.
  11. Stay close to 70-90 words. Check spelling, capitals and full stops.

Most report tasks follow the same pattern. Match each bullet to a section.

Headings: yes or no? Both are acceptable.

With headingsWithout headings
Helps you organise and shows the reader each section clearly.Saves words for detail.

Optional memo lines

Some students start with 'To: Mr Hassan, Head of Year' and 'From: [name], Year 10'. This is fine but it earns no marks and uses words. If you include it, keep it to one short line each, or leave it out and mention the reader in your first sentence.

6. The language of reports

a) Impersonal language. A report focuses on facts and the group, not on your emotions.

Too personal (blog style)Impersonal (report style)
I was soooo happy because the trip was amazing!The trip was very popular with students.
Me and my friends think the canteen is bad.Many students feel that the canteen offers too little choice.
We all went crazy for the robots.The robotics workshop was the highlight of the day.

b) The passive voice. Use it when the action is more important than who did it. Form: be (am/is/are/was/were/has been) + past participle.

ActivePassive (report style)
The guide showed us the ancient galleries.Students were shown the ancient galleries.
We asked twenty students five questions.Twenty students were asked five questions.
The school organised the event.The event was organised by the school.
The school should buy new equipment.New equipment should be bought.
Someone has repaired the computers.The computers have been repaired.

c) Recommending language. Most reports end with a recommendation. Learn these patterns exactly:

PatternExample
I would recommend + -ingI would recommend organising the trip again.
I would recommend that + subject + verbI would recommend that the school extend the visit. ('should extend' and 'extends' are also accepted in British English.)
It is recommended / suggested that…It is suggested that the library opens at lunchtime.
… should (be)…More benches should be provided in the playground.
It would be a good idea to…It would be a good idea to ask students for ideas first.
The best solution would be to…The best solution would be to start earlier.

d) Reporting findings without fake numbers. Simple numbers you could really know (e.g. 'twenty-four students answered') are fine. Do not fill the report with percentages: the task is about clear communication, not maths.

7. How to start

  • Purpose statement: 'The purpose of this report is to describe the Year 10 visit to the science centre.'
  • Aim + method: 'The aim of this report is to show how students spend their free time. Information was collected through a short questionnaire.'
  • Request + reply: 'As requested, this report gives details of last week's charity day.'
  • Facts first: 'On 3 March, forty Year 10 students visited a local water treatment plant.'
  • Introduction heading + one sentence: 'Introduction: This report evaluates the new after-school study club.'

8. How to end

  • Clear recommendation: 'I would recommend repeating the trip next year.'
  • Recommendation + reason: 'It is suggested that the club meets twice a week, as many students cannot attend on Mondays.'
  • Recommendation + condition: 'The event should be held again, provided that more volunteers are found.'
  • Summary + recommendation: 'Overall, the day was a success, and it should become an annual event.'
  • Two short recommendations: 'Firstly, the visit should be longer. Secondly, students should be allowed to choose a gallery.'

9. Useful phrases

Introduction

  • The purpose of this report is to…
  • This report describes / evaluates / summarises…
  • As requested, …
  • Information was collected by…

Giving facts

  • The visit took place on…
  • Students were divided into…
  • The event was attended by…
  • Activities included…

Giving findings and views

  • Most students felt that…
  • The majority of respondents said…
  • Several students mentioned…
  • The most popular … was…
  • Only a few students…

Positive and negative points

  • The main highlight was…
  • One clear advantage was…
  • However, one drawback was that…
  • A problem that was raised was…

Recommending

  • I would recommend + -ing
  • It is recommended that…
  • … should be + past participle
  • It would be a good idea to…
  • Overall, … should be repeated / continued.

10. Common mistakes and what to watch for

MistakeWhy it costs marksFix
Writing a story ('First we woke up early and got on the bus…')Too much time on unimportant events. Bullets lose detail.Skip the journey. Go straight to the facts the bullets ask for.
Forgetting the recommendation bulletPartial coverage: no top Content band.Number and tick the bullets.
Chatty blog tone ('Hey Miss! It was sooo cool!!!')Wrong register for the reader. Communication suffers.Formal or semi-formal words. One exclamation mark at most.
Writing a letter ('Dear Sir… Yours faithfully')Not a report. Wastes words.Start with a title or a purpose sentence.
Five or six headings with one line under eachHeadings eat the 70-90 words. Detail becomes thin.Maximum 3-4 short headings, or none.
Inventing complex statistics ('63.5% of respondents…')Sounds false. Adds nothing to the language mark.Use 'most', 'several', 'a few'.
Only 'I' sentencesLittle variety of structure.Mix in passives and 'students felt that…'.
Passive formed wrongly ('Students were show')Accuracy errors.be + past participle: were shown, was held, were given.

Timing: spend about 20 minutes on Question 3: 4 minutes planning (bullets numbered, a few words for each), 13 minutes writing and 3 minutes checking. Do not steal time from Question 4, which is worth more.

11. Model answers

The word counts below include every word: title and headings too.

Model 1: Report on a school trip (with headings)88 words · Content and Communication 8/8 + Language 8/8 = 16/16
Task: Last month your class went on a trip to a museum. Your head teacher has asked you to write a report about it. You must include: • where you went and when • what students did during the visit • what students thought of the trip • whether the trip should be organised again next year. Your report should be between 70 and 90 words. [16 marks]

Year 10 Museum Trip

Introduction

This report describes the Year 10 visit to the National History Museum on 14 October.

Activities

Students were given a guided tour of the ancient galleries. Afterwards, they completed a quiz in small groups, which encouraged teamwork.

Students' views

Most students found the tour informative, and the quiz was particularly popular. However, several felt that two hours was too short.

Recommendation

I would recommend repeating the trip next year but extending it to a full day, so that students can explore more galleries.

Why this gets the marks
  • Bullet 1 (where and when): first sentence: the National History Museum, 14 October.
  • Bullet 2 (what students did): guided tour + group quiz, with a result ('encouraged teamwork').
  • Bullet 3 (what students thought): a positive view, the most popular part, and a balanced criticism ('However, several felt…').
  • Bullet 4 (organise again?): a clear yes, plus a practical improvement and a reason ('so that students can explore more galleries').
  • Report register: impersonal, calm and formal. No story of the bus journey.
  • Wide variety of structures: passive ('were given'), relative clause ('which encouraged teamwork'), contrast with 'However', 'I would recommend + -ing' used twice ('repeating… extending') and a purpose clause with 'so that'.
  • Precise vocabulary: informative, particularly popular, extending, explore.
  • Accuracy: apostrophe in 'Students' views', correct capitals for the museum's name, full stops throughout.
Model 2: Report on a survey of students' free time (with headings)84 words · Content and Communication 8/8 + Language 8/8 = 16/16
Task: Your English teacher asked you to find out how students in your class spend their free time. Write a report for your teacher. You must include: • how you collected the information • the most popular free-time activities • one problem that students mentioned • what the school could do to help. Your report should be between 70 and 90 words. [16 marks]

Students' Free Time

Aim and method

The aim of this report is to show how Year 10 students relax. Twenty-four classmates answered a short questionnaire during an English lesson.

Findings

The most popular activity is football, followed by online gaming. However, many students complained that there is nowhere safe near their homes to play football in the evenings.

Recommendation

It is suggested that the school opens the sports hall twice a week after lessons, as this would give students a safe place to exercise.

Why this gets the marks
  • Bullet 1 (how collected): a questionnaire, how many students answered, when.
  • Bullet 2 (most popular): football first, online gaming second. A simple, believable ranking with no invented percentages.
  • Bullet 3 (one problem): there is nowhere safe near students' homes to play football in the evenings. A real, specific problem that leads straight to the recommendation.
  • Bullet 4 (what the school could do): a practical recommendation with a reason ('as this would give students a safe place to exercise').
  • Audience and register: written for a teacher. Formal but natural.
  • Variety: 'The aim of this report is to…', 'followed by', 'complained that', 'It is suggested that…', a conditional idea with 'would'.
  • Accuracy: hyphen in 'Twenty-four', correct apostrophe in the title, consistent tenses.
Model 3: Report on a charity event (no headings)85 words · Content and Communication 8/8 + Language 8/8 = 16/16
Task: Your school held a charity day last week. Write a report for the school council. You must include: • what the event was • how students raised money • what went well • one thing that could be improved. Your report should be between 70 and 90 words. [16 marks]

As requested, this report gives details of last Thursday's charity day, which raised money for a local children's hospital.

Each class ran a stall selling homemade cakes, second-hand books or handmade bracelets. In addition, teachers were challenged to a penalty shoot-out, and students paid to watch.

The day was a great success. Almost every stall sold out by lunchtime, and the atmosphere was friendly and lively.

However, the queues at the cake stalls were too long. I would recommend having two cake stalls next time.

Why this gets the marks
  • This report has no headings and still scores full marks: each paragraph covers one bullet, and the paragraph openings guide the reader ('As requested…', 'However…').
  • Bullet 1: a charity day for a local children's hospital. Bullet 2: stalls (three examples) + the penalty shoot-out. Bullet 3: stalls sold out, friendly atmosphere. Bullet 4: long queues → two cake stalls.
  • Detail: concrete examples ('homemade cakes, second-hand books or handmade bracelets'), not general words like 'things'.
  • Variety: relative clause, passive ('teachers were challenged'), 'In addition', 'However', 'I would recommend + -ing'.
  • Register: suitable for the school council: positive but objective.

12. Practice

  1. Task A (70-90 words): Your school has started a new after-school study club. Write a report for your head teacher. You must include: when and where the club meets, what students do there, how students feel about it, one suggestion to improve it. Write a short plan.

    Show answer
    Title: 'Report on the After-School Study Club'. Intro: purpose + Mondays and Wednesdays, 3-4 pm, in the library (bullet 1). Findings: students revise in small groups and older students help with maths (bullet 2). Most students find it useful and quieter than home, but a few say it is too noisy at the start (bullet 3). Recommendation: 'I would recommend that a teacher supervises the first ten minutes' / 'More tables should be provided' (bullet 4).

  2. Task B (70-90 words): Your class carried out a survey about how students travel to school. Write a report for your teacher. You must include: how the information was collected, the most common ways students travel, one problem that students mentioned, what could be done about it. Write a short plan.

    Show answer
    Method: thirty students answered five questions in a form-time survey (passive: 'Thirty students were asked…'). Findings: most come by school bus, followed by car. Only a few walk or cycle. Problem: traffic outside the gate in the morning makes students late. Recommendation: 'It is suggested that the school opens a second gate' or 'Parents should be encouraged to share cars.'

  3. Task C (70-90 words): Your school canteen has introduced a new healthy menu. Write a report for your head teacher. You must include: what has changed on the menu, which new dishes are most popular, what students do not like, one suggestion to improve the menu. Write a short plan.

    Show answer
    Intro: purpose + the new menu introduced at the start of term. Fried snacks have been replaced by grilled chicken, salads and fruit (bullet 1, with a passive). Findings: most students choose the chicken wraps and the lentil soup (bullet 2). Several say the portions are too small and the prices have gone up (bullet 3). Recommendation: 'I would recommend offering larger portions for older students' or 'It is suggested that students vote for one new dish each month' (bullet 4).

  4. Change into the passive: (a) The teachers organised a quiz. (b) We interviewed twenty students. (c) The school should repair the computers. (d) Someone has painted the hall.

    Show answer
    (a) A quiz was organised (by the teachers). (b) Twenty students were interviewed. (c) The computers should be repaired. (d) The hall has been painted.

  5. Rewrite in report style: 'OMG the trip was amazing!!! Me and my friends loved the robots so much and we want to go again.'

    Show answer
    Possible answer: 'The trip was extremely popular with students. The robotics workshop was the highlight of the day, and many students said they would like to visit again.'

  6. Complete with a recommending phrase: '_____ that the library stays open until five o'clock.'

    Show answer
    'It is recommended that…' / 'It is suggested that…' / 'I would recommend that…' (followed by the clause 'the library stays/stay open until five o'clock').

  7. A student's report covers the place, the activities and the students' opinions, but says nothing about whether the trip should be repeated (the fourth bullet). The English is excellent. Can it get 8/8 for Content?

    Show answer
    No. A missing compulsory bullet is partial coverage, so the answer cannot reach the top Content band, whatever the quality of the English.
Self-check before you hand in
  • Every bullet answered and ticked?
  • Purpose stated at the start?
  • Formal or semi-formal tone for the named reader?
  • At least one passive and one recommending phrase?
  • 'Most', 'several', 'a few' instead of fake statistics?
  • Clear recommendation at the end?
  • About 70-90 words? Spelling and full stops checked?
