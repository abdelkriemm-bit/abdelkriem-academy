The Exam: Overview

Everything about Cambridge IGCSE ESL 0510: the three papers, marks and weightings, every Paper 1 exercise, how writing is marked, listening and speaking, a 2-hour time plan and a 10-week revision plan.

British & IGCSE › Cambridge IGCSE English as a Second Language (0510)

This page is your map of the whole Cambridge IGCSE English as a Second Language exam. Read it before anything else. It tells you which papers you will sit, how long each one is, how many marks every exercise carries, how the writing is marked, and how to plan your time and your revision. Parents will find a short Arabic explanation at the end.

The qualification in one paragraph

Cambridge IGCSE English as a Second Language tests four skills: Reading, Writing, Listening and Speaking. Reading and Writing are tested together in Paper 1. Listening is Paper 2. Speaking is Paper 3. The same course is offered under different syllabus codes. The code decides two things: the grading scale (A*-G or 9-1) and whether your Speaking mark counts towards your final grade. Many Year 9 and Year 10 students in Egyptian British schools are entered for 0510, so this course is written for 0510, but the papers are the same for the other codes.

CodeGradesWhat happens to Speaking?
0510A* to GSpeaking is separately endorsed: it does not count in your overall grade. Your certificate shows a separate Speaking grade from 1 (highest) to 5.
0511A* to GCount-in Speaking: Speaking is worth 25% of your overall grade.
09939 to 1Speaking is separately endorsed as Distinction, Merit or Pass.
Check your code with your school

Your school chooses the code when it enters you for the exam. Ask your teacher or the exams office which one you are taking. Some schools in Egypt also offer a different, Egypt-only syllabus (0465, a Core syllabus graded C-G) with a shorter Paper 1 (1 hour 45 minutes) and shorter writing tasks (100-150 words). This course is for 0510/0511 - if you are entered for 0465, the reading skills here still help, but your paper is different.

The three papers at a glance

PaperHow long?MarksWeighting (0510)Weighting (0511)What is in it?
Paper 1 Reading and Writing2 hours6070%50%Four reading exercises (30 marks) and two writing exercises (30 marks). You write on the question paper. All questions are compulsory. No dictionaries.
Paper 2 ListeningAbout 50 minutes (including 6 minutes to transfer answers)4030%25%Five exercises, 40 multiple-choice questions. You hear each recording twice and shade your answers on an answer sheet.
Paper 3 SpeakingAbout 10-15 minutes40Separately endorsed (0%)25%A one-to-one test with your teacher or an examiner: an interview, a short talk and a discussion. It is recorded.

Look at the weighting for 0510: Paper 1 is worth 70% of your grade and Listening 30%. That means reading and writing together decide most of your result. Spend most of your revision time on Paper 1 - but do not ignore Listening, because 30% is a lot of marks to lose.

Key facts to remember
  • Paper 1: 2 hours, 60 marks, 6 exercises (Exercises 1-4 reading, Exercises 5-6 writing).
  • Paper 2: about 50 minutes, 40 marks, 40 multiple-choice questions, every recording played twice.
  • Paper 3: about 10-15 minutes, 40 marks. For 0510 it gives a separate grade from 1 to 5.
  • There are no tiers any more: everyone sits the same papers.

No more Core and Extended

Before 2024, students were entered for either Core or Extended papers. Core students could not get the top grades. Since the first exams in 2024, this tiering has been removed. Every student sits the same Paper 1, the same Listening paper and the same Speaking test, and every student can get any grade from A* to G. This is good news: you do not need to choose a level, and a strong performance on the day can take you all the way to A*.

Old books and websites

If a book, video or website talks about 'Core paper', 'Extended paper', 'form-filling' or 'summary writing', it is describing the old exam. Use it only for general practice, and always check its question types against the table on this page.

Grades and what they mean

  • Overall grade (0510): A*, A, B, C, D, E, F or G. Below G is 'ungraded'. It is worked out from Paper 1 and Paper 2 only.
  • Speaking grade (0510): a separate grade from 1 (high) to 5 (low), shown on the certificate when the standard is met.
  • Grade boundaries (how many marks you need for each grade) are set by Cambridge after every exam series. They change a little each time, so nobody can promise you an exact number before the exam.
  • Cambridge links the grades to the international CEFR scale. Roughly, an overall grade C plus a Speaking grade 2 is about B2 level - the level of a confident independent user of English.

Paper 1, exercise by exercise

Paper 1 is the most important paper, so you need to know every exercise by heart. Each exercise has its own page in this course.

ExerciseQuestionsThe textType of questionMarks
Exercise 1Q1-6One text of about 400-450 words (for example an article, a blog or a web page)Short answers - a word, a phrase or a number. Q1-5 need one answer each, and Q6 needs three answers.8
Exercise 2Q7 (a)-(i)About 600-650 words: one text in up to five sections, or up to five short texts (for example four young people writing about a hobby)Multiple matching: you write the letter of the section or person that matches each statement (9 statements)9
Exercise 3Q8-9About 400-500 words, usually an articleNote-making: complete short notes under two headings (7 bullet points in total). An example is given.7
Exercise 4Q10-15About 550-600 words, for example a blog or an articleMultiple choice with three options (A, B or C): main ideas, details, the writer's attitude and opinions6
Exercise 5Q16A short task with three bullet pointsWriting: an informal email (for example to a friend or relative), about 120-160 words15
Exercise 6Q17A short task with comments or a pictureWriting: a formal or semi-formal article, report, essay or review, about 120-160 words15

Add it up: reading = 8 + 9 + 7 + 6 = 30 marks, and writing = 15 + 15 = 30 marks. Total 60. The reading exercises test four skills: finding specific facts, understanding how ideas and opinions connect, choosing the right details for a purpose, and understanding what is implied (meant but not said directly).

How reading answers are marked
  • One mark for each correct answer, whole marks only.
  • Marking is positive: you are not punished for small spelling or grammar mistakes in reading answers - unless the mistake makes your meaning unclear or changes the word.
  • For short answers and notes, the mark scheme lists the acceptable answers. Extra words are not needed, so keep answers short.
  • For multiple choice and matching, only the correct letter gets the mark.

How the writing is marked

Both writing exercises use exactly the same marking criteria. Each task is marked out of 15: Content out of 6 and Language out of 9. The examiner reads your answer, decides which band fits it best, and then chooses a mark inside that band. Marks are given for what you do well, not taken away for each mistake.

Content (6 marks)Language (9 marks)
What it checksTask fulfilment: did you answer all parts of the task, stay relevant, use the right style for the text type, and keep the reader and purpose in mind? Development: are your ideas explained and expanded?Range and accuracy: how wide and how correct is your vocabulary and grammar? Organisation: is the writing in a clear order, with paragraphs and a good range of linking words?
Top band5-6: task fully done, everything relevant, style right all the way through, ideas very well developed7-9: skilful, with common and less common words, simple and complex sentences, very accurate grammar and clear organisation with many linking devices
Middle band3-4: task generally done, mostly relevant, style generally right, ideas generally developed4-6: clear, with mostly common words and some less common ones, mostly simple sentences with some complex ones, and errors that do not usually block meaning
Low band1-2: task only partly done, style not right, little development1-3: meaning not always clear, with only simple words and sentences and errors that make it hard to understand
  • Word count: both tasks ask for about 120-160 words. There is no automatic penalty, but writing far less usually means weak development, and writing far more wastes time and often adds mistakes and irrelevant ideas. Aim for 140-160.
  • Exercise 5 is always an informal email.
  • Exercise 6 can be an article, a report, an essay or a review in a formal or semi-formal style. Learn all four - especially the essay, which many students forget.

Paper 2 Listening: the five exercises

The whole Listening paper is multiple choice. You hear every recording twice. At the end you have 6 minutes to copy your answers onto the answer sheet.

ExerciseWhat you hearWhat you doMarks
Exercise 18 short extracts (conversations, messages, announcements)Choose one of four pictures (A-D) for each question8
Exercise 25 short extractsTwo questions per extract, three written options (A-C)10
Exercise 3One talk or presentation by one speakerComplete 8 sentences by choosing from three options (A-C)8
Exercise 46 short monologues (six different speakers)Match each speaker to one of 8 statements (two statements are not used)6
Exercise 5An interview between two speakers8 questions with three options (A-C) about the main speaker8

Total: 40 questions, 40 marks. The listening pages in this course explain how the wrong options (distractors) are built to trick you, and how to avoid them.

Paper 3 Speaking: the parts

Speaking is a one-to-one test of about 10-15 minutes with your teacher or an examiner. It is recorded, marked by your school and checked (moderated) by Cambridge. You may not take written notes into the test.

PartAbout how long?What happensMarked?
Introduction1 minuteThe examiner explains the test and checks your details.No
Warm-up1-2 minutesEasy questions to relax you.No
Part 1 Interview2-3 minutesThe examiner asks you set questions on a topic.Yes
Part 2 Short talk3-4 minutes (including 1 minute to prepare)You get a card with a topic and talk on your own.Yes
Part 3 Discussion3-4 minutesThe examiner asks discussion questions linked to the Part 2 topic.Yes

You are marked out of 40: Grammar 10, Vocabulary 10, Development 10 and Pronunciation 10 (pronunciation includes intonation - the music of your voice). For 0510 this gives your separate Speaking grade. For 0511 it is 25% of your overall grade.

What changed in 2024 (briefly)

  1. Core and Extended tiers were removed: one set of papers for everybody.
  2. Paper 1 became one 2-hour Reading and Writing paper with 60 marks and 6 exercises (4 reading, 2 writing).
  3. The summary-writing task was removed. Exercise 4 is now a multiple-choice reading exercise.
  4. Listening became 100% multiple choice: 40 questions, 40 marks, about 50 minutes.
  5. Speaking went up from 30 to 40 marks and now has three marked parts. You only see a card for Part 2.
  6. The writing mark scheme changed: both tasks are now Content 6 + Language 9.

The syllabus for 2027-2029 keeps the same structure, so everything on this page should still apply in the next few years. Always check the latest syllabus with your school.

Time plan for the 2-hour Paper 1

Cambridge does not tell you how long to spend on each exercise - you must manage the 2 hours yourself. Below is the plan I recommend to my students. It gives reading and writing about the same time, because each is worth 30 marks, and it keeps 10 minutes at the end for checking.

ExerciseMarksSuggested timeWhy
Exercise 1 (short answers)812 minutesShort text, answers mostly in order
Exercise 2 (matching)915 minutesLongest text: you must compare several writers
Exercise 3 (notes)713 minutesDetails are spread across the whole text
Exercise 4 (multiple choice)615 minutesLong text, and the options need careful checking
Exercise 5 (informal email)1525 minutes5 min plan, 17 min write, 3 min check
Exercise 6 (article / report / essay / review)1530 minutesHarder style: 5 min plan, 20 min write, 5 min check
Final check-10 minutesBlank answers, spelling of copied words, word count
Total60120 minutes
Two rules for timing
  • Wear a watch and write your 'finish' times at the top of the paper at the start (for example, if you start at 9:00, Exercise 1 ends at 9:12, Exercise 2 at 9:27...).
  • Never leave an answer blank. A sensible guess in multiple choice or matching can still win the mark. If you are stuck, guess, mark the question with a small star and come back in the final 10 minutes.

Some students prefer to do the writing first, while they are fresh. That is allowed - you may answer the exercises in any order. If you do this, keep the same times and be strict: do not let the writing eat into your reading time.

  • Study actively. Do every practice question before you click 'Show answer'. Write your answer on paper first.
  • Keep a mistakes notebook. For every wrong answer, write the question, your answer, the right answer and *why* you were wrong (for example: 'I copied too much', 'I chose the first number I saw').
  • Learn vocabulary in pairs. Paper 1 rewords everything. When you learn a word, learn a synonym with it (*buy - purchase*, *start - launch*).
  • Write by hand. The exam is on paper. Practise your writing tasks by hand and count your words.

A 10-week revision plan

This plan assumes about 4-5 hours a week. If you have less time, keep the order but shorten the practice. If your exam is closer than 10 weeks, combine two weeks into one.

WeekFocusWhat to do
1The exam + reading skillsRead this page and the Core reading skills page. Do all the skill practice. Do one Exercise 1 practice with no time limit to find your level.
2Exercise 1Study the Exercise 1 page. Do the practice test in 12 minutes. Write down every mistake type in your notebook.
3Exercise 2Study the matching method. Do one practice in 15 minutes. Practise finding paraphrases of opinions.
4Exercise 3Study note-making. Practise writing short notes (2-5 words) under headings. Watch spelling of copied words.
5Exercise 4Study multiple choice and attitude words. Do one practice in 15 minutes. For every option, write why it is right or wrong.
6Writing criteria + Exercise 5Read the Writing overview and the Informal email pack. Learn the structure and phrases. Write two emails (25 minutes each) and check them against the checklist.
7Exercise 6: article and reviewStudy both packs. Write one article and one review (30 minutes each).
8Exercise 6: report and essayStudy both packs. Write one report and one essay. Compare the four styles: who is the reader in each?
9Listening + SpeakingStudy both listening pages. Do listening practice every day (even 15 minutes). Practise a 2-3 minute talk on familiar topics with a family member or friend.
10Full mock examDo one full Paper 1 in exactly 2 hours and one listening paper. Mark them. Spend the rest of the week only on your two weakest exercises.
Every week, whatever the focus
  • Learn 15-20 new words with a synonym for each.
  • Read one English article or blog for pleasure (sport, science, technology - anything you like).
  • Listen to 15 minutes of clear spoken English (a podcast or the news).
  • Re-read your mistakes notebook.

Quick check

  1. How long is Paper 1, and how many marks is it worth?

    Show answer
    2 hours, 60 marks (30 reading + 30 writing).

  2. For 0510, what percentage of the overall grade is Paper 1?

    Show answer
    70%. Listening is 30%. Speaking is separately endorsed (0%).

  3. Does the Speaking test count towards a 0510 grade?

    Show answer
    No. It is separately endorsed with its own grade from 1 (high) to 5 (low). For 0511 it counts 25%.

  4. How many marks is Exercise 1, and how many answers does Question 6 need?

    Show answer
    8 marks. Questions 1-5 need one answer each, and Question 6 needs three answers.

  5. Which text types can appear in Exercise 6?

    Show answer
    An article, a report, an essay or a review (formal or semi-formal).

  6. How is each writing task marked?

    Show answer
    Out of 15: Content 6 + Language 9. The same criteria are used for Exercise 5 and Exercise 6.

  7. How many times do you hear each listening recording?

    Show answer
    Twice.

  8. Can a student who would once have taken 'Core' get an A* now?

    Show answer
    Yes. Tiers were removed from 2024, so everyone sits the same papers and can get any grade from A* to G.
