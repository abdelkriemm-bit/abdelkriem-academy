Session 2: Subordinating Conjunctions

The most common subordinating conjunctions — although, though, even though, when, while, if, unless, since, once, until, because, so that and that — what each one means, and the comma rule for DC, IC and IC DC. Plus a FANBOYS review.

British & IGCSE › Academic Writing: Sentence Structure

By the end of this session you can …
  • Explain what a subordinating conjunction does to a clause
  • Use the most common subordinating conjunctions with the right meaning
  • Use a comma after a dependent clause at the start (DC, IC) and no comma in IC DC
  • Never use although + but, or because + so, in the same sentence

1. Quick review: FANBOYS

Remember from Session 1
  • FANBOYS = for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so.
  • IC, FANBOYS IC → comma before the conjunction: It was late, so we took a taxi.
  • Two verbs, one subject → no comma: She ate quickly and left.

2. What does a subordinating conjunction do?

A subordinating conjunction goes at the start of a clause and makes it dependent. A dependent clause (DC) has a subject and a verb, but it cannot stand alone — it needs an independent clause (IC) to complete the meaning.

Independent clause (IC)Add a subordinating conjunctionDependent clause (DC) — not a sentence on its own
It was raining.becausebecause it was raining …
She finished her homework.afterafter she finished her homework …
You study.ifif you study …
A DC alone is a fragment
  • ✗ Because it was raining. (What happened? The idea is not complete.)
  • ✓ We stayed inside because it was raining. ✓ Because it was raining, we stayed inside.

3. The most common subordinating conjunctions

MeaningConjunctionsExample
contrast / concessionalthough, though, even though, while, whereasAlthough the test was hard, she finished it.
timewhen, while, before, after, until, since, once, as soon asWhen the bell rang, the students left.
reasonbecause, since, asWe stayed home because it was snowing.
conditionif, unless, onceIf you practise every day, you will improve.
purposeso thatShe saved money so that she could buy a laptop.
reporting / giving factsthatScientists believe that the climate is changing.

4. Mastering each one

ALTHOUGH · THOUGH · EVEN THOUGH = contrast
  • The two ideas are opposite: Although he was ill, he went to school.
  • Though means the same and is slightly less formal. Even though is stronger (more surprising).
  • ✗ Although it was cold, but we went swimming. — never use although + but together. Choose one: Although it was cold, we went swimming. / It was cold, but we went swimming.
WHEN vs WHILE (time)
  • When = at that moment (often a short action): When the phone rang, I answered it.
  • While = during a longer action (often with the past continuous): While I was cooking, the phone rang.
WHILE · WHEREAS = contrast between two facts
  • While can also compare two different things: My brother loves football, while I prefer tennis.
  • Whereas is used only for this comparison: Cairo is hot and dry, whereas London is cool and wet.
  • These two are the one place where we often put a comma in IC DC, because the two facts are being compared.
SINCE: two meanings
  • Time (from a point in the past until now) → use the present perfect: I have lived here since I was five.
  • Reason (= because): Since the shop was closed, we went home.
ONCE · UNTIL · AS SOON AS
  • Once = after / as soon as: Once you finish the test, hand in your paper.
  • Until = up to that time: Wait here until I come back.
  • With until, once, when, as soon as, before, after, if, use the present tense for the future: ✗ until I will come back ✓ until I come back.
IF · UNLESS = condition
  • If you leave now, you will catch the bus.
  • Unless = if … not: Unless you leave now, you will miss the bus. (= If you don't leave now …)
  • ✗ Unless you don't leave now — unless is already negative.
BECAUSE · SO THAT
  • Because gives the reason: She was late because her car broke down.
  • So that gives the purpose (the aim): She left early so that she would not be late.
  • ✗ Because she was tired, so she slept. — never because + so in one sentence.
THAT
  • That starts a clause that acts like a noun, often after verbs such as think, believe, know, say, show, suggest: The results show that the plan worked.
  • In academic writing, keep that after show, suggest, argue, believe: Research suggests that sleep improves memory.

5. The comma rule

OrderComma?Example
DC, IC (dependent clause first)✓ comma after the DCAlthough it was late, we kept working.
IC DC (independent clause first)✗ no commaWe kept working although it was late.
Easy way to remember
  • If the sentence starts with the subordinating conjunction, put a comma where the dependent clause ends.
  • If the conjunction is in the middle, you usually need no comma (except whereas / while when you compare two facts).
FANBOYS or subordinating conjunction?
  • It was cold, but we went out. = Although it was cold, we went out. Same meaning, different structure.
  • It was late, so we took a taxi. = Because it was late, we took a taxi.
  • Use one joining word for one link — not two.

6. Practice in the session

  1. Choose: ___ she was very tired, she finished the essay. (Although / Because)

    Show answer
    Although (contrast)

  2. Choose: Stay inside ___ the rain stops. (until / since)

    Show answer
    until

  3. Choose: I have known Omar ___ we were six. (since / while)

    Show answer
    since (time, present perfect)

  4. Choose: ___ you hurry, you will be late. (If / Unless)

    Show answer
    Unless (= if you don't hurry)

  5. Choose: She studied hard ___ she could get a scholarship. (so that / because)

    Show answer
    so that (purpose)

  6. Choose: ___ I was walking home, I saw an accident. (While / Once)

    Show answer
    While (during a longer action)

  7. Add the comma if needed: When the film ended we went home.

    Show answer
    When the film ended, we went home. (DC, IC)

  8. Add the comma if needed: We went home when the film ended.

    Show answer
    No comma (IC DC).

  9. Correct: Although the hotel was expensive, but the rooms were small.

    Show answer
    Although the hotel was expensive, the rooms were small. (or: The hotel was expensive, but the rooms were small.)

  10. Correct: Because it was raining. We stayed at home.

    Show answer
    Fragment. ✓ Because it was raining, we stayed at home.

  11. Correct: I will call you when I will arrive.

    Show answer
    I will call you when I arrive.

  12. Rewrite with although: The task was difficult, but we finished it.

    Show answer
    Although the task was difficult, we finished it.
